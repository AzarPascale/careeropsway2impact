### Takeaways: (a) Rules for the AI job-search system
**CV / PHP / P11**
1. Every bullet passes a 3-part test:
   - Did *I* do this, or did my team, cluster or agency?
   - What bottleneck broke or improved?
   - What verifiable number is attached?
2. Never write "Responsible for." Open each bullet with a strong, function-specific verb. Use present tense for the current role and past tense for earlier ones.
3. Bullet formula: **verb + task + scale/number + "so what" result.** Example: coordinated 14 partners across 3 governorates, and donor clearance dropped from 3 weeks to 5 days.
4. Colour logic the AI should check for in each bullet:
   - action verb
   - hard skills / tools (Logframe, ToC, IPTT, Kobo, Power BI, DHIS2, OECD-DAC)
   - metric
   - bridge/outcome word (improved, reduced, strengthened)
   - soft skill woven inside the technical bullet, never as a separate list
5. Mirror the vacancy's exact terms. If the TOR says "results framework," don't write "logic model." Screeners match wording, not meaning.
6. Find the skill that appears in most target job ads (paste 5-10 JDs and look for overlap). Make sure it is on the CV and backed by proof.
7. **One-line scope statement** at the top: years + function + programme types + budget range + number of operations + reach. Example: 12+ years M&E, $5M-$200M budgets, 6 country operations, 1.2M beneficiaries. The AI should produce one for every client.
8. In the first 6 seconds the top third must answer: target role, what you've done, proof of skills, and that you can repeat it here.
9. **Translate internal jargon.** Flag agency-only terms and acronyms (e.g. UNHCR "V4," "IRIS"). Flag the employer's name repeated too often. Flag national-officer titles that hide international-level work.
10. **UN → corporate translation rules:**
    - capacity building / logframe → strategic planning, change management, stakeholder alignment
    - add efficiency and business impact
    - "demilitarise" conflict language: say high-pressure, volatile environments
    - put scale (budgets, borders, team size) up front
11. Senior CVs: experience first, education last (education goes to the bottom after about 2 years in the field). Show both field and HQ. Never inflate beneficiary numbers; the sector is small and people check.
12. Keep the PHP/Inspira profile in the same discipline as the CV. Recruiters look at both.
13. Simple ATS-safe layout: white space, F-pattern, bold the wins, typo check by a second reader. Cut certifications that don't match the role. Highlight recognised ones (MEAL DPro, IPDET, PMD Pro).
14. Optional: one short testimonial line as social proof.
15. AI-polished sameness is now the baseline and gets ignored. The system should push for "unfakable field evidence," not prettier phrasing. Use AI to critique ("where am I losing points," "score me out of 100, be brutal"), not to generate.

**Cover letter**
16. Write one cover letter per vacancy, in the vacancy's own language. It must not be interchangeable ("could belong to anyone, fix that").
17. Name the career gap without apologising. Ask the AI for 3 framings.

**Interview**
18. Competency-based interviews: STAR stories mapped to each UN behavioural indicator, per grade (P2 to D1). Rehearse each story several times; one candidate rewrote hers 11 times.
19. Run mock panels (multi-panelist, voice mode) that are harder than the real one.
20. Never use AI live on a second screen. Panels notice the pause, the drifting eyes, the too-clean answers.
21. Teach the honest answer: "I don't know that one; here's how I'd find out."
22. Communication is scored as clarity: explaining complex data simply and answering the actual question.

**Job search strategy / scoring**
23. Track the stage where you lose, not the number of applications.
24. Apply within 72 hours. Shortlists often close before the deadline.
25. Get one human contact per application (a name, a message, a referral).
26. Always keep one live process running. Ask for feedback in one sentence.
27. Build proof outside the form: a case note or a public write-up.
28. **Fit filter before applying** (four questions):
    - What organisation size fits me?
    - What problem do I actually solve (builder vs optimiser)?
    - What environment brings out my best?
    - What drained me even though I was good at it?

    Her case: 15 applications a week down to 3, and shortlisting went from 0% to almost 40%.
29. Six months of silence is usually a translation problem, not a skills problem. The system should diagnose translation first.
30. Headline formula: [Role] | [Key skills] | [Sector]. Never "Open to work" or "Seeking opportunities."

### Takeaways: (a) Rules for the AI job-search system
**Diagnosis before rewriting**
1. Don't start by rewriting the CV. First assess **targeting** (is the client genuinely competitive for this role?), then **positioning**, then **evidence**. Always assess against one real vacancy, like her Application Audit. The AI's first output should be a match/competitiveness report: strengths, gaps, where the recruiter is lost, top priorities.
2. Gap rule: "Meet 60%? Apply. Missing a year? Apply." But if the gap is central to doing the job daily (e.g. a working language), it is a real gap. Score gaps by **centrality**, not percentage.
3. Low traction after 10-20 applications means stop and analyse. Ask: right roles? competitive? language understood? too many directions? network used? relying only on adverts? Consider the market too (e.g. USAID shutdown).

**CV**
4. Make **seniority** visible: scale managed, decisions owned, teams/budgets/portfolios, complexity and risk, accountability, autonomy, influence on decisions. Show progression, not 20 years of job descriptions.
5. Translate institutional titles. Add a plain-language descriptor after internal titles (e.g. "Head of Sub-Delegation (led an X-person office, budget Y, negotiations with authorities)").
6. Convert the institutional "we" into "I": what you led, decided, contributed, what changed. Do this without breaching confidentiality or claiming collective work.
7. Responsibilities → results. "Explain what changed because you were there."
8. Task-focused → value/judgement-focused (AI-proof). Stress human judgement, ethics, protection-sensitive analysis, risk mitigation.
9. Experienced profiles (45+): when not required, remove graduation years, focus on the last 15-20 years, and lead with current relevance. When an application form requires dates, give them accurately.
10. "Overqualified": don't shrink the profile (removing senior roles makes it look incoherent). Make the move make sense: why this role, why now, what the organisation gains.
11. Generalist managers: name "leading complexity" as a specialisation (bringing specialists together, negotiating priorities, stakeholder management).
12. Relevance over recency: lead with the experience that best supports this application. Reflect employer priorities (localisation, donor compliance, protection, partnerships).
13. ATS: avoid heavily designed Canva CVs, columns, text boxes and icons. Test with a free ATS parser. A simple Word document reduces misreading. Test: "Would it still make sense if all the design disappeared?"

**Cover letter**
14. The cover letter must explain why the experience is relevant to this role. Never repeat the CV.

**LinkedIn profile (part of the system)**
15. A profile has 3 readers: recruiter (level and fit fast), client/partner (what you solve), AI search (clear evidence). The headline must be more than a job title. The About section must say what you can contribute now, not tell a biography.
16. Photo: credible, trustworthy, "could represent us." No need for a €300 photographer. AI improvement is OK if it doesn't misrepresent.

**Job search strategy**
17. Search by **competencies and functions**, not job titles, on LinkedIn/ReliefWeb.
18. Timing: don't wait for September. Funding decisions in Apr-Jun turn into July recruitment for short cycles, and processes now take ~2-3 months. October can be less crowded than September.
19. Reassess the target list: which organisations are hiring now, where they advertise, which functions are prioritised. Look beyond traditional employers (foundations, universities, public sector, private sector, consulting).
20. Network beyond your own employer: reconnect with alumni who left, and talk to other sectors before you need it.
21. Two parallel strategies: secure the next role, and reduce dependency on it (relevance beyond current org, external relationships, explore directions early).
22. Transition questions to ask before searching vacancies:
    - What do I want to carry forward?
    - What do I want less of?
    - What am I still exceptionally good at?
    - Who values that?
    - Which trade-offs (salary, status, location, responsibility) can I accept?
23. Before another Master's: audit competencies, transferable skills, desired work, market needs, and **personal constraints** (children, parents, partner, finances, location).
24. Salary: help clients decide if a role is worth their time. Prepare answers to the salary question. Note the EU Pay Transparency Directive (range before interview; no salary-history questions).
25. Build in well-being: pausing applications to reassess is productive. The client's worth has not disappeared.

### Takeaways: (b) Ideas for the Way2Impact LinkedIn page
1. **"Translate the title" series:** take one insider job title (UN, NGO, donor, CSR) and show how to explain it to an outside reader. Ask the audience: "What did your title mean that nobody outside would guess?"
2. **Transition interview series** ("From X to Y"): Q&A with real clients or alumni across the 4 segments, hosted on the website and teased on the page. This is her second-best performer and is naturally company-page friendly.
