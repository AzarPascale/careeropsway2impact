# 05 · Career-ops for the social impact sector: rules from web research

**Prepared:** 16 November 2026
**For:** Kristin (Way2Impact). Input for a sector version of the open-source "career-ops" job-search agent (https://github.com/career-ops-hq/career-ops).
**Type:** Desk research from public web sources. New research, not a summary of files 00 to 04.
**Builds on:** research/01 (funding cuts), research/03 (hiring processes), `templates/cv/formats/` and `templates/cover-letter/framework.md`. Where this file adds to or disagrees with them, it says so under "Fits with existing files".

> **How to read this file**
> - Each section is a list of rules an AI agent can follow: **Do**, **Don't**, **Why**, then the source.
> - **(opened)** means the page was opened and read. **(snippet)** means the claim was seen only in a search-result summary, so the page itself was not read. Many Devex and Impactpool pages block automated reading, so they are mostly snippets.
> - **(uncertain)** or **(region-specific)** marks claims that are not settled or that do not hold everywhere.
> - **(interpretation)** marks a rule I derived from the evidence. It is not a sourced fact.
> - No quote is longer than 15 words.

## Summary

1. **The application is often a form, not a CV.** UN Inspira and agency portals use a Personal History Profile (PHP or P11). The form is the record that counts. An attached CV may not be read at screening (UNjobnet, opened). The agent must fill fields, not only make PDFs.
2. **Screening is a pass/fail check on stated minimums first.** UN portals ask yes/no questions on years, degree and languages. A strong letter cannot fix a missing minimum (snippet). The agent should check hard eligibility before it scores fit.
3. **Eligibility rules the corporate world does not have:** National Officer posts are for nationals of the duty-station country only. General Service posts need the legal right to work locally. EU permanent exams are for EU nationals only. The YPP has an age limit of 32 (UNICEF, opened; UNFPA, snippet; research/03).
4. **Evidence beats passion.** Recruiters want results with scale: budget, donor, people reached, partners, geography, languages. Mission talk without proof is a common weakness (Jayne Cravens, opened; Idealist, snippet; Bond, opened).
5. **Words matter in two directions.** Sector words (MEAL, logframe, safeguarding, localisation) show you belong. Saviour words ("help the poor", "voiceless", "rescue") count against you. Some readers now see colonial or charity framing as a red flag (Manchester Anthropology of Aid, opened; Bond, snippet).
6. **Interviews are competency-based and scored against a named framework.** Panels look for humility, emotional intelligence and your own role in the story (IOM recruiter via Devex, snippet). Safeguarding values questions and misconduct checks are now standard (research/03; CHS Alliance guidelines, snippet).
7. **Pay is mostly fixed.** UN grades are tied to the post. The step, start date and relocation terms are the levers. INGOs use pay bands, often with separate national and international scales (UNjobnet, snippet; Devex, snippet; Bond, snippet).
8. **Timelines are long.** Expect 3 to 9 months from UN deadline to offer. The UN's own 120-day target is not met. Silence is normal (UNjobnet, opened).
9. **The market shrank hard.** ReliefWeb vacancies fell about 65% from 2024 to 2025. Local NGOs cut quickest and deepest. Young professionals and local female staff were hit hardest (ALNAP State of the Humanitarian System 2026, opened). The scoring rubric needs a funding-security dimension.
10. **career-ops' rubric needs new blocks for this sector:** hard eligibility (nationality, right to work, age, language), funding security and donor dependency, contract type (staff vs consultant), duty station and security, process cost (tests, long timelines) and mission fit (interpretation, based on all sections).

## 1. CVs

### 1.1 Length and format

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Default to **2 pages** for NGO and INGO CVs. Allow **3** for senior staff and consultants with long project lists. | Don't force a one-page corporate resume on an experienced aid worker. | Devex says the one-page rule does not apply in development, and some recommend 2 to 3 pages (https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-write-global-development-cvs-if-you-re-new-to-the-sector-104860, snippet). UCL Careers suggests 2 sides but notes the UN may take longer (https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/ucl-careers/tag/notforprofit, snippet). |
| Put the strongest, most relevant content in the **top half of page 1**. | Don't open with a long objective statement. | Recruiters decide quickly; the top half does most of the work (UCL, snippet). |
| Keep it scannable: short bullets, white space, no spelling errors. | Don't attach extras (testimonials, affidavits, full thesis). | Bond: spelling errors and dense text get CVs set aside; very long CVs are a common mistake (https://www.bond.org.uk/news/2016/11/tips-to-get-an-ngo-job, opened). |
| For consultants, consider a **topical or functional** layout (by expertise area). | Don't use a functional layout for UN staff posts that check every month of experience. | Kathryn Harper (CV writer) recommends topical formats for consultants (Devex, snippet: https://www.devex.com/news/what-a-standout-consulting-cv-looks-like-106098). UN forms count months (UNjobnet, opened). |
| Use the format the advert names: online form, P11, Europass, or free CV. | Don't upload files the vacancy did not ask for. | Uploading unrequested files is listed as a common mistake (https://www.unjobnet.org/advice/un-p11-personal-history-profile-guide, opened). |

### 1.2 UN forms (Inspira PHP and P11)

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Treat the **form as the application**. Fill every field, including supervisor, reason for leaving, part-time percentage and last salary where asked. | Don't rely on an attached CV. Don't leave mandatory fields blank. | The PHP is the authoritative record; an attached CV may not be considered at screening. Blank mandatory fields can stall the application (UNjobnet P11 guide, opened). |
| Match legal name and nationality to the passport. **List every nationality held.** | Don't omit a second nationality. | Extra nationalities can widen eligibility (UNjobnet, opened). |
| Account for **every month**. Explain gaps in one line. | Don't leave unexplained gaps. | Same source (opened). |
| Show unfinished degrees as **ongoing**. | Never list an unfinished degree as completed. | Same source (opened). UNICEF does not accept incomplete degrees for NO posts (https://www.unicef.org/careers/requirements-national-officer-staff-category, opened). |
| Rate languages separately (reading, writing, speaking, understanding), honestly. | Don't overstate. | Overstated language ability is a common mistake (UNjobnet, opened); languages are tested (research/03 §3.2). |
| Keep one **master PHP** and a tailored copy per application. Draft long text offline, then paste. | Don't send one generic profile everywhere. | UNjobnet (opened). Not tailoring the PHP is one of the biggest problems UN recruiters see (Devex via search, snippet: https://www.devex.com/news/your-guide-to-the-un-job-application-process-94278). |
| Keep dates, titles and employers identical across form, CV, letter and LinkedIn. | Don't let versions drift. | Reviewers check that answers and profile are consistent (snippet via https://careers.tufts.edu/resources/applying-for-a-un-job-using-inspira/). |

### 1.3 How to show results

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Each relevant role: **scale + donor + partners + geography + result**. Example pattern: "Managed {donor}-funded {sector} project, {budget}, {n} partners, {n} people in {area}; {result}." | Don't copy duties from the old job description. | Achievements show value; responsibilities only describe the job (Impactpool, snippet: https://www.impactpool.org/article/application-mistakes-that-cost-you-the-job-and-how-to-avoid-them). |
| Name **which donors** (for example EU, FCDO, ECHO, GIZ, foundations) and what you did for them (proposals, reports, compliance). | Don't write "managed donor relations" with no donor. | Many employers look for experience with specific funders (Ask Devex, snippet: https://www.devex.com/news/ask-devex-how-to-successfully-present-your-experience-in-a-cv-94552). |
| Say **how** you did it and **with whom**: name partners and the groups reached (women, rural, refugees, literacy levels). | Don't write "stakeholders" or "beneficiaries" with no detail. | Jayne Cravens' repeated edits ask "how", "what", "which" and who was reached (https://www.coyotebroad.com/development/cv_edits_usual.shtml, opened). |
| Give counts: people reached, staff supervised, trainings, partners. **Check claims of scale** before using them. | Don't claim "every household" or round numbers up. | Cravens questions inflated reach (opened). The UNDP AI rules ban inflated experience (research/03 §3.7). |
| Show **efficiency and cost** results (cost per person, time saved, funds raised). | Don't show only activity counts. | Employers now want proof of doing more with less (research/01 §8.1). |
| Put **languages, duty stations and field experience** near the top. | Don't bury field and language experience. | ICRC values languages and experience in different cultural settings, with about 2 years' experience for professional posts (ICRC careers page via search, snippet: https://careers.icrc.org/content/what-we-look-for/). NRC screens languages first (research/03 §6.1). |
| Count **volunteer and short unpaid** sector work. | Don't drop it as irrelevant. | Short or unpaid NGO experience matters (Nigerian NGO CV guide, snippet: https://www.mycvcreator.com/blog/how-to-write-a-cv-for-nigerian-ngos-and-development-organizations). Service experience is important (UConn, snippet). |
| Explain transitions and gaps **briefly and plainly**. | Don't hide them. | Kathryn Harper urges candidates to contextualise gaps clearly and concisely (Devex, snippet: https://www.devex.com/news/considering-a-career-pivot-3-cv-tips-for-boosting-your-application-111002). |

### 1.4 Photo and personal data by region

| Context | Rule for the agent | Source |
|---|---|---|
| UN, INGO, most NGO CVs | **No photo** unless asked. Personal data only where a form asks for it. | Humanitarian CV guidance advises no photo unless requested (snippet via https://impactpool.org/article/humanitarian-cv-resume-advice-and-examples). Forms such as UN PHPs, the ICRC form and NRC's process collect date of birth themselves (https://www.nrc.no/globalassets/graphics/nrcpeople/privacy-notice-for-recruitment.pdf, snippet). |
| UK, US, Canada employers | No photo, no age, no marital status. FCDO wants fully anonymised CVs. | Career blogs, snippet (https://www.betterpic.io/blog/cv-photos-2025-best-practices); research/03 §5.1, §5.2. |
| Germany (including GIZ) | Photo **optional**. Often attached in German practice but not required by law. GIZ asks for CV and cover letter; its form sets photo size limits, which implies it is optional. **(region-specific)** | GIZ pages and German university guides, snippet (https://www.giz.de/en/worldwide/43524.html; https://uni-bremen.de/en/career-center/counselling/how-to-apply-in-germany/application-documents). |
| Gulf and wider Middle East, corporate and CSR roles | Photo **common**, conservative attire. Follow the advert. **(region-specific, career blogs only)** | Snippet (https://www.betterpic.io/blog/cv-photos-2025-best-practices). |
| France | Mixed: traditional, becoming optional. **(uncertain)** | Snippet (same). |

**Agent rule:** ask the client's target employer type and country, then apply the row. Default to no photo for UN, INGO and donor applications.

### 1.5 Keywords and tailoring

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mirror the advert's **exact terms** where true, including competency names. | Don't swap the advert's words for synonyms. | UN letters and forms are assessed against the competencies in the job opening (snippet via https://www.unjobnet.org/es/career-resources/how-to-write-a-un-cover-letter). |
| Tailor to the organisation's sub-sector (health NGO vs agriculture foundation). | Don't reuse one CV for every NGO. | Failing to align the CV with the post is one of the most common errors (Devex, snippet: https://www.devex.com/news/nonprofit-cvs-what-you-need-to-know-61037). |
| Tailor to the **problem the post was created to solve**. | Don't list generic skills. | Kathryn Harper via Devex (snippet: https://www.devex.com/news/considering-a-career-pivot-3-cv-tips-for-boosting-your-application-111002). |
| Spell out acronyms once (HR screeners are not always specialists). | Don't fill the page with jargon. | Bridgespan advises avoiding jargon (snippet: https://www.bridgespan.org/insights/writing-effective-resumes-cover-letters); existing `ngo-two-page.md` §6. |
| **US-funded employers (region-specific, 2025-2026):** check how the employer itself now describes gender and inclusion work, and mirror its wording. | Don't remove true experience. Don't guess. | Aid groups removed DEI, gender and climate language from websites ahead of US funding decisions (The New Humanitarian, snippet: https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/news/2025/04/24/aid-groups-are-erasing-climate-change-their-websites). A January 2026 US rule targets "equity ideology" in foreign assistance (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-27/html/2026-01517.htm, snippet). No evidence was found of CV screening changes. **(uncertain)** |

### 1.6 ATS and portals in the sector

- **Fact:** In a UNjobnet sample of international-organisation listings, the organisation's own system was most common (37.7%), then Oracle (13.8%), Workday (9.6%), Inspira (7.6%) and Cornerstone (2.8%). Oleeo did not appear (https://www.unjobnet.org/career-resources/international-organization-application-systems, snippet). Impactpool notes surprisingly low use of standard recruitment systems in international organisations (snippet: https://www.impactpool.org/employers/white_papers/guide-to-buying-a-recruitment-system).
- **Do:** detect the system from the apply link (for example "myworkdayjobs" means Workday) and choose the output: form text for bespoke portals and Inspira; a one-column, plain file for Workday, Oracle and corporate CSR ATS (snippet, same source; plain-layout rules are general practice, see `formats/corporate-csr.md`).
- **Don't:** assume keyword-scoring software reads UN motivation statements. No source confirmed that Inspira screens by keyword. Screening is by yes/no eligibility questions, then human review (snippet via https://careers.tufts.edu/resources/applying-for-a-un-job-using-inspira/). **(uncertain)**

### 1.7 Common mistakes the agent should flag

| Flag | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate buzzwords ("self-starter", "results-driven") | Replace with one proof per claim | Bond (opened) |
| Passion without evidence ("passionate about development") | Show a related experience or result | Idealist: show examples that demonstrate mission commitment (snippet: https://idealistcareers.org/what-do-hiring-managers-look-for/) |
| Saviour or pity language about communities | Describe people as actors with names of groups and roles | Manchester Anthropology of Aid (opened) |
| Vague phrases ("enhanced project outcomes") | Concrete result | Cravens (opened) |
| Bank or civil-service CV reused unchanged | Rewrite for sector | Nigerian NGO CV guide (snippet) |
| Mixing unrelated tasks in one bullet | One task per bullet | Cravens (opened) |
| Unverifiable claims | Only facts referees and documents can confirm | UNjobnet (opened); research/03 §4.3 |

## 2. Cover letters and motivation statements

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **One page or less** for a free-form letter. Follow any limit exactly. | Don't exceed portal limits. | Devex lists keeping the letter to one page as a basic rule (snippet: https://www.devex.com/news/5-common-global-development-cv-mistakes-86811). |
| Inspira: keep the motivation statement **within 2,000 characters**, about 3 to 4 paragraphs, targeted to the job. | Don't paste a 600-word letter into Inspira. | Snippet citing official guidance (via https://careers.tufts.edu/resources/applying-for-a-un-job-using-inspira/). **(verify on the live form)** |
| State **what you offer**, not what you want from the UN. | Don't write about your dream of working for the UN. | Snippet (same search, UN guidance). |
| Map each paragraph to the **named competencies and requirements**. | Don't write a general story of your life. | UN letters are assessed against competencies and the terms of reference (snippet via https://www.unjobnet.org/es/career-resources/how-to-write-a-un-cover-letter). |
| UK and some INGOs: write a **supporting statement against the person specification**, one heading per essential criterion. | Don't skip it. | Charities weight the statement heavily; skipping it can mean no interview (ClientEarth via search, snippet: https://clientearth.org/about/careers/practical-tips). CharityJob guidance on criteria headings (snippet: https://www.charityjob.co.uk/careeradvice/how-to-write-a-personal-statement-for-a-charity-job/). Some charities allow up to two sides of A4 (snippet). |
| **Mission alignment done right:** name one specific programme, place, strategy or value, then link it to one real experience. | Don't use clichés: "make a difference", "give back", "help those in need", "passionate". | 86% of hiring managers rate understanding of the mission as very important (Idealist survey, snippet). Bond warns that empty buzzwords tell the reader little (opened). Pity or rescue framing signals colonial assumptions (Manchester, opened). |
| Treat motivation as **reciprocal and reflective** where asked about values: what you learned, the limits of outside help, working under local leadership. | Don't present yourself as a rescuer or as doing the sector a favour. | Manchester toolkit asks reviewers to look for reflexivity and structural awareness (opened). A recruiter warns against treating the move as a favour to the sector (snippet via https://directory.onpurpose.org/en/career-change-hub/ search results). |
| Smaller nonprofits: treat the letter as a **writing sample**. Proofread twice. | Don't send with one error. | Bridgespan: letters matter more in nonprofits; one error raises red flags (snippet). |
| Check each employer's **AI rule** before using AI on the letter. | Don't assume one rule fits all. | UNDP allows proofreading (research/03 §3.7). A charity guide on CharityJob advises against AI writing the statement (snippet: https://downloads.charityjob.co.uk/attachments/approved_how_to_apply_for_a_vacancy_using_your_cv_and_supporting_statement_july__2026_07_07_04_18_48_pm.pdf). |
| Check **eligibility before writing**. | Don't write letters for posts where minimums are not met. | No letter overcomes missing education or experience minimums (snippet). |

**Application questionnaires (agent rule):** answer each pre-screening question directly, truthfully, and consistently with the form. One point, one example. Yes/no screening questions are usually eliminatory (snippets; research/03 §2).

## 3. Terminology

### 3.1 Corporate to sector translation (for shifters, segment C)

| Corporate term | Sector term to use (if true) | Notes and source |
|---|---|---|
| Customers, users | People reached, communities, participants, rights-holders, people affected by crisis | CHS 2024 uses "people and communities affected by crisis" language (https://www.chsalliance.org/get-support/article/2024-a-stronger-chs-created-with-people-in-crisis/, opened). "Rights-holders" and "duty-bearers" come from the human rights-based approach (https://www.unfpa.org/node/8495, snippet). |
| KPIs, performance tracking | Indicators, results framework, logframe, MEAL (Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning) | IRC MEAL Handbook (https://www.rescue.org/sites/default/files/2022-12/EN_MEAL%20Handbook_2022%20EXTERNAL.pdf, snippet). |
| Strategy rationale | Theory of change (why a strategy should work in a context, short to long term) | ARISE MEL glossary (snippet: https://www.arisefdn.org/_files/ugd/d4fd98_e498bd4fdd05442bb38c6eb3ac13aea1.pdf). |
| Client funding, revenue | Grants, awards, donor funding, resource mobilisation, grant management, donor compliance | Existing `formats/corporate-csr.md` §5. |
| Suppliers, vendors | Implementing partners, local partners, sub-grantees | Same. |
| Risk and compliance | Safeguarding, PSEA, Do No Harm, conflict sensitivity, anti-fraud, donor compliance | PSEA: IASC Six Core Principles (https://psea.interagencystandingcommittee.org/update/iasc-six-core-principles, snippet). |
| Quality standard | Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS, 2024 edition, nine commitments), Sphere | CHS Alliance (opened). |
| Local market expansion | Localisation, locally led action, partnership with local and national actors | Grand Bargain 25% target, extended to 2026; reported progress far lower (snippet: https://alnap.org/help-library/resources/sohs-2026/chapter-4-power-imbalances-and-improving-accountability/4-1-funding-pathways-and-the-politics-behind-the-numbers/). |
| Field visits | Field / duty station / country office experience | Existing formats. |

**Donor names the agent should recognise and expand once:** USAID (dismantled 2025, residual State Department funding), FCDO (UK), ECHO (EU humanitarian), EU INTPA / Global Europe, GIZ and KfW (Germany), Sida (Sweden), AFD (France), development banks (World Bank, regional banks), Gulf donors (QFFD, KSrelief, UAE), foundations (research/01 §2).

### 3.2 Terms to avoid or use with care

| Avoid | Prefer | Why and source |
|---|---|---|
| "Help the poor", "save", "rescue", "voiceless", "the needy" | Name the group and their role: "women-led cooperatives", "refugee-led organisations", "communities in X" | Pity or rescue framing signals colonial assumptions (Manchester, opened). |
| "Give back", "make a difference" (as the main motive) | A specific link between your skill and their programme | Cliché; does not show fit (Bond, opened; interpretation). |
| "Empower" (as something you do to people) | Rights, autonomy, equality, community organising | Bond's language grid replaces "empowerment" (snippet: https://www.bond.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Bond_Decoloinising-and-depoliticising_updated-2022.pdf). |
| "Capacity building" (one-way) | Sharing learning and knowledge, partnership, accompaniment | Bond grid (snippet). |
| "Beneficiaries" (in values or motivation text) | People, communities, participants, rights-holders. **Keep "beneficiaries" when the advert or donor uses it** (for example in logframe or reporting contexts). | Bond members agreed to drop it but found no universal alternative (snippet). IASC PSEA text itself still uses "beneficiaries" (snippet). **(context-dependent)** |
| "Developing countries", "third world" | Name the country or region | Bond grid (snippet). |
| "Mission" for a work assignment (MSF context) | "Assignment" | MSF anti-racist guide (snippet: https://www.msf.org/sites/default/files/2023-12/Anti-racist%20Best%20Practices.pdf). |
| Corporate superlatives: "leverage", "synergy", "disrupt" | Plain verbs | Bridgespan, Bond (above). |

**Agent rule:** the right register depends on the reader. Donor and UN forms use technical terms (logframe, beneficiaries, outputs). Values and motivation text should avoid charity framing. Advocacy and rights-based organisations prefer "rights-holders" (interpretation).

## 4. Hiring processes and interview norms

(Research/03 covers the 5-step pattern, tests, frameworks and checks. This section adds rules and new facts only.)

### 4.1 Process rules

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Plan for **3 to 9 months** from UN deadline to offer. Run applications in parallel. A status enquiry after about 3 months is reasonable. | Don't read silence as rejection. Don't chase weekly. | UNjobnet timeline guide (https://www.unjobnet.org/career-resources/real-un-application-timeline, opened). GA's 120-day target is still not met (same). |
| Treat the **day before the deadline** as the real cutoff. Weekend deadlines are not extended. | Don't apply on the last day. Applying early does not improve odds either. | Same (opened). Median UN application window is about 14 days (same; unvacancies September 2026 data, snippet). |
| Keep the profile, PHP and evidence ready before a post appears. | Don't start the PHP from zero for each post. | Same (opened). |
| Treat a **roster** placement as a real asset; keep the profile current. | Don't rely on it: being rostered is not a queue that reliably reaches you. | UNjobnet hidden market page (snippet: https://www.unjobnet.org/career-resources/hidden-un-job-market). Roster validity is **(uncertain)**: see "Fits with existing files". |
| Prepare **references early** (UN: often 3; US embassies: 3 personal + 3 professional), consent for the Misconduct Disclosure Scheme, police certificate. | Don't wait until the offer. | research/03 §5.1, §6.4. |
| Know the **contract type** before applying (see §5.3). | Don't assume consultancy leads to staff posts. | Contractors cannot be hired directly into staff posts; JPO, UNV and consultancy carry no expectation of a staff job (snippets: https://www.unjobnet.org/career-resources/un-contract-types-explained; hidden market page). |
| Watch for **proposal recruitment**: "contingent on funding", "key personnel", "letter of commitment". | Don't count it as a firm job. | A key-personnel commitment applies only if the bidder wins (DOE/NNSA template, snippet: https://www.energy.gov/nnsa/articles/section-l-attachment-g). Applies to USAID-style contractors and EU-funded bids (interpretation). |
| Treat any request for **fees or bank details** as a scam. | Never pay for "processing", "visa" or "induction". | UN vacancies carry a no-fee statement; UN does not ask for bank details (UNESCAP, snippet: https://unescap.org/about/fraud-alert). UNON warned of scams tied to relocations to Nairobi in 2025 (snippet: https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2025-08-28-beware-of-scammers-unon-warns-kenyans). |

### 4.2 Interview norms that differ from corporate

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Answer competency questions with **one real past example**, your own actions, a result. | Don't answer with hypotheticals or "we always...". | UN CBI training (research/03 §3.3). UN coach Judit Toth: panels want your role in past situations (LinkedIn post, snippet). Safer-recruitment guides avoid hypotheticals (snippet: https://www.schools.norfolk.gov.uk/article/68061/8.-Interview-Structure-and-Questions). |
| Show **humility and emotional intelligence**: credit the team, then state your part. ## 5. Salary and negotiation

### 5.1 UN system

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Treat the **grade as fixed** (it belongs to the classified post). Ask about the **step**: "How was my step placement determined, given my experience beyond the minimum?" | Don't ask for "more salary" or a higher grade after the offer. | UNjobnet negotiation guide (snippet: https://www.unjobnet.org/career-resources/negotiating-un-job-offer). |
| Know the step rule: new appointees start at Step I by default; each full year above the minimum can add a step, up to a ceiling (Step VII for P-1 to P-5 in the Secretariat). | Don't accept Step I without checking if you have extra years. | Same (snippet). Verify against UN Staff Rule 3.3 guidance (https://policy.un.org/sites/default/files/2025-05/Implementation%20of%20Staff%20Rule%203.3%20on%20Salary%20Policy%2C%20Determination%20of%20step%20upon%20appointment%2C%20reappointment%2C%20promotion%2C%20or%20change%20of%20category.pdf, not opened). |
| Compare offers on **net pay at the duty station**: base salary is global; post adjustment varies by location. | Don't compare headline base salaries across cities. | Same (snippet). |
| Negotiate what is flexible: **start date**, relocation and shipment, rental subsidy eligibility. | Don't push on fixed scale items. | Same (snippet). |
| Use minimum experience by grade to check fit: P-2 about 2 years, P-3 5, P-4 7 (with a master's; add about 2 with a bachelor's at UNDP). UNICEF NO-1 1 year, NO-2 2, NO-3 5, NO-4 8, NO-5 10. | Don't apply two grades above your experience. | UN Women and UNFPA (snippet: https://www.unfpa.org/resources/united-nations-staff-categories); UNDP table (snippet); UNICEF (opened). Labels differ by agency (NO-A to NO-D vs NO-1 to NO-5). |
| Consultancies: quote within the **published fee band** for the level; clarify whether the call wants a daily rate or a total. | Don't quote far above the band. | UN Secretariat consultant bands, USD 190 a day at level A up to about USD 1,000 to 1,100 at level E (OHR/PG/2025/2, snippet: https://policy.un.org/sites/default/files/2025-01/OHR_PG_2025_2_consultant_contractor_fee_range.pdf). UNDP raised its bands in Sept 2025 (snippet: https://popp.undp.org/policy-page/individual-contract). Donors check rates against market and salary history (Devex FAQ, snippet). |

### 5.2 INGOs, NGOs and donor-funded projects

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ask for the **salary scale and the placement criteria**. Research local cost of living and comparable posts. | Don't play hardball with a strict pay band. | Devex: hardball rarely works with strict bands; may work with private consultancies (snippet: https://www.devex.com/news/salary-negotiation-strategies-for-aid-workers-and-consultants-a-primer-74029). Impactpool MENA advice: research the scale and placement criteria (snippet: https://impactpool.org/article/earning-the-salary-you-deserve-for-work-in-the-mena-region). |
| Raise pay **only once an offer is near**. Ask whether there is room and what criteria they use. | Don't open with salary. | Devex primer (snippet). |
| Know that **national and international scales** usually differ. | Don't assume a national hire can move to an expat package. | Dual salary systems are common and hurt national staff motivation (Bond, snippet: https://www.bond.org.uk/news/2021/01/interrogating-ingos-dual-salary-systems-to-address-inequality). Some INGOs are moving to single scales (snippet). |
| Negotiate non-salary items: leave, flexible or remote work, learning budget, start date. | Don't ignore benefits. | CharityJob (snippet: https://www.charityjob.co.uk/careeradvice/what-to-negotiate-in-a-job-offer-besides-salary/). |
| US-funded projects: leverage is highest **at proposal stage**, when the budget is being built. | Don't expect much movement after award. | Devex on USAID contractors (snippet: https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-negotiate-your-salary-with-a-usaid-contractor-86908). Historical; USAID is gone but the same logic applies to other donor-funded bids (interpretation). |
| Shifters: expect **lower pay** than corporate. Say so calmly if asked. | Don't anchor on a corporate salary. | Laura Gassner Otting, nonprofit search firm founder, via Chronicle of Philanthropy (snippet: https://www.philanthropy.com/news/a-recruiter-gives-guidance-to-those-seeking-switch-to-a-nonprofit-career/). Of laid-off staff who found work, 83% earn less (research/01 §4.1). |

## 7. Current market (2025-2026) and job security

(Research/01 has the funding numbers. New facts only.)

- **Vacancies collapsed.** ReliefWeb vacancies fell about 65% between 2024 and 2025. Terminating contracts was the most common organisational response (50.4% of survey respondents) (ALNAP SOHS 2026, https://alnap.org/help-library/resources/sohs-2026/chapter-1-prioritisation-under-pressure/1-3-organisational-priorities-after-funding-cuts/, opened). Devex data shows overall aid job postings down about 25% in 2025 (snippet: https://www.devex.com/news/the-roles-most-affected-by-aid-cuts-and-those-which-aren-t-111075).
- **Who was hit:** local NGOs cut quickest and deepest because of project funding and low reserves; local female staff and young professionals were hit hardest; several UN bodies removed junior posts (ALNAP, opened).
- **Roles:** M&E postings down 38%; chief of party postings down 94.1%; global health roles down over 45%; ICT postings more than doubled; funding and fundraising roles up 149% in the first nine months of 2025 (Devex, snippets, same URL and https://www.devex.com/news/what-happened-to-key-development-roles-in-2025-111245).
- **UN80:** the Secretariat plans about a fifth fewer staff positions in 2026 (21% in one source; 18.8% in research/03); about 230 posts relocate to lower-cost duty stations such as Nairobi; hiring freezes are widespread (snippets: https://ukraine.un.org/en/node/316288; https://www.mopan.org/en/our-work/performance-insights/multilateral-effectiveness-in-a-shifting-landscape/multilateral-effectiveness-in-a-shifting-landscape-key-messages-un80.html).
- **Employer preferences:** some INGOs now look for private-sector experience for country director roles; mergers and restructuring are rising (Devex, snippets: https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573/amp).
- **Single-donor risk is real:** one organisation lost 60% of its funding in six months when its main bilateral donor shifted (DevelopmentAid column, snippet: https://developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/192460/how-to-win-donors-when-aid-budgets-collapse).

### Rules for evaluating how secure a job is

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Look for the **funding source** in the advert (donor, grant name, project end date). Ask at interview: share of budget from the largest donor, grant end date, whether the post is tied to one award. | Don't assume an INGO post is permanent. | Devex reporting suggests asking these questions (snippet, above). |
| Score **lower** for: "subject to funding", proposal recruitment, single-donor projects, US-funded projects, sub-sectors with the deepest cuts (global health, humanitarian, governance; research/01 §2.4). | Don't treat these as automatic no's. | They can still be good bridges (interpretation). |
| Score **higher** for: core-funded posts, multi-donor organisations, growing funders (development banks, EU, Gulf donors, some foundations), growing functions (fundraising, partnerships, ICT, compliance). | | research/01 §5, §7; Devex role data (snippets). |
| For segment D, treat consultancies and short contracts as **bridges** and say so in the plan. | Don't frame them as failure. | Consulting keeps people in the sector but is not long-term security (snippet: https://www.devex.com/news/the-new-reality-for-global-development-consultants-111968). |

## 10. How the career-ops scoring rubric should differ

career-ops scores 1 to 5 against the CV, in blocks A to H (role summary, weighted CV match, level strategy, comp research, personalisation, interview prep, posting-open check). It flags explicit no-sponsorship as a hard blocker and advises skipping roles below 4.0 (https://github.com/career-ops-hq/career-ops, opened). All weights below are **(proposal)**.

### 10.1 Hard gates (run before scoring; a fail means "not eligible", not a low score)

| Gate | Check | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Nationality | NO posts: nationals of the duty-station country only. EPSO permanent exams: EU nationals only. Some programmes (YPP, JPO) list eligible countries each year. | UNICEF (opened); research/03 §3.5, §4.1 |
| Right to work | G posts and embassy local posts need existing legal right to work; no sponsorship. | UNFPA (snippet); research/03 §5.5 |
| Minimum education and years | Eliminatory at UN screening. Use the grade table in §5.1. | Snippet (§2); UNICEF (opened) |
| Required languages | Treat "required" as a gate; "desirable" as a score. | ICRC (snippet); research/03 |
| Age limit | YPP: 32 or younger in exam year. | research/03 §3.5 |
| Documents | Europass, declaration on honour, P11, statement: all present? | research/03 §4.3 |

### 10.2 Scored dimensions (proposal)

| Dimension | Weight | What raises the score | What lowers it |
|---|---|---|---|
| CV match to essential criteria | 30% | Each essential criterion met with a real example | Missing essentials; only desirables met |
| Funding security | 15% | Core-funded; multi-donor; growing funder or function | "Subject to funding"; single donor; proposal recruitment; deep-cut sub-sector |
| Contract type and length | 10% | FTA or staff post; 2+ years | Under 6 months; consultancy (unless the client wants it) |
| Career trajectory | 10% | One grade up (segment B); entry route (A); bridge into sector (C, D) | Two grades up; dead-end non-staff loop |
| Mission and values fit | 10% | Client's stated themes and approaches match | Mismatch (for example faith-based or advocacy stance the client rejects) |
| Duty station and security | 10% | Matches client's mobility, family status, risk tolerance | Non-family or high-risk post the client ruled out; forced relocation |
| Pay vs client's needs | 5% | Net pay at duty station meets client's floor | National scale below floor; unpaid internship |
| Process cost | 5% | Short process; no test or one test | Written test plus assessment centre plus 6-month timeline when the client needs income fast |
| Competition signal | 5% | National post, niche language, rare skill | Generic global P-3 post with hundreds of applicants |

### 10.3 Segment adjustments (proposal)

- **A New grads:** raise weight on entry routes (NO-1/NO-A, UNV, paid internships, YPP); lower the 4.0 skip threshold to 3.5 because entry posts are scarce (ALNAP: young professionals hit hardest).
- **B Moving up:** flag any post more than one grade above current level.
- **C Shifters:** add a "translation" check: do the client's corporate results map to the advert's sector terms (§3.1)? Prefer CSR, foundations, banks, fundraising, partnerships and ICT (Devex role data).
- **D Repositioning:** raise funding-security weight to 25%; accept consultancies as bridges; flag deep-cut sub-sectors and suggest adjacent ones.

### 10.4 Other agent behaviours

- **Repost rule:** UN posts are often re-advertised; a repost is not a red flag by itself (interpretation; career-ops Block G).
- **Fact check stays on:** career-ops blocks unsupported facts; keep it. UN forms are signed as true (UNjobnet, opened).
- **Region switch:** photo and personal-data rules from §1.4.
- **AI disclosure:** surface each employer's AI rule before generating text (§2).
- **Calibrate:** log outcomes per sector and grade; sector hiring is slow, so calibrate over months, not weeks (UNjobnet timeline, opened).
