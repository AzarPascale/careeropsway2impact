## 1. The sector in 2026: ten-bullet summary

1. **Aid has shrunk sharply.** OECD preliminary data show DAC donors gave USD 174.3 billion in ODA in 2025. That is a 23.1% real-terms fall, the largest annual drop on record, bringing aid back to about 2015 levels. The OECD projects a further fall of about 5.8% in 2026. Source: OECD press release, 9 April 2026, https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2026/04/international-aid-fell-sharply-in-2025-says-oecd.html `[snippet; page returned 403]`, also https://sdg.iisd.org/news/oda-in-historic-decline-latest-oecd-data/ `[snippet]`
2. **The US withdrawal is the main driver.** US ODA fell about 57% and accounted for roughly three-quarters of the global drop (same OECD sources `[snippet]`). USAID ended about 6,780 of 7,510 awards between January 2025 and March 2026, and its remaining programmes moved to the State Department. Sources: GAO-26-108607, https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108607 `[snippet]`; Devex, https://www.devex.com/news/remaining-usaid-programs-now-under-state-department-5-200-programs-canceled-109607 `[snippet]`
3. **European donors are cutting too.** The UK is moving from 0.5% to 0.3% of GNI by 2027. France, Germany, the Netherlands and Sweden have announced reductions. Sources: https://www.developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/192723/uk-reduce-aid-to-0-3 `[snippet]`; https://www.cgdev.org/blog/will-uk-miss-its-much-reduced-aid-target `[snippet]`
4. **Humanitarian appeals are now "prioritised".** The Global Humanitarian Overview 2026 counts 239 million people in need. It targets 135 million, with a top priority of 87 million lives, at USD 23 billion prioritised (about USD 33 billion in total). Sources: https://humanitarianaction.info/article/2026-global-humanitarian-overview-collective-push-protect-millions-lives `[snippet]`; https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/news/2025/12/08/five-takeaways-uns-aid-plans-2026 `[snippet]`
5. **2025 brought the largest funding gap in appeal history.** Only 35% of UN-coordinated appeal requirements were met, a USD 30.5 billion shortfall. Source: ALNAP State of the Humanitarian System (SOHS) 2026, https://alnap.org/help-library/resources/sohs-2026/chapter-1-prioritisation-under-pressure/1-1-the-reality-of-funding-gaps/ `[opened]`
6. **The "Humanitarian Reset" is reshaping coordination.** Tom Fletcher launched it in March 2025. Its main elements are hyper-prioritisation, fewer clusters, more local leadership and lighter bureaucracy. Sources: https://www.nutritioncluster.net/sites/nutritioncluster.com/files/2025-07/Humanitarian%20Reset%20-%20Cluster%20Simplification%20final%20proposal.pdf `[snippet]`; https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/06/1164421 `[snippet]`
7. **The UN itself is downsizing and restructuring.** The 2026 regular budget proposal cut USD 577 million (15.1%) and 2,681 posts (18.8%). UN80 reform proposals include merging UNDP with UNOPS and UNFPA with UN Women, and sunsetting UNAIDS. Sources: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/12/2/un-will-cut-2026-budget-by-577m-by-slashing-workforce `[snippet]`; https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/04/1167253 `[snippet]`
8. **The jobs market contracted hard.** ReliefWeb vacancies fell about 65% between 2024 and 2025. More than 12,000 staff contracts were terminated by April 2025, and local NGOs were hit fastest and deepest. Source: ALNAP SOHS 2026, https://alnap.org/help-library/resources/sohs-2026/chapter-1-prioritisation-under-pressure/1-3-organisational-priorities-after-funding-cuts/ `[opened]`
9. **The donor map is diversifying.** Gulf donors (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar) rose from 10% to 17% of public humanitarian funding in 2025. Philanthropy is growing but cannot fill the gap, for example Gates plans about USD 200 billion before closing in 2045. Sources: https://alnap.org/help-library/resources/sohs-2026/chapter-1-prioritisation-under-pressure/1-1-the-search-for-alternative-funding/ `[opened]`; https://www.devex.com/news/bill-gates-commits-most-of-his-fortune-to-gates-foundation-closing-2045-110021 `[snippet]`
10. **The SDGs are off track, while climate, AI and "shift the power" set the agenda.** Only 35% of SDG targets are on track or making moderate progress. Sources: https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/files/report/2025/SDG_Report_2025_Press_Release_EN.pdf `[snippet]`; COP30 outcome, https://www.wri.org/insights/cop30-outcomes-next-steps `[snippet]`

### 2.2 How money flows

```
Taxpayers / endowments / corporate profits
        |
Bilateral donor (FCDO, ECHO, BMZ, State Dept...) / foundation / Gulf fund
        |-----------------> Multilateral (World Bank, global funds) --> Government ministries --> contractors / NGOs
        |-----------------> UN agency (WFP, UNICEF, UNHCR...) ---------> INGO or national NGO "implementing partner"
        |-----------------> Pooled fund (CERF, CBPF, Start Fund) -------> UN / INGO / local NGO
        |-----------------> INGO (prime / lead) ------------------------> local NGO (subgrantee / partner) --> communities
        |-----------------> Contractor (DAI, Chemonics) ----------------> subcontractors / local grantees
```

Key points for the AI:
- Each layer takes overhead (ICR or NICRA) and passes compliance down. Local actors carry the risk but receive little money directly. In 2023 Grand Bargain signatories gave 4.4% of funding to local and national actors, and only 0.6% directly. Source: https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2025/article/local-possible-progress-remains-incremental `[snippet]`
- Country-Based Pooled Funds are the exception: about 31% went directly to local actors in 2023. Source: https://odihpn.org/en/publication/localisation-in-words-centralisation-in-practice-why-local-leaders-still-dont-lead/ `[snippet]`
- Government donors that signed the Grand Bargain gave only USD 0.5 billion directly to local and national actors over 2022 to 2024. Source: https://media.odi.org/documents/HST-LHL_localistion_report_final.pdf `[snippet]`
- Total international humanitarian assistance fell about 20% in 2025 to USD 33.3 billion (preliminary). Source: GHA Report 2026 summary, https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/node/89686_fr `[snippet]` `UNCERTAIN`, as figures differ by scope
- Private funding is about 20% of international humanitarian assistance. Source: https://alnap.org/help-library/resources/sohs-2026/chapter-1-prioritisation-under-pressure/1-1-the-search-for-alternative-funding/ `[opened]`
- New US model: in December 2025 the US pledged USD 2 billion for UN humanitarian aid in 2026, through an OCHA-managed pooled mechanism. Conditions include consolidation and new oversight, and some contexts (for example Yemen, Afghanistan, Gaza) are excluded. Source: https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/u-s-pledges-2-billion-for-un-humanitarian-aid-as-trump-slashes-funding-and-warns-agencies-to-adapt-shrink-or-die `[snippet]`

### 2.3 Humanitarian, development and peace: the "nexus"

| | Humanitarian | Development | Peacebuilding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal | Save lives, reduce suffering now | Long-term poverty reduction, systems, institutions | Prevent conflict, sustain peace |
| Time frame | Weeks to 12 months, annual appeals | 3 to 7 year programmes | Multi-year, political |
| Principles | Humanity, neutrality, impartiality, independence | National ownership, aid effectiveness | Conflict sensitivity, inclusion |
| Main tools | HRP, clusters, CERF, cash, in-kind aid | Country strategies, MDB loans, budget support | Mediation, social cohesion, SSR, DDR |
| Key actors | OCHA, WFP, UNHCR, ICRC, MSF, NRC | World Bank, UNDP, GIZ, contractors | DPPA, PBF, Interpeace, Search for Common Ground |

The "triple nexus" (HDP nexus), formalised in the OECD DAC Recommendation of 2019, asks these actors to work towards "collective outcomes" in protracted crises. As funds shrink, nexus language now appears with resilience, anticipatory action and government-led social protection. `UNCERTAIN`: this summary is common sector knowledge and no specific 2026 source was opened for it.

## 3. Glossary (140 terms)

Format: **Term**: plain-English meaning. *Job ads/CVs:* how it appears.

### 3.1 Programme cycle and MEAL (22)

1. **Project cycle / Programme cycle**: the steps of assessment, design, implementation, monitoring, evaluation and learning. *Ads:* "full project cycle management experience".
2. **Needs assessment**: structured study of what affected people need (for example MSNA, multi-sector needs assessment). *CV:* "Led MSNA covering 3,200 households".
3. **Theory of Change (ToC)**: the logic of how activities lead to long-term change. *Ads:* "develop ToC and results frameworks".
4. **Logframe (logical framework)**: a table of goal, outcomes, outputs, activities, indicators and assumptions. *CV:* "designed logframes for ECHO and FCDO proposals".
5. **Results framework**: a donor's version of a logframe (USAID/State, World Bank). *Ads:* "results-based management (RBM)".
6. **Indicator (SMART)**: a measurable sign of progress. *CV:* "tracked 24 indicators, 92% on target".
7. **Baseline / endline**: measurements at the start and end of a project. *CV:* "managed baseline and endline surveys".
8. **MEAL / MEL / M&E**: monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning. *Ads:* "MEAL Manager", "MEL Specialist".
9. **Monitoring**: routine tracking of activities and outputs. *CV:* "post-distribution monitoring (PDM)".
10. **Evaluation (mid-term, final, real-time, impact)**: independent judgement of results, often using OECD DAC criteria. *Ads:* "commission and manage evaluations".
11. **OECD DAC evaluation criteria**: relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, impact, sustainability. *Ads:* in evaluation ToRs.
12. **Learning / adaptive management**: changing course based on evidence. *CV:* "introduced quarterly learning reviews leading to programme pivots".
13. **Third-party monitoring (TPM)**: an outside firm monitors where staff cannot go. *Ads:* common in Somalia, Syria, Afghanistan.
14. **Remote management**: running programmes without access to the area. *CV:* "remote-managed operations in hard-to-reach areas".
15. **Inception phase / inception report**: start-up period that confirms plans with the donor. *Ads:* in consultancy ToRs.
16. **Terms of Reference (ToR)**: the scope of work for a consultant or evaluation. *Ads:* "see attached ToR".
17. **Proposal development / concept note**: writing funding bids. *Ads:* "lead proposal development", "business development".
18. **Data protection / responsible data**: safe handling of personal data of affected people. *Ads:* "knowledge of data protection principles".
19. **KoBo / ODK / Power BI / ActivityInfo**: common data collection and dashboard tools. *CV:* list them by name.
20. **Feedback and complaints mechanism (CFM / FCRM)**: channels for communities to raise issues. *CV:* "set up hotline, 1,400 cases resolved".
21. **Value for money (VfM)**: economy, efficiency, effectiveness, equity (FCDO "4Es"). *Ads:* FCDO-funded roles.
22. **Cost per beneficiary / cost-efficiency analysis**: cost of reaching each person. *CV:* "cut cost per beneficiary by 18%".

### 3.2 Humanitarian coordination (20)

23. **OCHA**: UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs. *Ads:* "Humanitarian Affairs Officer".
24. **ERC (Emergency Relief Coordinator)**: head of OCHA (Tom Fletcher since 2024). *Context:* drives the Humanitarian Reset.
25. **IASC (Inter-Agency Standing Committee)**: top forum of UN, Red Cross and NGO leaders on humanitarian policy. *Ads:* "familiarity with IASC guidance".
26. **HC / RC / RC-HC**: Humanitarian Coordinator and Resident Coordinator, the most senior UN official in a country. *Ads:* senior roles.
27. **HCT (Humanitarian Country Team)**: country-level strategic forum. *CV:* "represented NGO forum at HCT".
28. **Cluster system**: sector coordination groups (Food Security, Health, WASH, Shelter, Protection, Nutrition, Education, Logistics, CCCM and others), each with a lead agency. In 2025 the reset proposed streamlining clusters (reported as down to 8). Source: https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2025/06/mil-250619-unocha02.htm `[snippet]` `UNCERTAIN` on the final count. *Ads:* "Cluster Coordinator", "Co-coordinator".
29. **Area of Responsibility (AoR)**: sub-cluster under Protection (Child Protection, GBV, Mine Action, HLP). *Ads:* "GBV AoR Coordinator".
30. **Inter-cluster coordination group (ICCG)**: where clusters coordinate with each other. *CV:* "member of ICCG".
31. **HPC (Humanitarian Programme Cycle)**: annual cycle of needs overview, response plan and monitoring. *Ads:* "lead HPC process".
32. **HNO (Humanitarian Needs Overview)**: annual analysis of needs in a country. *Ads:* in IM roles.
33. **HRP / HNRP (Humanitarian (Needs and) Response Plan)**: the country appeal and strategy. *CV:* "contributed to HNRP 2026 chapter".
34. **GHO (Global Humanitarian Overview)**: the annual global appeal, launched each December. *Context:* GHO 2026 at https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026 `[snippet]`.
35. **Hyper-prioritisation**: focusing appeals on the most life-threatening needs only. Source: https://alnap.org/help-library/resources/sohs-2026/chapter-1-prioritisation-under-pressure/1-2-prioritising-people-and-programmes/ `[snippet]`. *Ads:* "prioritisation and targeting".
36. **Flash appeal / regional refugee response plan (RRP)**: a sudden-onset appeal, or a UNHCR-led plan for refugees. *CV:* "Sudan RRP".
37. **CERF (Central Emergency Response Fund)**: global UN emergency fund managed by OCHA. *CV:* "managed CERF allocation".
38. **CBPF (Country-Based Pooled Fund)**: OCHA-managed country funds that also fund NGOs directly. *Ads:* "Humanitarian Financing Officer".
39. **Scale-up / system-wide activation**: an IASC declaration for a major crisis. *Ads:* surge roles.
40. **Humanitarian access / civil-military coordination (CMCoord)**: negotiating safe reach to people and coordinating with armed actors. *Ads:* "Access Officer", "access negotiation".
41. **Humanitarian Reset**: the 2025 reform push by the ERC for a leaner, more local, prioritised system. *CV:* frame efficiency and localisation work in reset terms.
42. **Information management (IM)**: data, maps, 3W/4W matrices (who does what, where, when). *Ads:* "IM Officer, 4W, dashboards".

### 3.3 Finance, grants and compliance (22)

43. **Grant management**: running a donor grant from award to close-out. *Ads:* "Grants Manager".
44. **Award / agreement / contract**: grant (assistance) vs contract (acquisition). Contracts are tighter. *CV:* name the instrument type.
45. **Prime / sub-award / subgrantee**: lead recipient and the partners it funds. *CV:* "managed 12 sub-awards worth USD 4.2m".
46. **Cooperative agreement**: US grant with substantial donor involvement. *Ads:* US-funded roles.
47. **Budget vs actuals (BvA) / burn rate**: spending tracking. *CV:* "kept burn rate within 5% of plan".
48. **Cost extension / no-cost extension (NCE)**: more time with more money, or more time only. *CV:* "negotiated NCE".
49. **Modification / amendment / realignment**: formal change to a grant. *Ads:* grants roles.
50. **Eligible / allowable costs**: what a donor will pay for. *Ads:* "knowledge of donor eligibility rules".
51. **ICR / NICRA / overhead**: indirect cost recovery. NICRA is the US Negotiated Indirect Cost Rate Agreement. *Ads:* finance roles.
52. **Co-financing / match funding**: a share paid from other sources (common with EU). *CV:* "secured 10% co-financing".
53. **Due diligence / partner capacity assessment**: checking a partner's systems before funding. *CV:* "conducted 30 partner capacity assessments".
54. **Compliance**: following donor rules, laws and internal policies. *Ads:* "Compliance Officer".
55. **Anti-terrorism / sanctions screening**: checking staff, partners and vendors against lists. *Ads:* "counter-terrorism compliance".
56. **Fraud, corruption, diversion**: misuse of resources, and the controls to stop it. *Ads:* "anti-fraud", "aid diversion".
57. **Audit (internal, external, donor)**: independent check of finances. *CV:* "zero audit findings".
58. **Procurement thresholds / tender**: rules on buying goods and services. *Ads:* "Supply chain / procurement".
59. **Close-out**: final reporting and disposal at grant end. *CV:* "managed close-out of 5 grants".
60. **Pipeline / business development (BD)**: future funding opportunities. *Ads:* "BD Director" in contractors.
61. **Unrestricted vs restricted / earmarked funding**: flexible versus tied money. *Context:* flexible funding is scarce in 2026.
62. **Multi-year / flexible funding**: Grand Bargain quality-funding goal. *CV:* "secured three-year flexible grant".
63. **Termination / stop-work order**: donor ends or pauses a grant (frequent with USAID in 2025). *CV:* "managed orderly termination and staff transition" is now a credible skill.
64. **Blended finance**: mixing public, philanthropic and private money. *Ads:* MDB, impact investment roles.

72. **PEPFAR / PMI**: US global health initiatives (HIV, malaria). *Context:* under review and reduced. `UNCERTAIN` on current scale.
73. **FCDO**: UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office. *Context:* aid going to 0.3% GNI by 2027.

### 3.5 Principles, standards and accountability (18)

87. **Humanitarian principles**: humanity, neutrality, impartiality, independence. *Ads:* "commitment to humanitarian principles".
88. **IHL (International Humanitarian Law)**: laws of war (Geneva Conventions). *Ads:* ICRC, protection roles.
89. **Do No Harm**: making sure aid does not fuel conflict or hurt people. *CV:* "applied Do No Harm analysis".
90. **Conflict sensitivity**: understanding how a programme interacts with conflict. *Ads:* peacebuilding, fragile states.
91. **CHS (Core Humanitarian Standard)**: nine commitments on quality and accountability, revised in 2024. Source: https://www.chsalliance.org/get-support/article/2024-a-stronger-chs-created-with-people-in-crisis/ `[snippet]`. *Ads:* "knowledge of CHS".
92. **CHS verification / HQAI certification**: independent audit against the CHS. *CV:* "led CHS self-assessment".
93. **Sphere Handbook**: minimum standards in WASH, food, shelter and health, plus the Humanitarian Charter. Current edition 2018. *Ads:* "Sphere standards".
94. **Companion standards**: INEE (education), CPMS (child protection), LEGS (livestock), MERS (economic recovery). *Ads:* technical roles.
95. **AAP (Accountability to Affected People)**: giving communities voice, information and influence. *Ads:* "AAP Officer".
96. **Community engagement / CEA / RCCE**: engaging communities (RCCE is used in health emergencies). *Ads:* comms roles.
97. **PSEA**: protection from sexual exploitation and abuse by aid workers. *Ads:* "PSEA focal point".
98. **Safeguarding**: keeping everyone, especially children and vulnerable adults, safe from harm by the organisation. *Ads:* mandatory statement in almost every ad.
99. **SEAH**: sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment (FCDO term). *Ads:* UK roles.
100. **Misconduct Disclosure Scheme**: inter-agency reference checks on past misconduct. *Ads:* "subject to MDS checks".
101. **Code of Conduct (Red Cross/NGO, 1994)**: ten principles for disaster relief. *Ads:* NGO roles.
102. **Duty of care / security risk management (SRM)**: protecting staff. *Ads:* "Safety and Security Manager".
103. **Gender and inclusion / GEDSI**: gender equality, disability and social inclusion. *Ads:* "GEDSI Advisor".
104. **Disability inclusion / Washington Group questions**: standard data tool on disability. *CV:* "disaggregated data by disability".

### 3.6 Localisation, power and partnerships (8)

105. **Localisation**: shifting money, power and leadership to local and national actors. *Ads:* "Localisation Advisor", "partnership".
106. **Grand Bargain**: a 2016 deal between donors and agencies (target of 25% to local actors "as directly as possible"). The target was never verifiably met. Source: https://alnap.org/help-library/resources/sohs-2026/chapter-4-power-imbalances-and-improving-accountability/4-1-funding-pathways-and-the-politics-behind-the-numbers/ `[snippet]`.
107. **L/NNGO, LNA**: local or national NGO, local and national actors. *Ads:* in partnership roles.
108. **Locally led development / LLA**: initiatives owned and led by people in their own context (LLA also means locally led adaptation in climate). Source: https://www.peacedirect.org/content/uploads/2026/05/From-Aid-to-Solidarity.pdf `[snippet]`.
109. **Shift the Power / decolonising aid**: movement to transfer agenda-setting and budget control to the Global South. Same source `[snippet]`.
110. **Equitable partnership / Charter4Change / Pledge for Change**: commitments by INGOs on fair partnership. *CV:* "co-designed programme with partners".
111. **Capacity sharing / strengthening**: preferred over "capacity building" (which implies one-way). *CV:* use "capacity sharing".
112. **Fair share of overhead for partners**: passing ICR to local partners. *Ads:* partnership roles.

### 3.7 Thematic sectors and approaches (18)

113. **HDP nexus / triple nexus**: joining humanitarian, development and peace work. *Ads:* "nexus programming".
114. **Cash and voucher assistance (CVA) / MPCA**: giving money or vouchers instead of goods. MPCA means multipurpose cash. CVA was USD 7.3 billion in 2025, 21% of humanitarian assistance. Source: https://alnap.org/help-library/resources/sohs-2026/chapter-2-what-works-and-at-what-cost/2-2-cash/ `[snippet]`. *Ads:* "Cash Officer", "CVA".
115. **CALP / Cash Working Group**: cash learning network and its coordination group. *CV:* "chaired CWG".
116. **Anticipatory action**: acting before a forecast shock hits (pre-arranged finance and triggers). It reached 9.6 million people in 2025. Source: https://www.climatecentre.org/17288/anticipatory-action-reached-9-6-million-people-in-2025/ `[snippet]`. *Ads:* "AA frameworks", "early action protocols".
117. **Resilience**: ability of people and systems to absorb and recover from shocks. *Ads:* "resilience programming".
118. **DRR (Disaster Risk Reduction) / Sendai Framework**: reducing disaster risk. *Ads:* climate, DRR roles.
119. **Climate adaptation / loss and damage**: adjusting to climate impacts, and finance for unavoidable losses. *Ads:* "climate-resilient livelihoods".
120. **Shock-responsive social protection**: using government safety nets in crises. *Ads:* World Bank, WFP roles.
121. **Protection**: keeping people safe from violence, coercion and deprivation, and upholding rights. *Ads:* "Protection Officer".
122. **GBV (gender-based violence) / GBViE**: prevention and response, including case management. *Ads:* "GBV Specialist".
123. **Child protection (CP) / CPiE**: protecting children in emergencies. *Ads:* "CP Case Worker".
124. **MHPSS**: mental health and psychosocial support. *Ads:* health and protection roles.
125. **WASH**: water, sanitation and hygiene. *Ads:* "WASH Engineer".
126. **Food security / IPC**: the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification, phases 1 (minimal) to 5 (catastrophe/famine). *Ads:* "IPC analysis".
127. **Nutrition (CMAM, SAM/MAM)**: treatment of acute malnutrition. *Ads:* "Nutrition Officer".
128. **Livelihoods / economic recovery / market systems (MSD)**: income and markets. *Ads:* "Livelihoods Coordinator".
129. **Shelter / NFI / CCCM**: shelter, non-food items, camp coordination and management. *Ads:* "CCCM Officer".
130. **Durable solutions**: return, local integration or resettlement for displaced people. *Ads:* UNHCR, IOM, NRC.

### 3.8 HR and employment terms (20)

131. **Duty station**: place of assignment. *Ads:* "Duty station: Juba".
132. **Hardship classification (A to E)**: UN rating of living conditions. H is HQ, E is hardest. No hardship allowance at A. Reviewed 1 January and 1 July. Source: https://www.unjobnet.org/career-resources/hardship-danger-pay-and-mobility `[snippet]`.
133. **Non-family duty station**: dependants cannot live there. Source: https://www.unjobnet.org/advice/un-non-family-duty-stations `[snippet]`.
134. **R&R (Rest and Recuperation)**: mandatory breaks, usually every 8 or 12 weeks in hard stations. Source: https://icsc.un.org/Home/RestRecuperation `[snippet]`.
135. **Danger pay**: an allowance for very dangerous locations. Source: https://policy.un.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/OHR_PG_2026_Danger_pay_Jan_Mar_2026_1.pdf `[snippet]`.
136. **National vs international staff**: hired locally on local terms, versus recruited internationally with expat package. *Context:* the trend favours national staff.
137. **UN grades: G (General Service), NO-A to NO-D (National Officers), P-1 to P-5, D-1/D-2 (international professional and director)**. *Ads:* "NOB", "P3". *Note:* NOB is roughly equivalent to an early P-level role but nationally paid.
138. **Contract types: FT (fixed-term), TA (temporary appointment), Continuing**: UN staff contracts. *Ads:* "FTA, 1 year renewable".
139. **Non-staff contracts: Consultant / Individual Contractor (IC), SSA, IICA, PSA, UNOPS LICA, UNV**: SSA is Special Service Agreement, IICA is International Individual Contractor Agreement (UNOPS), PSA is Personnel Service Agreement. UNV is UN Volunteer. *Ads:* "Consultancy, 6 months, home-based".
140. **Roster / surge / standby partners**: pre-vetted pools for rapid deployment (for example NORCAP, RedR, DRC Standby, CANADEM, IFRC surge). *CV:* "member of NORCAP roster, 3 deployments".
141. **JPO (Junior Professional Officer)**: UN entry roles funded by donor governments for their nationals. *Ads:* nationality-restricted.
142. **YPP / Young Professionals Programme**: UN entry exam route. *Ads:* nationality-restricted.
143. **Competency-based interview (CBI) / written test / assessment centre**: standard UN and INGO selection methods. See `03-hiring-processes-and-tests-in-the-sector.md`.
144. **UN Values and Behaviours Framework / CHCF**: competency frameworks that interviews test. CHCF covers six core competency areas, for example applying humanitarian principles, and motivating and influencing others. Source: https://www.chsalliance.org/what-we-do/chcf `[snippet]`.
145. **Home-based / remote / regional hub**: location model. *Context:* moves from Geneva and New York to lower-cost hubs (Nairobi, Bonn, Amman, Bangkok) are increasing (see section 5).
146. **Mobility / rotation**: expected moves between duty stations (UNHCR, WFP, ICRC). *Ads:* "willingness to be mobile".
147. **Inspira / Workday / UN Careers / ReliefWeb / Devex / Impactpool / UNjobs**: main job platforms. See section 6.
148. **P11 / PHP (Personal History Profile)**: old and new UN application formats. *Advice:* UN applications are long-form and keyword-heavy.
149. **Reference checks and misconduct screening (ClearCheck)**: UN database of staff dismissed for sexual misconduct. *Ads:* in UN ads.
150. **Nationality / gender / geographic representation**: UN preference for un- and under-represented countries and gender parity. *Ads:* "female candidates and nationals of non-represented member states encouraged".

## 5. Emerging context 2025 to 2026 and what it means for job seekers

### 5.1 Funding collapse and (weak) recovery signals

- **Collapse:**
  - ODA fell 23.1% in 2025, and humanitarian ODA fell about 35.8% to USD 15.5 billion. Source: OECD, via https://www.one.org/press/latest-oecd-dac-oda-data/ `[snippet]`
  - UN appeal funding fell 35% between 2024 and 2025 to USD 16.6 billion. Source: ALNAP `[opened]`
  - Donor Tracker estimates a 33% cut in international humanitarian aid across 2025 to 2026. Source: https://donortracker.org/publications/humanitarian-action-in-an-era-of-shrinking-oda `[snippet]`
- **Further pressure:**
  - The OECD projects a further 5.8% decline in 2026 (OECD `[snippet]`).
  - The proposed US FY2027 budget cuts about USD 12 billion more. Source: https://www.wola.org/analysis/key-things-to-know-about-the-proposed-foreign-aid-budget-for-fy2027/ `[snippet]`
- **Recovery and stabilisation signals:**
  - The US USD 2 billion OCHA-managed pledge for 2026 (PBS `[snippet]`).
  - The EU kept a EUR 1.9 billion humanitarian budget (ECHO `[snippet]`).
  - Gulf donors increased their spending (ALNAP `[opened]`).
  - Gates is spending about USD 9 billion a year to 2045 (Devex `[snippet]`).
  - The Global Fund raised USD 11.3 to 11.9 billion (aidspan `[snippet]`; https://archive.theglobalfund.org/media/knvnf1ea/archive_bm54-02-resource-mobilization-eighth-replenishment_update_en.pdf `[snippet]`).
  - Eight DAC members increased ODA in 2025 (IISD `[snippet]`).
- **Watch:** GHO 2027 is expected in early December 2026 (countercurrents `[snippet]`, `UNCERTAIN`).

### 5.3 Workforce shifts

- **Fewer jobs, more competition.** ReliefWeb vacancies fell about 65% between 2024 and 2025 (ALNAP 1.3 `[opened]`). 26% of surveyed workers considered leaving the sector (Devex survey, https://pages.devex.com/rs/685-KBL-765/images/USAID%20Funding%20Cuts%20Survey%20-%20Devex.pdf?version=0 `[snippet]`).
- **Local organisations cut deepest.** 55% of local and national NGOs reported staff cuts, compared with 45% of INGOs and 37% of UN respondents. Source: https://developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/200036/tracking-the-humanitarian-layoff-surge `[snippet]`
- **Shift to consultancies and short contracts.** `UNCERTAIN`: there is no hard aggregate data. Hiring freezes and an 18% UN vacancy rate (Al Jazeera `[snippet]`) suggest more short consultancies (SSA, IC, IICA) and roster work.
- **Shift to national staff and cheaper hubs.** IFRC planned to cut global and regional staff more than country staff. 9 of 18 multilaterals in a MOPAN survey moved functions out of high-cost hubs. UNICEF went from 7 regional offices to 5, and WFP merged its Johannesburg bureau into Nairobi (ALNAP 1.3 `[opened]`).
- **Mergers and consolidation.**
  - The General Assembly approved merging the UN System Staff College and UNITAR into a new institute from 1 January 2027. Source: https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168276 `[snippet]`
  - The UNDP/UNOPS and UNFPA/UN Women merger proposals met resistance and were not endorsed as of September 2026. Source: https://theglobalobservatory.org/2026/09/un-structural-reform-backward/ `[snippet]` `UNCERTAIN` on the current status.
  - A leaked idea to combine OCHA, UNHCR and IOM was only a proposal. Source: https://healthpolicy-watch.news/exclusive-full-text-of-un80-task-force-pitch-for-streamlined-un-including-who-and-unaids-merger/ `[snippet]`
  - NGO mergers and consortium bids are also rising `UNCERTAIN`.
- **Leadership transition.** The Secretary-General's term ends 31 December 2026, so expect new UN priorities in 2027. Source: UN80 search results `[snippet]` `UNCERTAIN`.

### 5.4 Geographic shifts

- **Funding is concentrating on a few mega-crises.** Sudan, Gaza/Palestine, DRC, Ukraine, Syria, Afghanistan, Yemen, Haiti, Myanmar and the Sahel dominate the appeals. Sudan's regional refugee plan had the biggest per-person shortfall (25% funded). Source: ALNAP funding gaps `[opened]`
- **Gulf money is concentrated.** 46% of Gulf humanitarian spending went to Yemen and 16% to Palestine in 2025 (ALNAP `[opened]`).
- **US priorities are narrowing.** The first US 2026 pooled funding was reported for Bangladesh, DRC, Haiti, Syria and Ukraine, with 17 priority countries (PBS/WTTW `[snippet]`).
- **Regional hubs are moving south and east.** Nairobi, Amman, Dakar, Bangkok and Panama are growing relative to Geneva and New York (ALNAP 1.3 `[opened]` for the relocation trend). The named cities are `UNCERTAIN`.
- **Middle-income countries and domestic finance.** IDA leans more on market borrowing and concessional loans (CGD `[snippet]`). Expect more government-led programming and less NGO-led programming.

### 5.5 What it means for job seekers (summary)

1. Expect longer searches and more consultancy work. Build a portfolio career and get on rosters.
2. National staff and local NGO experience is an asset, not a lower tier. The sector is moving towards it.
3. Diversify donor exposure. A CV with only USAID experience needs translating to EU, UN, Gulf and foundation frameworks.
4. Lead with efficiency, prioritisation, partnership and compliance stories.
5. Climate, cash, anticipatory action, data/AI, MEAL and non-traditional fundraising are the best growth bets.

## 7. Rules for the AI

### 7.1 Reading and scoring job ads

1. **Identify the funding source.** Look for the donor named in the ad, the project title, or "funded by". If the main donor is a sharply cut source (for example ex-USAID/State, FCDO in a country losing UK aid, a bilateral donor that is shrinking), flag "funding risk: short or uncertain contract" and lower the stability score, not the fit score.
2. **Classify the actor type** (UN fund/programme, specialised agency, Red Cross, INGO, local NGO, donor, MDB, contractor, foundation, CSR, social enterprise). Adjust expectations: UN means a long-form application, competency interview and written test, and grade matters. Contractors mean BD and donor-rules depth. Local NGOs mean hands-on breadth.
3. **Decode the grade and contract.** NOA to NOD and G grades are national only. P grades and IICA are international. SSA, IC and consultant posts have no benefits. JPO and YPP are nationality-restricted. Check whether the candidate is eligible (nationality, right to work in the duty station) before scoring high.
4. **Read the duty station.** Hardship E or a non-family station means R&R cycles and family impact. Ask about candidate constraints, but never assume them.
5. **Map required "must-haves"** to glossary terms: donor names, standards (CHS, Sphere), sectors (WASH, CVA, IPC), tools (KoBo, Power BI), languages. Score evidence, not keyword presence.
6. **Value national staff and local NGO experience** as at least equal to international experience for context knowledge, partnership and language. Never describe it as "junior" because of the contract type.
7. **Reward growth-area skills** (localisation, CVA, anticipatory action, climate, MEAL, data/AI, compliance, non-traditional fundraising, private sector partnerships) with a small bonus. Flag roles in shrinking areas as higher risk.
8. **Check the timing and status of UN reforms.** If the ad is from an entity under a merger or sunset proposal (for example UNAIDS, UNOPS, UN Women, UNFPA), note the uncertainty. Do not discourage the candidate, as such roles may still be good bridges.

### 7.2 Writing CVs, cover letters and interview answers

9. **Translate corporate terms into sector language.**

| Corporate term | Sector term |
|---|---|
| Customers / users | People we serve, affected people, communities, participants (avoid "beneficiaries" with some NGOs, but keep it for UN or donor reporting) |
| Clients (funders) | Donors, partners |
| Vendors | Suppliers, implementing partners (if they deliver programmes) |
| KPIs | Indicators, results, targets |
| Product launch / rollout | Programme start-up, scale-up |
| Stakeholder management | Coordination, partnership, donor relations, community engagement |
| Customer feedback | Feedback and complaints mechanism, AAP |
| ROI | Value for money, cost-efficiency, cost per person reached |
| Risk management | Compliance, safeguarding, security risk management, Do No Harm |
| Sales / business development | Resource mobilisation, fundraising, proposal development |
| CSR or ESG work | Partnerships, shared value, safeguards (E&S) |
| Training staff | Capacity sharing or strengthening, mentoring |

10. **Lead with quantified results:** people reached (sex, age and disability disaggregated if available), budget managed (USD/EUR), number of sub-awards or partners, indicators met, audit results, evaluation findings.
11. **Name donors and frameworks explicitly** (for example "ECHO-funded MPCA", "FCDO VfM", "CHS-aligned feedback mechanism"). Recruiters search for these terms.
12. **Show principled and humble behaviour.** Give examples of listening to communities, deferring to local partners, applying Do No Harm, and handling safeguarding concerns. Avoid saviour language ("helping the poor", "giving back to Africa").
13. **Turn crisis experience into strength.** Managing a grant termination, staff transition, programme prioritisation or office consolidation in 2025 to 2026 shows adaptability and cost discipline. Frame it factually, with no blame.
14. **Use localisation language accurately:** "partnership", "co-design", "capacity sharing", "locally led". Do not claim localisation credit for simply subcontracting.
15. **Match the competency framework.** For UN roles, structure answers using the UN Values and Behaviours or the agency's own competencies. For NGOs, use CHCF and STAR-style examples. Always include one example each on safeguarding, teamwork in hard conditions, and dealing with ambiguity.
16. **Adapt to the donor culture.** EU means logframe, co-financing and visibility. UK means VfM and SEAH. US/State means compliance and cost-effectiveness. Gulf donors means government relations and Arabic. Foundations mean innovation, evidence and learning.
17. **Mention AI use responsibly.** List the real tools used, and stress data protection, human oversight and "do no digital harm". Never overclaim.
18. **Language and location.** State working languages with levels (CEFR or UN levels). State eligibility for national roles where relevant. State willingness or constraints on hardship duty stations honestly.

### 7.3 Accuracy and integrity rules

19. **Do not invent donor names, grant values, or results.** If the candidate lacks a number, ask for it or use a qualitative description.
20. **Treat all figures in this primer as dated.** Re-check against the section 6 sources when advising on a specific donor or agency. Anything marked `UNCERTAIN` must not be stated as fact to clients.
21. **Respect privacy and security.** Do not write sensitive locations, beneficiary data or security incidents into public CVs. Summarise them at the right level.
22. **Plain English first.** Use acronyms only after spelling them out once, unless the ad itself uses them.
