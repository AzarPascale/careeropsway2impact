## 1. The four-paragraph structure

Every letter has four paragraphs, in this order. The same four jobs apply in English, French and Arabic.

| # | Paragraph | Job | Length (proposal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Why them** | Show you know this employer and why you want to work there, not anywhere. | 2-4 sentences |
| 2 | **Why this role** | Name the exact role and say why it is the right next step, using the positioning statement. | 2-4 sentences |
| 3 | **Proof** | Show, with real examples, that you meet what the advert asks for. | The longest part. 2-3 examples, or one per criterion |
| 4 | **The ask** | Ask for the interview. Give the practical facts the employer needs. | 2-3 sentences |

Week 2 of the programme gives a short version of the same structure: name the role, answer each criterion with one real example, close with why this employer. This framework puts "why them" first and repeats it in one line in the close. Both are fine. Use the one that reads better for the advert.

### Paragraph 1: Why them

- Name one specific thing: a programme, a place, a strategy, a value, a recent piece of work. "Your work on education for out-of-school children in the Bekaa" beats "your important mission".
- Use the employer's own words where they have them. Many employers publish a values or competency framework: the UN Values and Behaviours, the IRC Way values, the CHCF for humanitarian roles (research/03 §3.3, §6.2, §6.4).
- Connect it to the client in one sentence. Why does this matter to them?
- Do not open with "I am writing to apply for...". The subject line already says that.

### Paragraph 2: Why this role

- Use the exact job title and reference number from the advert.
- Say what the client brings and what they want to grow, in one or two sentences from the Week 1 positioning statement.
- Segment angle:
  - **A New grads:** studies, internships, volunteering and the skill they want to build. Entry routes are national roles, internships and short contracts (research/01 §8.2).
  - **B Moving up:** the next-grade story. Revenue, partnerships, strategy and leading through change (research/01 §8.2).
  - **C Shifters:** the translation. The corporate background is an asset. Name how the mission-credibility gap is closed: volunteering, a board seat or a short course (research/01 §8.2).
  - **D Repositioning:** the bridge story. Transferable language, and one neutral line on the end of the last contract (program Week 2, D swap).

### Paragraph 3: Proof

- One real example per point. Each example has a situation, what the client did, and a result with a number where possible. This is the same Situation, Action, Result shape the UN uses in interviews, or STAR in the UK (research/03 §3.3).
- Every fact must be in the master CV. If it is not, it does not go in the letter.
- Show efficiency and donor value where you can: cost saved, time saved, funds raised, people reached (research/01 §8.1 points 1 and 3).
- Pick the 2-3 strongest criteria for a short letter. Answer every criterion when the advert asks for it (see section 3).

### Paragraph 4: The ask

- Ask for a conversation or interview in plain words.
- Give the practical facts: availability or notice period, location, right to work, languages.
- For embassy jobs, state the right to work locally. Embassies do not sponsor permits (research/03 §5.5).
- Thank the reader in one line. Sign off.

## 2. Length and format rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| The advert wins | If the advert sets a word limit, a format or a form, follow it exactly. |
| Standard letter (proposal) | One page. About 250-400 words. Four paragraphs. |
| Criteria letter or statement | As long as needed to answer every criterion, within any limit given. One Australian Embassy Beirut advert asked for 500-700 words against the criteria. That advert is from 2017, so check the current format (research/03 §5.3). |
| Portal fields | UN and NGO portals may ask pre-screening or motivation questions instead of a letter (research/03 §2, §3.1). Answer each box with the same logic: one point, one example. |
| Sentences | Short. One idea per sentence. Active voice. |
| Bullets (proposal) | Only in paragraph 3, only when the employer accepts a free-form letter, and never more than 5. |
| Header | Client's name, city, email, phone. Date. Employer name and address if known. Subject line with the job title and reference. |
| Personal details | Leave off religion, marital status, age and photo unless the advert asks. US embassies say to leave race, religion, marital status and similar details off the CV (research/03 §5.1). Apply the same care to the letter. |
| Anonymised processes | FCDO asks for anonymised CVs (research/03 §5.2). If an employer asks for anonymous documents, check whether that covers the letter too, and follow the instruction. |
| File | PDF unless the portal asks for another format. File name: `Surname_FirstName_CoverLetter_JobRef.pdf` (proposal). |
| Language | The language of the advert. GIZ, for example, asks candidates to apply in the language of the advert (research/03 §7). |

## 3. How to answer the job advert's criteria

Screening checks the application against the minimum requirements first (research/03 §2). Make the reader's job easy.

1. **Copy the criteria.** Put every requirement from the advert into a table: essential, desirable, and any competencies or values it names.
2. **Find the evidence.** For each line, find the best fact in the master CV or the story bank. Mark it strong, partial or missing.
3. **Use the advert's words.** If the advert says "partner capacity building", say "partner capacity building", not "supporting local organisations".
4. **One example per criterion.** Situation, action, result. A number if there is one.
5. **Keep the advert's order** when the employer scores criteria. EU Delegation selection criteria earn points (research/03 §4.3). Number your answers to match theirs.
6. **Handle gaps honestly.** Never claim a skill the client does not have. Show the nearest real evidence, and name the course or certificate in progress from the Week 0 gap analysis.
7. **Check coverage.** US embassies say the CV or application form must cover every requirement, or it will not be considered (research/03 §5.1). Before sending, tick every line of the criteria table.

### When the advert asks for a separate statement

Some embassies and donors want a statement against the selection criteria, not a letter (research/03 §5.3, §9 module F). Then:
- Use one heading per criterion, in the advert's wording.
- Under each heading, one or two examples in Situation, Action, Result form.
- Keep a short covering note that does paragraphs 1, 2 and 4 in a few lines.

## 5. Writing in French

Use French when the advert is in French. Adapt the letter. Do not translate the English version line by line.

- **Register:** formal. Always "vous".
- **Subject line:** `Objet : Candidature au poste de [intitulé exact du poste] (réf. [référence])`. French puts a space before the colon.
- **Opening:** `Madame, Monsieur,` when you do not know the name. `Madame [Nom],` or `Monsieur [Nom],` when you do. Avoid "Cher" or "Chère" in a first letter.
- **Structure:** French letters are often taught as "vous, moi, nous": the employer, then the candidate, then what they will do together. That maps onto our four paragraphs: paragraph 1 is "vous", paragraphs 2 and 3 are "moi", paragraph 4 is "nous".
- **Closing:** a full formula is still expected in a formal letter, for example `Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées.` In a short email body, `Cordialement,` is enough.
- **The ask:** for example `Je serais heureux de vous présenter ma motivation lors d'un entretien.` Use `heureuse` for a woman. Check every gender agreement for the client.
- **Watch out for:** anglicisms, English word order, and overused words such as "passionné" and "dynamique". Use the job title exactly as the advert writes it.
- **Length:** French letters run a little longer than English ones for the same content. Keep to one page.

## 6. Writing in Arabic

Use Arabic when the advert is in Arabic. Adapt the letter. Do not translate the English version line by line.

- **Register:** Modern Standard Arabic (الفصحى), not dialect. Formal and respectful, but keep sentences short and the proof concrete.
- **Subject line:** `الموضوع: طلب التقدم لوظيفة [المسمى الوظيفي] (الرقم المرجعي [المرجع])`
- **Opening:** `حضرة السيدة / السيد [الاسم] المحترمة / المحترم،` when the name is known. `السادة [اسم الجهة] المحترمون،` when it is not. Then `تحية طيبة وبعد،`
- **Paragraph 1 opener, for example:** `يسعدني أن أتقدم بطلب لشغل وظيفة [المسمى الوظيفي] المعلن عنها على [موقع الإعلان].`
- **The ask, for example:** `وأتطلع إلى فرصة لقائكم لمناقشة كيف يمكنني المساهمة في عملكم.`
- **Closing:** `وتفضلوا بقبول فائق الاحترام والتقدير،` then the client's name.
- **Address the reader** in the respectful plural (حضرتكم، لقائكم). Check every verb and adjective for the client's gender.
- **Sector terms:** use the Arabic terms the employer uses. The CHCF is available in Arabic (research/03 §6.4), so it is a good source for competency words in humanitarian letters. Keep acronyms such as UNDP or M&E in Latin letters if the advert does.
- **Numbers and dates:** pick one digit style (1 2 3 or ١ ٢ ٣) and use it everywhere. Use Gregorian dates.
- **Formatting:** set the paragraph direction to right-to-left in Google Docs or Word. Check that Latin words and numbers inside Arabic lines sit in the right place before exporting to PDF.
- **Watch out for:** long ceremonial openings that push the proof down the page. One greeting line is enough. The four paragraphs still apply.

## 7. Final checklist (use on every letter)

- [ ] Employer name, job title and reference number are correct and spelled as in the advert.
- [ ] Paragraph 1 names something specific about this employer.
- [ ] Every criterion the advert scores is answered, in the advert's order and words.
- [ ] Every fact, number and date is in the master CV and is true.
- [ ] No generic AI lines. The client can say every sentence out loud and mean it (research/03 §3.7).
- [ ] Length fits the advert's limit, or one page.
- [ ] Language and register match the advert. Gender agreement checked in French and Arabic.
- [ ] No personal details the employer did not ask for.
- [ ] The ask is clear, with availability and right to work where relevant.
- [ ] The client sends the application themselves.
