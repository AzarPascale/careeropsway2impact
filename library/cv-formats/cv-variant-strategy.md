# CV variant strategy: one master, four variants

**For:** you, Kristin. "You" is Kristin. "The client" is the person you coach.
**Status:** draft, 4 October 2026.
**Agrees with:** `business/offer-and-pricing.md` (variant caps) and `program/6-week-programme.md` (Week 2 and its segment swaps). Hiring claims cite research/03. Segment claims cite research/01.

---

## 1. Why a master CV

Screening checks every application against the minimum requirements of the advert first (research/03 §2). A client who sends one generic CV to every advert fails that check often. A client who rewrites from scratch each time burns hours and makes mistakes with dates and numbers.

The fix is one master CV per client. It holds every fact. Each variant is cut from it. Tailoring a variant to one advert then takes about an hour (the Week 2 homework allows 1 hour for the first variant), and the facts stay consistent across every CV, letter, form and LinkedIn profile.

---

## 2. Build the master CV (Week 2, first half of the main block)

### What goes in
- **Every role**, paid and unpaid, with exact start and end months, the employer, the location, and the contract type (staff, consultant, intern, volunteer).
- **Every result**, written as a full bullet with all its numbers. Start from the Week 1 homework: 10 achievements, each with a number or a result.
- **Every qualification**: degrees, courses, certificates, with dates. Include the certificate plan from the Week 0 gap analysis, marked "in progress" only if it has started.
- **Languages**, with an honest level. If the client has a certificate, record its name and date. Some employers verify language certificates (research/03 §4.3).
- **Tools and skills**, including AI and data tools the client really uses. AI literacy is now asked for (research/01 §8.1 point 5).
- **Referees**: names and how to reach them, kept in the master only. Many employers check references, and safeguarding checks are standard in INGOs (research/03 §1 point 6, §2).
- **Raw material**: thesis title, volunteer roles, side projects, awards, publications, trainings given.

### How to write each master bullet
One bullet = one result. Use this order:

> **Action verb** + **what** + **for whom or with whom** + **scale** (numbers) + **result** (what changed) + **how** (method, if useful)

Keep the long version. Variants shorten it. You cannot lengthen a bullet later without going back to the client for facts.

### Rules for the master
- It is never sent. It has no page limit.
- One master per language. A second-language master starts as a translation and is then checked by a fluent reader.
- Every number must come from the client and be checkable.
- Mark anything uncertain with `<<CHECK>>` until the client confirms it.

## 3. Derive the four variants

The facts stay the same. Four things change: **order, words, emphasis, and what is left out.**

| | UN | NGO | Donor / embassy | Corporate CSR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Where it goes** | Inspira form (Secretariat), agency portals, P11 (`formats/un-inspira-and-p11.md`) | Two-page CV (`formats/ngo-two-page.md`) | Europass for EU Delegations; anonymised or criteria-led CVs for other embassies and donors (`formats/europass.md`) | ATS-friendly corporate CV (`formats/corporate-csr.md`) |
| **What the reader scores** | The competencies and requirements in the job opening. The UN Values and Behaviours Framework (research/03 §3.1, §3.3) | Minimum requirements, languages, then experience (NRC, research/03 §6.1). Frameworks such as the IRC Success Model and the CHCF (research/03 §6.2, §6.4) | Each selection criterion, often for points (EU Delegation, research/03 §4.3). Every requirement covered (US embassies, research/03 §5.1) | Normal corporate hiring through an applicant tracking system, then a recruiter and a hiring manager (research/03 §8, secondary sources) |
| **Words to use** | The job opening's own competency and requirement wording | Programme words: partners, communities, households, donors, reporting | The criterion's own wording, one criterion at a time | Business words: stakeholders, partners, budget, efficiency, impact |
| **Lead with** | Results tied to the listed competencies | Field delivery, technical area, languages | The criteria that earn the most points | Measurable business-relevant results |
| **Leave out or shorten** | Detail the form does not ask for | Old or unrelated roles | Personal details the employer bans (research/03 §5.1, §5.2) | Sector jargon and acronyms |

### The worked example: one bullet, four ways

**This is an invented example, not a real client.** Every number below comes from the master bullet. No variant adds a fact.

**Master bullet (long form):**
> Coordinated delivery of hygiene kits to 1,200 households in 3 municipalities with 2 local partner NGOs, January to June 2025. Re-planned distribution routes and split tasks between the partners, which cut average delivery time from 10 days to 6. Wrote monthly progress reports to the donor and tracked spending against a USD 150,000 budget line.

**1. UN variant**
> Planned and delivered hygiene-kit distribution to 1,200 households in 3 municipalities with 2 national partners; re-planned routes and partner roles, cutting delivery time from 10 to 6 days.

*Why:* it shows planning and delivery of results with partners. That maps to behaviours in the UN Values and Behaviours Framework such as "Analyse and Plan", "Deliver Results with Positive Impact" and "Connect and Collaborate" (research/03 §3.3). Do not paste the framework labels into the bullet. Use the wording of the job opening's own "Competencies" section (research/03 §3.1), and save the full story for the competency interview (research/03 §3.3).

**2. NGO variant**
> Managed hygiene-kit distribution to 1,200 households in 3 municipalities through 2 local partner NGOs; re-planned routes and partner roles, cutting delivery time from 10 to 6 days; wrote monthly donor progress reports.

*Why:* NGO readers look for field delivery, partner work and donor reporting in programme language. Keep the technical area visible.

**3. Donor / embassy variant** (for an advert whose selection criteria include contract and financial management and drafting, as in the EU Delegation example in research/03 §4.3)
> Tracked spending against a USD 150,000 budget line and drafted monthly progress reports to the donor for a 6-month, partner-implemented project reaching 1,200 households.

*Why:* the same fact, turned to face the criteria that earn points. The budget and the reporting lead. The logistics detail moves to another bullet or drops out.

**4. Corporate CSR variant**
> Ran a 6-month community programme with 2 delivery partners, reaching 1,200 households in 3 locations; redesigned logistics to cut delivery time by 40% (10 to 6 days); owned budget tracking (USD 150K) and monthly stakeholder reporting.

*Why:* business readers want efficiency and ownership. The 40% is the same fact as "10 to 6 days", shown as a rate. "Donor" becomes "stakeholder". No sector acronyms.

**The test for every variant bullet:** can you point to the master bullet it came from? If not, delete it.

## 4. Rules per segment

The segments and their Week 2 swaps come from `program/6-week-programme.md`. Week 2 is swapped for A, C and D. B follows the core week.

### A. New grads (lead segment): little paid experience

**The problem:** a thin CV, and a client who thinks they have nothing to show.

**Rules:**
1. **Lead with education**, then internships, volunteering, thesis and projects (Week 2, A swap).
2. **Treat unpaid work as experience.** Give internships, volunteer roles and student projects the same full bullet format as a job: action, scale, result.
3. **Use the thesis and coursework as evidence** of research, analysis and writing. Written tests are common in this sector (research/03 §1 point 3).
4. **Name skills employers ask juniors for:** AI, data and fundraising (research/01 §8.2). Only list what the client can show.
5. **Keep it short.** Proposal: one page for the NGO and CSR variants, until the client has more than one year of relevant work.
6. **Point the variants at open routes:** national posts, internships, short-term contracts, Gulf and local organisations (research/01 §8.2), and UN Volunteers (research/03 §3.1). Build a UNV-ready version if the client targets UN Volunteers. UNV first asks for a profile in the UNV Global Talent Pool (research/03 §3.1).
7. **Check YPP eligibility before writing for it:** 32 or younger in the exam year, at least a first degree in the field, fluent English or French, and a nationality taking part that year (research/03 §3.5).
8. **Never pad.** A short, true CV beats a long, inflated one. Inflating experience is explicitly banned by UNDP (research/03 §3.7).

**Single-service CV clients** (USD 60) get a master CV and no variants. Make the master good enough to send to their main target with small edits, and teach them the variant method in this file.

### B. Moving up: insiders wanting a bigger role

**Rules:**
1. Lead with scope: budgets, teams, partners, geographies.
2. Position on revenue, partnerships and strategy, and on leading through change and cost control (research/01 §8.2).
3. Cut early-career detail to one line per role.
4. For lateral moves into banks, foundations or Gulf donors (research/01 §8.2), use the donor or CSR variant wording.

### C. Shifters: translating corporate language

**The problem:** strong results written in words sector readers skip.

**Rules:**
1. **The corporate background is an asset.** Commercial, finance and partnership skills are in demand (research/01 §8.2). Do not hide it. Translate it.
2. **Build two variants early:** a translation CV for NGO, UN or donor roles, and an ATS-friendly CV for CSR roles, which follow normal corporate hiring (research/03 §8, secondary sources; Week 2, C swap).
3. **Show the mission credibility** the client has: volunteering, a board seat, a short certificate (research/01 §8.2). Put it high on the page, not under "Interests".
4. **Use the translation table below** for the NGO, UN and donor variants. Keep the corporate words in the CSR variant.

| Corporate wording | Sector wording (NGO, UN, donor variants) |
|---|---|
| Clients, customers | Communities, people served, participants (use the word the advert uses) |
| Business development | Partnerships, resource mobilisation, fundraising |
| P&L ownership, budget owner | Budget management, financial reporting, grant budget follow-up |
| KPIs, targets | Indicators, results, targets in the results framework |
| Vendors, suppliers | Suppliers, service providers, implementing partners (only if they delivered programme work) |
| Go-to-market, launch | Roll-out, programme start-up |
| Account management | Partner management, donor relations |
| Revenue growth | Funds raised, new funding secured (only if true in the new sense) |
| Stakeholder management | Stays the same. The sector uses it. |

Check each swap against the real advert. Use the advert's words where they differ from this table.

### D. Repositioning after a layoff: including the gap

**The problem:** a strong record, a field that shrank, and a gap or a sudden end date the client is ashamed of.

**Rules:**
1. **Reframe, do not hide.** Rewrite titles and results in transferable language for the new target field (research/01 §8.2; Week 2, D swap).
2. **Bridge out of the hardest-hit fields where needed.** Project leads and M&E roles were hit hardest, and health, humanitarian and governance funding fell most (research/01 §8.1 point 4). Lead with the skills the new field wants.
3. **Show efficiency:** cost savings, results, doing more with less (research/01 §8.1 point 3).
4. **Put bridge work on the CV.** Consulting, short-term and portfolio work are a normal part of the market now (research/01 §8.1 point 6). A consulting role is a role. Give it a full entry.
5. **Present the gap honestly, in one neutral line** (Week 2, D swap).

**How to write the gap line:**

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| State the reason in plain words, once: "Programme closed after donor funding cuts." | Blame the employer, the donor or a person |
| Say what the client did in the gap: courses, consulting, volunteering, caring duties if the client wants to say so | Invent a job or a consultancy to fill the dates |
| Keep exact months if the form asks for them | Switch to years only, or stretch an end date, to hide the gap |
| Say "role ended" or "position cut" only if true | Say "laid off for performance" or anything not true |

**Template (fill the curly braces; leave out any part that does not apply):**
> {Month Year} to {Month Year or "present"}: Career transition. {Role} ended when {programme or organisation} closed after donor funding cuts. Since then: {course or certificate}, {consulting or volunteer work}, {other}.

**Where it goes:** in date order, as its own entry, in the experience section. In a form such as Inspira or the P11, use the field the form gives for it and keep the same wording.

**Why honest matters:** references are checked before offers (research/03 §2, §6.2). A stretched date that a reference contradicts costs more than the gap.

**Emotional note:** writing this line can bring back the loss. Many people who lost sector jobs grieve the mission, not only the job (research/01 §4, §8.1 point 9). Write it together in the session, not as homework.

**Segment D clients** (laid off in the cuts) often need a repositioned master CV and one variant for a second direction, such as CSR or consulting. Use one of their tier's variants for it.

## 5. Languages

- Documents follow the language of the advert (`program/6-week-programme.md`, "Languages"; GIZ asks for this, research/03 §7).
- A CV in a second language counts as one CV variant (`business/offer-and-pricing.md` §1).
- Translate from the master, not from a variant. Then cut the variant again in the new language.
- A fluent reader checks every translated CV before it is sent. If you are not fully fluent in that language for professional writing, say so and find a reader.

---

## 6. Quality checklist before any variant goes out

- [ ] Every bullet traces back to a master bullet.
- [ ] Every minimum requirement in the advert is visibly met, in the advert's own words, or the gap is known and accepted.
- [ ] Dates, titles and numbers match the master, the LinkedIn profile and the cover letter.
- [ ] Personal details follow the employer's rules (for example none on an FCDO CV, research/03 §5.2).
- [ ] The format matches the matching file in `formats/`.
- [ ] Spelling checked in the document's language.
- [ ] The client has read it and confirms every line is true.
