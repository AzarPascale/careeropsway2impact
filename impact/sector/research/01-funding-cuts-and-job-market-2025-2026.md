# Funding cuts and the social-impact job market, 2025–2026

**Prepared:** 4 October 2026
**For:** Kristin's career-coaching practice (research file 01)
**Type:** Desk research from public web sources
**Scope:** Global picture, with a focus on MENA and Lebanon
**Fact-checked:** 4 October 2026 — six key claims checked against their sources. See "Fact-check (4 Oct 2026)" at the end.

> **How to read this file.** Every number has its source link next to it.
> "(unverified)" means I saw the claim only in a search-result summary or a second-hand report, and I could not open or confirm the original.
> Lines marked **Interpretation** are my reading of the evidence. They are not sourced facts.

## 1. Summary in ten points

1. **Aid fell by about 23% in 2025.** This is the largest drop ever recorded. Total official aid (ODA) was USD 174.3 billion ([OECD, Apr 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2026/04/a-historic-decline-in-foreign-aid-preliminary-2025-oda-data.html)).
2. **It keeps falling.** The OECD projects a further 6.9% drop in 2026, to USD 152 billion. That is the lowest level since 2014. By 2028, aid is projected to be 9.9% below 2025 ([OECD, Jun 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html)).
3. **The US caused most of it, but not all.** US aid fell 56.9%, about three quarters of the total drop. Yet 26 of 34 OECD donors cut too ([The New Humanitarian, Apr 2026](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/maps-and-graphics/2026/04/10/what-latest-oecd-numbers-tell-us-about-future-aid)).
4. **Some fields were hit much harder than others.** From 2024 to 2026, health aid is projected to fall 29–46%, humanitarian aid 40.3%, and "government and civil society" 39.8% (mostly because aid to Ukraine is falling). Core funding to the UN falls about 31% ([OECD, Jun 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html)).
5. **Job losses are in the hundreds of thousands.** A self-reported tracker counts 258,161 confirmed global jobs lost, plus 23,053 American jobs, from 183 organisations ([USAID Stop-Work, as of 13 Jan 2026](https://www.usaidstopwork.com/)).
6. **The UN shrank, unevenly.** IOM lost 29.5% of its staff and UNHCR 22% in 2025. But 20 of 39 UN entities grew, including UNDP (+5.6%) ([ReformWorks, Aug 2026, using UN CEB data](https://www.reformworks.org/post/who-got-hit-hardest-by-the-2025-un-staff-cuts)).
7. **Hiring shifted toward short contracts.** UN job postings on the Devex job board fell 43.3% (24 Jan–16 Jun 2025, against the same period of 2024). Long-term consulting fell 66.8%. Short-term contracts fell least, at 31.7% ([Devex report, Jun 2025](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/2025-06/UN-Job-Cuts.pdf)).
8. **Many laid-off people are still stuck.** In an informal survey of 725 people, 68% were still unemployed, underemployed, freelancing against their will, or forced into early retirement one year later ([Devex via USAID Alumni, Mar 2026](https://usaidalumni.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Where-are-former-aid-staff-now.-Devex-31-March-2026.pdf)). Respondents were mostly senior, US-linked former USAID staff and partners, so this is not a picture of MENA job seekers ([survey author, May 2026](https://mypivot.substack.com/p/seeing-the-unseen-725-voices-from)).
9. **There are still bright spots.** Fundraising and partnership roles, development banks, Gulf donors, climate skills inside companies, impact investing, and reconstruction work. Details in section 6.
10. **MENA is doubly hit.** Lebanon faced the aid cuts and then a new war from late February 2026. Its 2026 Flash Appeal was only 48% funded in August ([UN News, 18 Aug 2026](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168163)). Jordan lost about 35,000 jobs tied to US projects ([The National](https://thenational.shorthandstories.com/us-usaid-jordan-funding/)).

## 2. What happened to the money

### 2.1 Total aid

- ODA from OECD donors was **USD 174.3 billion in 2025, down 23.1%**. It is the largest annual drop on record. Aid is back to where it was in 2015 ([OECD data explainer, 9 Apr 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2026/04/a-historic-decline-in-foreign-aid-preliminary-2025-oda-data.html)).
- The OECD's later brief puts the 2025 fall in net ODA from DAC countries at **23.3%**, after an 8.5% fall in 2024 ([OECD, 19 Jun 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html)). The two figures use slightly different groups of donors.
- **2026 projection: −6.9%, to USD 152 billion**, the lowest since 2014. This replaces the OECD's April estimate of −5.8%. Sixteen donors are cutting. Seventeen are increasing, but only by USD 0.7 billion in total ([OECD, Jun 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html)).
- The last time aid fell three years in a row was 1992–95, after the Cold War ([OECD, Jun 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html)).

### 2.2 The United States

- US ODA fell **56.9%** in 2025. This was the largest cut by any donor in any year on record. The US alone drove about three quarters of the global decline ([OECD, Apr 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2026/04/a-historic-decline-in-foreign-aid-preliminary-2025-oda-data.html)). The OECD's June brief, on its own donor group, puts the US share at 70% ([OECD, Jun 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html)).
- The freeze began with an executive order on 20 January 2025 ([The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/features/why-usaid-freeze-shaking-lebanons-development-sector)). USAID stopped operating in July 2025 and about 83% of its programmes were cancelled ([The Conversation, Jan 2026](https://theconversation.com/international-aid-groups-are-dealing-with-the-pain-of-slashed-usaid-funding-by-cutting-staff-localizing-and-coordinating-better-273184)).
- Some US aid still flows, through the State Department. Congress appropriated USD 5.5 billion for humanitarian aid in its 2026 package. The State Department is setting up 12 regional hubs for emergency response ([AP via Naharnet, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.naharnet.com/stories/en/319173-1-million-displaced-in-lebanon-but-aid-groups-are-crippled-by-foreign-aid-cuts)).

### 2.3 Europe and other traditional donors

- The five largest donors (Germany, US, UK, Japan, France) made **95.7% of the 2025 cut**. Germany became the largest donor for the first time, at USD 29.1 billion ([OECD, Apr 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2026/04/a-historic-decline-in-foreign-aid-preliminary-2025-oda-data.html)).
- **UK:** aid to fall from 0.5% to 0.3% of national income by 2027 ([DevelopmentAid, Sep 2025](https://www.developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/200036/tracking-the-humanitarian-layoff-surge)).
- **France:** about €2.3 billion cut in the 2025 budget ([DevelopmentAid](https://www.developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/200036/tracking-the-humanitarian-layoff-surge)). A further €803 million cut for 2026 was reported (unverified).
- **Germany:** €1.7 billion trimmed ([DevelopmentAid](https://www.developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/200036/tracking-the-humanitarian-layoff-surge)). Its 2026 budget keeps a more than 50% drop in humanitarian aid. The Foreign Office is restructuring with "sizable staff cuts", and its crisis department is being dissolved ([Devex Newswire, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-newswire-aid-cuts-keep-coming-but-one-gulf-state-offers-good-news-111517)).
- **Sweden:** stopping aid to Zimbabwe, Tanzania, Mozambique, Liberia and Bolivia in 2026, to fund Ukraine. It is closing its embassies in Bolivia, Liberia and Zimbabwe ([Devex Newswire, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-newswire-aid-cuts-keep-coming-but-one-gulf-state-offers-good-news-111517)).
- **Belgium:** plans to cut 25% from development cooperation ([DevelopmentAid](https://www.developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/200036/tracking-the-humanitarian-layoff-surge)).
- A few donors increased aid in 2025: Denmark (+3%), Spain (+10.7%), Sweden (+9.6%), Norway (+1.7%), Luxembourg (+8.9%) ([The New Humanitarian, Apr 2026](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/maps-and-graphics/2026/04/10/what-latest-oecd-numbers-tell-us-about-future-aid)).
- **Interpretation.** Donors now say openly that aid follows national interest: Ukraine, migration, defence, trade and raw materials. Germany's development ministry is refocusing on "economic cooperation and private partnerships" ([Devex Newswire](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-newswire-aid-cuts-keep-coming-but-one-gulf-state-offers-good-news-111517)).

### 2.4 Which sectors lost the most (OECD projections, 2024 → 2026)

| Sector | Projected change | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Health (all) | −29% to −46% | [OECD, Jun 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html) |
| Population and reproductive health (incl. HIV) | up to −54.1% | same |
| Malaria / tuberculosis control | up to −59.6% / −57.2% | same |
| Humanitarian aid | −40.3% (USD 9.3 bn) | same |
| Government and civil society | −39.8% (USD 8.9 bn); mostly falling aid to Ukraine, which got 37.8% of this sector's aid in 2024 | same |
| Water and sanitation | −22.3% | same |
| Core funding to UN organisations | about −31% | same |

- Humanitarian aid had already fallen **35.8% in 2025**, to USD 15.5 billion ([OECD, Apr 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2026/04/a-historic-decline-in-foreign-aid-preliminary-2025-oda-data.html)).
- Core contributions to the UN fell **27% in 2025**, the largest drop on record. In contrast, **contributions to the World Bank and regional development banks increased** ([OECD, Apr 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2026/04/a-historic-decline-in-foreign-aid-preliminary-2025-oda-data.html)).

### 2.5 New donors are growing

- Twelve non-OECD donors reported USD 13.3 billion in ODA for 2025 ([OECD, Apr 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2026/04/a-historic-decline-in-foreign-aid-preliminary-2025-oda-data.html)).
- The UAE reports a 55.5% rise in its aid, and Qatar a 23.4% rise ([The New Humanitarian, Apr 2026](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/maps-and-graphics/2026/04/10/what-latest-oecd-numbers-tell-us-about-future-aid)).
- The Qatar Fund for Development gave USD 642.4 million in 2024 and is expanding, notably in Syria ([Devex Newswire, Dec 2025](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-newswire-aid-cuts-keep-coming-but-one-gulf-state-offers-good-news-111517)).
- Saudi Arabia says it was the second-largest humanitarian donor in 2025, per the UN Financial Tracking Service ([KSrelief](https://www.ksrelief.org/en/Pages/NewsDetails/528ddf39-2ec6-4c32-bde0-d74175874653)) (unverified).

### 2.6 The 2026 Middle East war made it worse

- A crisis in the Middle East has been running since late February 2026. It pushes up food, fuel and fertiliser prices. Most donors told the OECD it will **not** raise their aid. None reported new measures for its economic fallout ([OECD, Jun 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html)).
- UNHCR estimated 1 million people displaced in Lebanon after strikes on Iran began on 28 February 2026 ([AP via Naharnet, Mar 2026](https://www.naharnet.com/stories/en/319173-1-million-displaced-in-lebanon-but-aid-groups-are-crippled-by-foreign-aid-cuts)).

## 3. The UN liquidity crisis and UN80

### 3.1 Budgets

- The UN's 2026 regular budget is **USD 3.45 billion**. It cuts financial resources by 15% and staffing by nearly 19% ([UN press release, 30 Dec 2025](https://press.un.org/en/2025/gaab4507.doc.htm)).
- Unpaid dues (arrears) were USD 1.586 billion as of 1 December 2025 ([same](https://press.un.org/en/2025/gaab4507.doc.htm)).
- The 2026 Global Humanitarian Overview was lowered to USD 33 billion ([Devex Newswire, Dec 2025](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-newswire-aid-cuts-keep-coming-but-one-gulf-state-offers-good-news-111517)). The "hyper-prioritised" ask is USD 23 billion for 87 million people. It was only one third funded in March 2026 ([AP via Naharnet](https://www.naharnet.com/stories/en/319173-1-million-displaced-in-lebanon-but-aid-groups-are-crippled-by-foreign-aid-cuts)).

### 3.2 Who cut staff in 2025 (UN system data)

Source: [ReformWorks, Aug 2026](https://www.reformworks.org/post/who-got-hit-hardest-by-the-2025-un-staff-cuts), analysing UN Chief Executives Board (CEB) personnel data.

| Entity | Change 2024 → 2025 |
|---|---|
| IOM | −4,471 staff (−29.5%) |
| UNHCR | −2,932 staff (−22%) |
| UNAIDS | −118 staff (−19%) |
| UNICEF | −1,614 staff (−10.5%) |
| WFP | about −7% |
| UN Secretariat | −6% (more cuts expected in 2026) |
| WHO | −5% (restructuring targets −23% overall) |
| UNDP | **+411 staff (+5.6%)** |

- **20 of 39 entities increased staff** in 2025 ([ReformWorks](https://www.reformworks.org/post/who-got-hit-hardest-by-the-2025-un-staff-cuts)).
- **General Service (support) staff were hit hardest**: about 7,500 fewer, more than all professional and field categories combined ([ReformWorks](https://www.reformworks.org/post/who-got-hit-hardest-by-the-2025-un-staff-cuts)).
- **Junior professionals (P-1 to P-3) were cut more than national officers** in IOM, UNHCR and UNICEF. In the Secretariat, P-3 saw the biggest proportional cut ([ReformWorks](https://www.reformworks.org/post/who-got-hit-hardest-by-the-2025-un-staff-cuts)).
- UNHCR itself says it cut its workforce by a third in 2025 and closed offices in 140 locations. Its funding fell from USD 5.178 billion (2024) to USD 3.932 billion (2025). By July 2026 it had only 32% of the USD 8.5 billion it needs ([UN News, 29 Sep 2026](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168486)). The difference from the CEB figure above is likely because "workforce" includes non-staff contracts (interpretation).

### 3.3 Hiring at the UN

From Devex job-board data only (not all UN hiring), 24 Jan – 16 Jun 2025 versus the same period of 2024 ([Devex report, Jun 2025](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/2025-06/UN-Job-Cuts.pdf); summary at [Devex, Jul 2025](https://www.devex.com/news/un-job-postings-are-down-43-where-are-the-remaining-opportunities-110426)):

- All UN postings: **−43.3%**.
- UNHCR −83.4%, WHO −81.1%, UNICEF −52.1%, UNDP −49.2%, UNOPS −37%, FAO −28%, WFP −4.2%.
- **Up:** IOM +70.8% (mostly short-term), UNEP +126%, UN-Habitat +212.7%.
- By contract: long-term consulting −66.8%, full-time staff −44.5%, short-term −31.7%.
- By location: **Jordan −63%** (−62.5%; the steepest among the ten locations with the most UN postings in 2024), Ukraine −61%, Ethiopia −51%, Switzerland −38%, US −36%.
- UNICEF is merging regional offices into four "Centres of Excellence", one of them in **Amman** ([Devex report](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/2025-06/UN-Job-Cuts.pdf)).
- The US zeroed its funding for Junior Professional Officer (JPO) posts, meaning few or no new US JPOs ([CSIS, Aug 2025](https://www.csis.org/analysis/why-us-junior-professionals-un-still-matter)) (unverified). Other donor countries still fund JPOs.

## 4. Job losses: the main estimates

| Estimate | Scope | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **258,161** confirmed global jobs lost; **23,053** US jobs | Layoffs and furloughs reported by 183 organisations, linked to the USAID freeze, as of 13 Jan 2026. Advocacy site; self-reported. | [USAID Stop-Work](https://www.usaidstopwork.com/) |
| "Over 250,000" global development jobs | Cites the same tracker | [Devex, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.devex.com/news/what-has-happened-to-the-people-who-lost-their-jobs-in-the-aid-cuts-112116) |
| 55% of NGOs laid off staff; 67% report disrupted programmes | ICVA survey, early 2025 | [DevelopmentAid, Sep 2025](https://www.developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/200036/tracking-the-humanitarian-layoff-surge) |
| About 35,000 jobs in Jordan | Workers on US-funded projects; preliminary NGO-sector estimate | [The National](https://thenational.shorthandstories.com/us-usaid-jordan-funding/) |
| About 2,000 employees affected in Lebanon | Staff on USAID-funded projects, early 2025, "according to local news sources" | [Now Lebanon, 19 Feb 2025](https://nowlebanon.com/serious-damage-to-thousands-of-individuals-in-lebanon-amid-suspension-of-usaid-funds/) (unverified count) |

**Examples of named organisations** ([DevelopmentAid tracker, Sep 2025](https://www.developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/200036/tracking-the-humanitarian-layoff-surge)):

- **Contractors:** DAI laid off about 500 (about 70% of staff). Chemonics furloughed more than 750. RTI cut 35% of its global workforce.
- **NGOs and health groups:** Management Sciences for Health cut about 70%. John Snow Inc. laid off about 1,100. HIAS more than 500. Alight cut 50% of 2,300 staff. NDI released 66% of its Washington staff.
- **UN and alliances:** FAO terminated about 600. UNAIDS shrinking from 600 to 280–300. Gavi cut 155 jobs (about 24%).
- **Universities:** Johns Hopkins cut more than 2,000 jobs tied to global health.

**Governments and foundations also shrank:**

- **US State Department:** 1,350 laid off in July 2025, within about 3,000 total personnel reductions ([GovExec, 11 Jul 2025](https://www.govexec.com/workforce/2025/07/state-department-lays-1350-employees/406665/)). It later started "quiet hiring" to run aid functions left by USAID ([Devex Newswire, Dec 2025](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-newswire-big-ngos-talk-how-they-re-thinking-about-hiring-in-2026-111600/amp)).
- **UK FCDO:** planning 15–25% headcount cuts by 2029, starting with 500 voluntary exits ([Civil Service World, Jul 2025](https://www.civilserviceworld.com/professions/article/fcdo-planning-up-to-25-headcount-cuts)).
- **Germany:** all federal ministries, including the development ministry, are to cut about 8% of staff (unverified, via [Devex search summary](https://www.devex.com/news/germany-the-world-s-top-aid-donor-proposes-development-cuts-112423)). GIZ stops sending new "development workers" from 2026 (unverified).
- **Gates Foundation:** record USD 9 billion budget in 2026, but will cut up to 500 positions by 2030, about a fifth of its headcount. It caps running costs at USD 1.25 billion a year and closes in 2045 ([GeekWire, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/gates-foundation-will-cut-up-to-500-positions-by-2030-to-help-reach-ambitious-goals/)).

### 4.1 What happened to the people

From an informal LinkedIn survey of **725** development professionals, run in late February 2026 by former USAID-er Becky Band Jain ([Devex, 31 Mar 2026, via USAID Alumni](https://usaidalumni.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Where-are-former-aid-staff-now.-Devex-31-March-2026.pdf)). Who answered: two thirds had 15+ years of experience; almost 40% were USAID direct hires, 33% implementing partners, 15% contractors; early-career people were under-represented ([survey author's write-up, 20 May 2026](https://mypivot.substack.com/p/seeing-the-unseen-725-voices-from)):

- **68%** were still unemployed, underemployed, freelancing involuntarily, or in forced early retirement, one year on.
- **38%** had been searching for more than 12 months.
- Of those who did find work, **83% earn less than before**, most with pay cuts of 20% or more ([survey author's write-up](https://mypivot.substack.com/p/seeing-the-unseen-725-voices-from)).
- Older respondents reported **ageism** and being "overqualified". One younger respondent wrote: "Us 30-somethings are in the worst spot imaginable… the middle got hit hardest."
- The top theme was **"mourning the mission"**, not only the job. People also reported drained savings and damage to identity and mental health.

Some went into consulting, law, small businesses, politics and advocacy ([same](https://usaidalumni.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Where-are-former-aid-staff-now.-Devex-31-March-2026.pdf)).

Caution: this was a self-selected survey. The author calls the numbers "directional, not representative". It likely over-represents people who are still struggling, and senior US-linked staff ([same](https://mypivot.substack.com/p/seeing-the-unseen-725-voices-from)).

## 5. Sub-sector by sub-sector: what shrank, what held, what grew

| Sub-sector | Direction | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Humanitarian** | Down: Sharp fall | Humanitarian ODA −35.8% in 2025; −40.3% projected 2024–26 ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html)). | **Global health** | Down: Deepest cut | −29% to −46% (2024–26); HIV, malaria and TB worst ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html)). |
| **Governance, democracy, civil society** | Down: Sharp fall | −39.8% projected, largely because aid to Ukraine is falling ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html)). NDI cut global offices from 60 to 14 ([DevelopmentAid](https://www.developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/200036/tracking-the-humanitarian-layoff-surge)). |
| **UN system** | Down: Mixed | Core funding −27% in 2025. IOM and UNHCR cut deeply, but 20 of 39 entities grew ([ReformWorks](https://www.reformworks.org/post/who-got-hit-hardest-by-the-2025-un-staff-cuts)). |
| **Development banks (World Bank, regional banks)** | Up: Holding or growing | Donor contributions to the World Bank and regional banks increased in 2025 ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2026/04/a-historic-decline-in-foreign-aid-preliminary-2025-oda-data.html)). Banks drove a 33% jump in private climate finance mobilised in 2024 ([OECD, May 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2026/05/developed-countries-exceed-usd-100-billion-climate-finance-goal-for-third-consecutive-year.html)). |
| **Climate (public finance)** | Flat: Flat to falling | Climate finance provided and mobilised was USD 136.7 bn in 2024 and still rising ([OECD, May 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2026/05/developed-countries-exceed-usd-100-billion-climate-finance-goal-for-third-consecutive-year.html)). But public climate finance fell for the first time ever (USD 109.9 bn → 106.2 bn), and the GEF's latest round was the smallest in two decades ([NRDC, Jul 2026](https://www.nrdc.org/midway-through-tumultuous-2026-where-do-we-stand-international-public-climate-finance)). These figures lag: the 2025 aid cuts are not yet in them. |
| **Climate and green skills (private sector)** | Up: Growing | Green hiring rose 7.7% in 2024–25, against 4.3% growth in green skills supply. People with green skills are hired at a 46.6% higher rate. 53% of green-skilled hires went into roles without a "green" title ([LinkedIn data via ESG Today, Dec 2025](https://www.esgtoday.com/hiring-rate-for-workers-with-green-skills-dramatically-outpaces-broader-market-as-demand-outpaces-supply-linkedin-survey/)). UNEP job postings +126% in 2025 ([Devex report](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/2025-06/UN-Job-Cuts.pdf)). |
| **Corporate sustainability / CSR / ESG** | Flat: Stable, quieter | 2026 Sustainability Census (2,340 people, 76 countries): team sizes unchanged for 53%, grew for 29%, shrank for 17%. Nearly two thirds had stable or higher budgets. Only 3% of respondents were in Africa and the Middle East ([Eco-Business, Jul 2026](https://www.eco-business.com/news/global-sustainability-hiring-holds-steady-despite-tougher-business-climate-survey-finds/)). The EU cut its sustainability reporting rule (CSRD) to companies with over 1,000 staff and over €450 m turnover, removing about 80% of companies (unverified; see [summary](https://financialregulations.eu/blog/eu-omnibus-csrd-simplification-2026)). |
| **Philanthropy and foundations** | Flat: Busy but cannot fill the gap | Gates spends a record USD 9 bn in 2026 but is cutting staff ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/gates-foundation-will-cut-up-to-500-positions-by-2030-to-help-reach-ambitious-goals/)). ## 6. MENA and Lebanon specifics

### 6.1 Lebanon

**Before 2026**
- In 2024 the US was Lebanon's top donor of grants reported by UN agencies and NGOs: USD 293 million, or **22%** of USD 1.3 billion ([The New Arab, Feb 2025](https://www.newarab.com/features/why-usaid-freeze-shaking-lebanons-development-sector), citing UN aid tracking).
- The freeze hit local NGOs directly. Examples: a recycling NGO fully funded by USAID; therapists left unpaid; an NGO in Tripoli losing eight jobs; another unable to hire 15 planned staff ([The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/features/why-usaid-freeze-shaking-lebanons-development-sector)).
- About 2,000 employees on USAID projects were affected. The USAID-funded Kitaabi education project suspended about 100 staff ([Now Lebanon, Feb 2025](https://nowlebanon.com/serious-damage-to-thousands-of-individuals-in-lebanon-amid-suspension-of-usaid-funds/)) (count unverified).
- An economist noted that small Lebanese NGOs may find EU or Canadian money. Large INGOs that depend on US funds, and their sub-grantees, are more exposed ([The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/features/why-usaid-freeze-shaking-lebanons-development-sector)).

**2026: war on top of cuts**
- Heavy fighting resumed in March 2026 and displaced over a million people. About 860,000 had returned by August; about 360,000 were still displaced ([UN News, 18 Aug 2026](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168163)).
- The **Flash Appeal of USD 639.9 million was 48% funded**. UNHCR's Lebanon operation was **24% funded** against USD 472.3 million ([UN News, 18 Aug 2026](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168163)).
- The IRC said its Lebanon programme "was itself faced with funding cuts just as a major scale-up is needed" ([AP via Naharnet, Mar 2026](https://www.naharnet.com/stories/en/319173-1-million-displaced-in-lebanon-but-aid-groups-are-crippled-by-foreign-aid-cuts)).
- **UNRWA** announced a 20% cut in its activities from 1 February 2026, affecting services for Palestinian refugees in Lebanon ([L'Orient Today, Jan 2026](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1491957/palestinians-in-lebanon-angered-by-unrwas-20-cut-in-operations.html)). UNRWA also separated about 571 local staff outside Gaza in January 2026 (unverified, via [Sweden Herald](https://swedenherald.com/article/un-agency-unrwa-forced-to-lay-off-nearly-600-employees-amid-financial-crisis)).

**Reconstruction money exists**
- The World Bank's **Lebanon Emergency Assistance Project (LEAP)** is a USD 1 billion framework. The Bank gave the first USD 250 million (approved June 2025). It is run by the Council for Development and Reconstruction (CDR). Damage from the 2023–24 conflict was put at USD 7.2 billion, with USD 11 billion in recovery needs ([World Bank FAQ](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/factsheet/2026/02/17/lebanon-emergency-assistance-project-frequently-asked-questions)). These figures predate the 2026 fighting.

### 6.2 Jordan

- About **35,000** Jordanian and American workers lost jobs on USAID-funded work across public bodies, private firms, contractors and partners. One implementing partner let go of 62 staff in one morning ([The National](https://thenational.shorthandstories.com/us-usaid-jordan-funding/)).
- UN job postings in Jordan fell **63%**, the steepest of any major UN duty station ([Devex report, Jun 2025](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/2025-06/UN-Job-Cuts.pdf)).
- Yet Amman remains a regional hub. UNICEF chose it as one of four global "Centres of Excellence" ([same](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/2025-06/UN-Job-Cuts.pdf)).
- Devex described Jordan's earlier success at localisation as now "coming back to bite its aid sector" ([Devex headline](https://www.devex.com/news/jordan-s-success-at-localization-comes-back-to-bite-its-aid-sector-110113)) (article body not read).

### 6.3 Syria

- The World Bank estimates reconstruction at about **USD 216 billion**, almost ten times Syria's 2024 economy ([The National, 26 Sep 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/09/26/syria-faces-multi-decade-recovery-but-current-aid-is-insufficient-warns-charity-chief/)). Aid reaching Syria is called "woefully insufficient" ([same](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/09/26/syria-faces-multi-decade-recovery-but-current-aid-is-insufficient-warns-charity-chief/)).
- Qatar has made Syria a priority since December 2024 ([Devex Newswire](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-newswire-aid-cuts-keep-coming-but-one-gulf-state-offers-good-news-111517)).
- Reported but not confirmed: US and EU sanctions largely lifted in 2025; Saudi (USD 6.4 bn) and Qatari (USD 4 bn) pledges; a UN 2026 appeal of USD 2.8 bn only 20% funded; humanitarian staffing cuts of at least 40% (all unverified, from search summaries).

### 6.4 The Gulf

- **Qatar:** QFFD gave USD 642.4 million in 2024 (0.32% of national income) and is growing ([Devex Newswire](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-newswire-aid-cuts-keep-coming-but-one-gulf-state-offers-good-news-111517)).
- **UAE:** aid up 55.5% in 2025, mostly humanitarian aid for Palestine ([TNH](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/maps-and-graphics/2026/04/10/what-latest-oecd-numbers-tell-us-about-future-aid)). Abu Dhabi hosted a polio pledging summit that raised over USD 1.8 billion ([Devex Newswire](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-newswire-aid-cuts-keep-coming-but-one-gulf-state-offers-good-news-111517)).
- **Saudi Arabia:** non-profit sector above SAR 100 billion. 23% of Saudi citizens volunteered and 47% donated in 2024 ([SPA, Mar 2025](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2275712)). KSrelief's 2026 plan covers 113 projects in 44 countries with 11 Saudi non-profits (unverified, via [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2630234/saudi-arabia)).
- **Interpretation.** Gulf donors fund on strategic and regional interest. They tend to fund through their own national agencies and partners, and they value Arabic, regional knowledge and government relations.

## 7. Where hiring still happens

1. **Fundraising, donor engagement and partnerships.** "Fundraising teams are hiring." Fundraising and marketing were "among the least affected" roles (Save the Children US) ([Devex, Dec 2025](https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573)).
2. **"Growth strategists."** ActionAid wants "people who can literally open new markets, secure diversified income, and build multiyear revenue pipelines". Blended finance, corporate partnerships and commercial skills are "highly sought after" ([same](https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573)).
3. **Development banks and blended finance.** Contributions to the World Bank and regional banks rose in 2025 ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2026/04/a-historic-decline-in-foreign-aid-preliminary-2025-oda-data.html)). Banks are driving private climate finance ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2026/05/developed-countries-exceed-usd-100-billion-climate-finance-goal-for-third-consecutive-year.html)).
4. **National and local roles.** Localisation and new hubs in Africa, Asia and Latin America create roles for national professionals with "local insight" plus analytical and digital skills ([Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573)). UN agencies cut national officers less than junior international staff ([ReformWorks](https://www.reformworks.org/post/who-got-hit-hardest-by-the-2025-un-staff-cuts)).
5. **Short-term and consulting work.** Short-term contracts were the least-cut contract type at the UN ([Devex report](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/2025-06/UN-Job-Cuts.pdf)). Recruiters note "the gig economy continues to grow" ([Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573)).
6. **Growing UN entities.** UNDP added staff in 2025 ([ReformWorks](https://www.reformworks.org/post/who-got-hit-hardest-by-the-2025-un-staff-cuts)). UNEP and UN-Habitat increased postings ([Devex report](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/2025-06/UN-Job-Cuts.pdf)).
7. **Climate and green skills in companies** (section 5).
8. **Gulf donors, Gulf foundations and the Gulf non-profit sector** (section 6.4).
9. **Reconstruction:** Lebanon (World Bank LEAP via CDR), Syria, Ukraine. These need engineers, procurement, safeguards, finance and project managers (interpretation).
10. **AI and digital.** "At a minimum, everyone needs to be AI literate" (PCDN Global) ([Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573)).
11. **Entry level.** "Some INGOs are reinstating their internship programs" ([Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573)).
12. **Compliance, counter-fraud, procurement, budget.** Recent featured full-time roles included counter-fraud at CARE, procurement at UNOPS and budget advice at DT Global ([Devex Career Hub, Feb 2026](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-career-hub-3-new-rules-for-landing-globaldev-roles-111910)). Treat this as an example, not a trend.

## 8. What this means for how a candidate must position themselves

### 8.1 General rules (with evidence)

1. **Lead with money-raising and donor value.** "Put yourself out there as not just an international USAID award manager… but as a donor liaison. It's still all about the donor — it's just [a question of] who are the donors today" (Save the Children US, via [Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-career-hub-3-new-rules-for-landing-globaldev-roles-111910)).
2. **Know the new funders.** Development banks, the EU, Gulf donors, foundations and private capital. Speak their language: return, efficiency, national interest, partnership (interpretation based on sections 2 and 5).
3. **Prove efficiency.** Organisations now operate "with more complexity and fewer resources" (ActionAid, via [Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573)). Show cost savings, results and "doing more with less".
4. **Avoid pitching only for the hardest-hit roles.** Project leads and M&E roles were "hit hardest"; new openings there "remain limited" ([Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573)). Health, humanitarian and governance funding fell most ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oda-projections-for-2026-and-the-near-term_d7c74fa2-en/full-report.html)). People from these fields need a bridge story.
5. **Be AI literate and data-comfortable.** "You don't need to be a data scientist, but you do need to be comfortable operating in this digital environment" (ActionAid, via [Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573)).
6. **Accept flexible contracts.** Plan for short-term, consulting and portfolio work ([Devex report](https://www.sipa.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/2025-06/UN-Job-Cuts.pdf)).
7. **Network "like a detective".** Look beyond big brands to "collaborators, grantees, and partners". Be "relentlessly creative" ([Devex Career Hub, Feb 2026](https://www.devex.com/news/devex-career-hub-3-new-rules-for-landing-globaldev-roles-111910)). Offer free mentoring or coaching to build connections (Save the Children US, via [Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573)).
8. **Use local and regional insight as a strength.** For MENA candidates: Arabic, regional networks, and knowledge of Gulf donors and reconstruction actors (interpretation, based on section 6).
9. **Plan for a long search and protect mental health.** 38% of surveyed laid-off workers searched for more than a year. Grief for the mission is common ([Devex via USAID Alumni](https://usaidalumni.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Where-are-former-aid-staff-now.-Devex-31-March-2026.pdf)).

### 8.2 By Kristin's four target groups

Build AI, data and fundraising skills early. Use fellowships and free courses to upskill ([Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/how-to-navigate-the-global-development-job-market-in-2026-111573)). |

Large competing talent pool (section 4). | Reframe titles and achievements into transferable language. Pivot away from the deepest-cut fields where needed. Combine emotional support with a clear weekly plan. Consider consulting as a bridge, not a failure. |

### 8.3 What this means for Kristin's practice (interpretation)

- **The need is real and large.** Hundreds of thousands lost jobs, and many are still searching after a year (section 4).
- **The emotional side is not optional.** "Mourning the mission", ageism and drained savings show up in the data. Kristin's emotional-support element directly matches this.
- **Ability to pay varies.** Laid-off clients may have less cash. Corporate shifters and people moving up may pay more. Pricing tiers may be needed.
- **The "job tracker" deliverable has extra value now.** Hiring is spread across many platforms and contract types (UN, INGOs, banks, Gulf donors, consulting).
- **MENA angle.** Kristin can stand out by knowing the regional market: Gulf donors, the reconstruction actors in Lebanon and Syria, and hubs like Amman.

## 9. Open questions to raise in Kristin's interview

1. Which of her four groups does she already have contacts in, and which pays best?
2. How much does she know about Gulf donors, development banks and reconstruction programmes? These are where money is moving.
3. Does she plan to help clients with fundraising and partnership roles, the most resilient job family?
4. How will she handle clients who are grieving or financially stressed? What is her referral path to therapists?
5. Will her job tracker cover short-term and consulting roles, not only staff posts?

## Limitations

- **Desk research only.** No interviews with recruiters, NGOs or job seekers in Lebanon or the region.
- **Paywalls.** Several Devex articles are paywalled. I used only their free parts or headlines. The September 2026 Devex analysis of "more than 20,000 jobs" could not be read.
- **"(unverified)" items** were seen only in search summaries or second-hand reports. Check them before quoting them to clients.
- **Job-loss totals are rough.** The 258,161 figure comes from an advocacy tracker built on self-reports. Organisations count "jobs", "staff" and "workforce" differently. For example, UNHCR's own "cut by a third" differs from the UN CEB staff figure of −22%.
- **Survey bias.** The 725-person survey by Becky Band Jain was informal and self-selected. It likely over-represents people still struggling, and senior US-linked staff. (An earlier draft called it the "Aid on the Hill" survey. That was wrong: she ran it herself; she only did volunteer work for Aid on the Hill.)
- **Data lag.** OECD 2025 figures are preliminary; final data comes in December 2026. Climate finance data stops at 2024 and does not yet show the 2025 aid cuts.
- **Thin MENA data.** I found no systematic count of NGO job losses in Lebanon. Most Lebanon figures are funding figures, not jobs. The Lebanon damage estimate (USD 11 billion) predates the 2026 fighting.
- **CSR, embassies and impact investing** have little job-market data. These rows rely on budget and headcount signals, not hiring counts.
- **Fast-moving situation.** The Middle East war, UN80 reforms and donor budgets were all still changing as of 4 October 2026.
- **Interpretation sections** (marked) are my reading and should be tested in Kristin's interview and with her own network.
