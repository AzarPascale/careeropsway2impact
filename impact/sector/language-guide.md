# Sector language guide

The words a candidate uses tell a sector recruiter in seconds whether they belong. The agent runs this check on every draft. Sources: `impact/sector/research/05` §3, §1.7, §2; Bond language guide; Manchester Anthropology of Aid; MSF anti-racist guide; CHS 2024.

## 1. Corporate to sector translation (use only if the work is genuinely the same)

| Corporate | Sector |
|---|---|
| Customers, users, clients | People reached, communities, participants, rights-holders, people affected by crisis |
| KPIs, dashboards, performance tracking | Indicators, results framework, logframe, MEAL (monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning) |
| Strategy rationale, business case | Theory of change |
| Revenue, sales, funding | Grants, awards, donor funding, resource mobilisation, grant management |
| Investors, shareholders | Donors, funders, back donors |
| Suppliers, vendors, contractors | Implementing partners, local partners, sub-grantees |
| Risk and compliance | Donor compliance, safeguarding, PSEA, Do No Harm, conflict sensitivity, anti-fraud |
| Quality standards | Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Sphere, donor rules |
| Market expansion, local presence | Localisation, locally led action, partnership with local and national actors |
| Site visits | Field visits, field monitoring, duty station, country office |
| Product launch | Programme start-up, inception phase |
| Project close | Close-out, handover, exit strategy, sustainability |
| Customer feedback | Feedback and complaints mechanism, accountability to affected people (AAP) |
| Team lead, manager | Line manager, supervisor (and say how many staff, national and international) |
| Head office | Headquarters (HQ), regional office, country office |
| Report to the board | Donor reporting, narrative and financial reports |

## 2. Words to avoid and what to use instead

| Avoid | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Help the poor", "save", "rescue", "the needy", "voiceless" | Name the group and their role: "women-led cooperatives", "refugee-led organisations", "displaced families in X" | Pity and rescue framing signals colonial assumptions |
| "Give back", "make a difference" as the main motive | A specific link between the client's skill and the organisation's programme | Cliché; shows no fit |
| "Empower" (as something done to people) | Rights, autonomy, community organising, "supported X to lead Y" | Implies power is given by outsiders |
| "Capacity building" (one-way) | Partnership, accompaniment, sharing learning, "co-designed with" | One-way framing |
| "Developing countries", "third world" | Name the country or region | Outdated, vague |
| "Beneficiaries" in motivation or values text | People, communities, participants, rights-holders | **Keep it** in donor, logframe and reporting contexts, or when the ad uses it |
| "Mission" for an assignment (MSF context) | Assignment | MSF guidance |
| "Passionate about development" | One real experience that shows it | Passion without evidence is the most common weakness |
| "Self-starter", "results-driven", "dynamic", "synergy", "leverage", "disrupt" | Plain verbs and one proof per claim | Corporate buzzwords read as outsider language |
| "Managed donor relations" | "Wrote 6 quarterly reports to ECHO and led the mid-term review" | Name the donor and the task |
| "Stakeholders" with no detail | Name them: ministry of X, 4 local partners, the WASH cluster | Vague |
| "Responsible for..." | Action verb + scale + result | Duties are not achievements |

## 3. Register depends on the reader

| Reader | Register |
|---|---|
| UN forms, donor-funded posts, MEAL roles | Technical: outputs, outcomes, indicators, logframe, beneficiaries, compliance |
| Motivation letters, values questions | Reflective and specific; no charity framing; show you learned from the people you worked with |
| Rights-based and advocacy organisations | Rights-holders, duty-bearers, accountability, participation |
| Localisation-focused organisations | Locally led, partnership, shift the power, sharing decision-making |
| CSR and corporate foundations | Business and impact together: ESG, social value, SROI, stakeholder engagement (here "stakeholders" is fine) |
| US-funded employers (2025-2026) | Check how the employer currently describes gender and inclusion work and mirror it. Never remove true experience. **(uncertain, policy in flux)** |

## 4. Tone

- Own your part ("I designed", "I led") after crediting the team.
- Humble, factual, specific. No superlatives.
- Show context knowledge (the country, the crisis, the organisation's programme there).
- Show you know the limits of outside help, especially in values questions.

## 5. Agent check (run on every draft)

1. Search the draft for every word in section 2 "Avoid". Replace or justify.
2. Count claims without a number or a named example. Add one or mark [ADD METRIC].
3. Check "beneficiaries" against the reader (section 3).
4. Remove em-dashes and en-dashes.
5. Read the first three lines as a recruiter: does it sound like a practitioner?
