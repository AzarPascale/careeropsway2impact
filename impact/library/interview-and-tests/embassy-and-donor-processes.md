## 1. What a local post is

Embassies, the EU Delegation and donor agencies hire local staff in the country where they work. These are not diplomatic posts. You apply as a local candidate, and the process looks like most of the sector: screening, a test, a competency interview, checks, then an offer or a reserve list (research/03 §2).

**Three things to check before you apply.**
1. **You need the right to work in that country already.** Embassies do not sponsor work permits (research/03 §5.5). If you live in Dubai and the post is in Beirut, ask yourself this first.
2. **Language tests are normal** (research/03 §5.5). Read the advert for the languages it names and the level it asks for.
3. **Security vetting is normal** (research/03 §5.5). Have your documents and referees ready early (section 8).

## 3. FCDO-type processes (UK)

The UK FCDO is both an embassy employer and a donor. In Lebanon, jobs at the British Embassy Beirut and other UK offices are posted on the FCDO local staff site (research/03 §5.2).

**The framework: Success Profiles.** It has five elements: Behaviours, Strengths, Ability, Experience and Technical (research/03 §5.2). One FCDO country post asked about two behaviours, "Communicating and Influencing" and "Seeing the Big Picture" (research/03 §5.2). Each advert names its own. Read the advert and answer the ones it names.

**Behaviour questions on the form.** Candidates are told to read the Success Profiles behaviours and answer with **STAR**: Situation, Task, Action, Result (research/03 §5.2). Write each answer as one real example. Section 6 shows how.

**Assessments you may meet** (research/03 §5.2): the Civil Service judgement test, personality tests, verbal and numerical reasoning tests, presentations, in-tray exercises, written analysis, role-play, oral briefings and assessment centres. Not every post uses all of these. The invitation tells you which.

**Strengths questions.** These ask what energises you, not what you did. The official advice is "Do not rehearse your answers" (research/03 §5.2). So we do not script them. Before the interview, spend 20 minutes writing down which tasks give you energy and which drain you, with one example each. Then answer from that, in your own words.

**Your CV.** Anonymise it: no name, address, email, age, date of birth, gender or nationality (research/03 §5.2).

**Afterwards.** Offers depend on security vetting, and you arrange your own police certificate (research/03 §5.2). If you are a strong runner-up, you may go on a 6-month reserve list (research/03 §5.2). Ask whether you were placed on it.

## 4. GIZ-type processes (Germany)

GIZ lists six steps: online application, confirmation, document review, an invitation to a selection process that may last two days, individual feedback, then the contract (research/03 §7).

**What we know.**
- Only a CV and a cover letter are needed at first. References are asked for at interview (research/03 §7).
- Apply in the language of the advert (research/03 §7).
- Country offices post national jobs on country pages, sometimes with their own GIZ application form (research/03 §7).
- The trainee programme uses a group selection event "with interviews and tasks to test your aptitude and skills" (research/03 §7).

**What we do not know.** The research did not record what happens on each day of a GIZ selection process. Do not trust a description you cannot trace to the invitation or to GIZ itself.

**How we prepare when you are invited.**
1. Read the invitation line by line with Kristin. List every task it names, with its time.
2. Practise the task types that assessment events in the sector commonly use: a short presentation, a written task under time, a role-play and a panel interview (research/03 §9 module E). Practise only the ones your invitation names.
3. Plan your energy for two days: sleep, food, travel, a quiet space between tasks.
4. Ask for the individual feedback GIZ lists as a step (research/03 §7), whatever the result. Write it down.

## 5. Language checks

| Employer | What the research found | Source |
|---|---|---|
| US embassies | Language posts can include speaking, reading and writing tests. | research/03 §5.1 |
| EU Delegation Lebanon | The example post scored English and Arabic among its selection criteria. Language certificates are checked before hiring. | research/03 §4.3 |
| Australian Embassy Beirut | Arabic was essential (2017 ad). | research/03 §5.3 |
| GIZ | Apply in the language of the advert. | research/03 §7 |
| NRC (an NGO, for comparison) | Language tests often happen during the interview. | research/03 §6.1 |

**How to prepare (coaching method, not an employer's rule).**
1. **Be honest about your level.** A level you cannot show in the interview will cost you more than a lower level stated plainly. Certificates are checked at the EU Delegation (research/03 §4.3).
2. **Prepare in each language the advert names:** your 30-second introduction, three of your strongest stories, and a list of 20 technical words for the role.
3. **Practise switching.** In your mock with Kristin, part of the interview switches language without warning (research/03 §9 module G).
4. **Practise reading and summarising.** Read a short public report in one language and summarise it out loud or in writing in the other.
5. **If you freeze,** slow down and use simple sentences. A clear simple answer beats a complex broken one.

Kristin coaches in English, French and Arabic.

## 6. Selection-criteria statements and behaviour answers

Many embassy and donor posts score your written application against each criterion. The EU Delegation lists selection criteria separately, and they earn points (research/03 §4.3). US embassies reject a CV that does not cover every requirement (research/03 §5.1). The FCDO asks behaviour questions on the form (research/03 §5.2).

**One structure for all of them.**
1. **Copy every criterion** from the advert into a list. Number them.
2. **Use the advert's words** as your heading or first sentence for each one.
3. **Give one real example** per criterion, in Situation, Task, Action, Result form.
4. **End each one with a result:** a number, a change or a decision.
5. **Check coverage:** every criterion has an answer, and every answer names the criterion.

**Word budget for a 500-700 word statement** (coaching guide, for 4-5 criteria):

| Part | Words |
|---|---|
| Opening: the role and why this employer, in two sentences | 40-60 |
| Each criterion: heading, one example, result | 100-140 each |
| Closing: one sentence on what you bring | 20-30 |

**Example skeleton for one criterion.**

> **Criterion 3: Drafting skills in English and Arabic.**
> In [year], at [organisation], I [situation in one line]. My task was [task]. I [action 1], [action 2] and [action 3]. As a result, [result with a number or change].

**Rules.** Your examples must be your own and true. AI may help you proofread and structure. It may not invent or inflate your experience (research/03 §3.7). Do not include race, religion, marital status or similar details on a US embassy CV (research/03 §5.1).

## 7. Drafting exercises and written tests

**What we know.**
- EU Delegation finalists sit a written test as well as an interview (research/03 §4.3). The Delegation does not discuss the selection procedure (research/03 §4.3), so the exact format is not public.
- Some US embassy posts include a job-related test (research/03 §5.1).
- UK Success Profiles assessments can include written analysis and in-tray exercises (research/03 §5.2).
- For an EU Delegation post, you also need to know what the EU does in Lebanon (research/03 §4.3).

**How we practise.** Kristin sets timed tasks built from public material or fictional scenarios. They train the skill, not a known test. Typical practice tasks:

| Practice task | Time (practice setting) | What it trains |
|---|---|---|
| A one-page briefing note on a public report, for a senior reader | 60 min | Picking what matters, writing for the reader |
| A summary of a public document in a second language | 45 min | Language and accuracy |
| A reply to a partner's email that raises a problem | 30 min | Tone, clarity, diplomacy |
| A short analysis: three options and a recommendation | 60-90 min | Structure and judgement |
| An in-tray: 5-6 short items to rank and act on | 45 min | Priorities under pressure |

**How your work is marked.** Kristin uses five points: logical structure, concision, clarity, fit to audience and purpose, and good use of the material provided. These are the EU essay "anchors", used here as a marking rubric (research/03 §4.1, §9 module B).

**The first five minutes of any timed task.**
1. Read the whole task once. Underline what is asked and who the reader is.
2. Write a plan: headings and one line under each.
3. Split your time: about 10% reading and planning, 75% writing, 15% checking (coaching guide).
4. Start writing.

**AI is banned in any test where it is not explicitly allowed** (research/03 §3.7). If the invitation does not say, assume it is not allowed.

| Two referees | Australian Embassy (2017 ad) | research/03 §5.3 |

| Police certificate, arranged by you | FCDO | research/03 §5.2 |
| Medical exam and security investigation after a tentative offer | US embassies | research/03 §5.1 |
| Medical, background, diploma and language-certificate checks | EU Delegation | research/03 §4.3 |
| Security vetting | FCDO, Australian Embassy | research/03 §5.2, §5.3 |

**Two rules.**
- **Do not resign from your current job until the formal offer arrives** (US embassy guidance, research/03 §5.1). It is a safe rule for every employer.
- **Tell your referees early** what role you applied for and what to expect.

## 9. Timing, feedback and what silence means

- US Mission Switzerland: shortlisting and interviews usually finish within 6 weeks of the closing date. There is **no feedback** after an unsuccessful interview. Eligible family members of US staff get hiring preference (research/03 §5.1).
- The EU Delegation does not discuss the selection procedure (research/03 §4.3).
- GIZ lists individual feedback as a step (research/03 §7).

Silence is normal and is not a verdict on you. Log every application in your tracker with its closing date, and move on after the time the employer gives. Talk to Kristin if a rejection hits hard. That is part of the work.

**Scam check.** Real employers do not charge fees to apply. The IRC, for example, never charges fees and never asks for passport numbers during interviews (research/03 §6.2, §9 module I). If someone asks you for money, stop and tell Kristin.

## Checklist before an embassy or donor interview or test

- [ ] I have the right to work in the country of the post.
- [ ] I have the advert saved, with every criterion numbered.
- [ ] I have one story for each criterion and each named behaviour.
- [ ] I know which languages will be tested and have practised in each.
- [ ] I know the format: panel, written test, presentation, assessment day.
