# Question bank (starter)

**For:** you, Kristin. This file speaks to you as "you". You can share the questions with clients. Keep the "strong answer" notes as your coaching notes, or share them once the client has drafted their own answer.
**Status:** **STARTER BANK, draft of 4 October 2026.** It is meant to be merged with your own interview and test question lists, which go in `Kristin-question-lists/` (section 4).
**Agrees with:** `un-competency-based-interviews.md` (framework and answer structure), `mock-interview-protocol.md` (how questions are picked) and `program/6-week-programme.md` (Week 5).

---

## 1. Where these questions come from

- **3 questions are published UN examples**, quoted word for word from the UN's CBI training page (research/03 §3.3). They are marked **Published**.
- **37 questions are starter questions** written for this bank in the common competency-based format. They are **not** taken from a real panel or a real employer. They are marked **Starter**.
- The groups follow the **UN Values and Behaviours Framework**: 4 values and 5 behaviours (research/03 §3.3). Extra groups cover two UK FCDO behaviours (research/03 §5.2), motivation, field resilience (research/03 §6.1) and UK strengths questions (research/03 §5.2).
- The "Also fits" tags link each question to the **IRC Success Model** (Receptive, Resourceful, Resilient, Deliver Results) and **"The IRC Way"** values (Integrity, Service, Accountability, Equality) (research/03 §6.2). These links are your coaching suggestions, not IRC definitions.

**What every strong answer shares** (see `un-competency-based-interviews.md` §4): one real example, told in Situation, Action, Result (or STAR) form, with "I" not "we", most of the time on actions, and a result said out loud.

**Segment tags.** "All" means every segment. A, B, C or D marks questions that suit one segment best. For A "New grads", examples from studies, internships, volunteering and student projects count. Say so to the client.

## 2. The 40 questions

### Value: Integrity (4)

**Q1. Tell us about a time you were asked to do something you thought was wrong. What did you do?**
Starter · Also fits: IRC Way Integrity · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* a real pressure, not a hypothetical. The line the candidate drew and why. How they raised it, through which channel. What happened next, including any cost they accepted. No blaming or drama.

**Q2. Describe a mistake you made at work. How did you handle it?**
Starter · Also fits: IRC Way Accountability · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* a real mistake with real consequences. Owning it quickly. Telling the right person. The fix. What the candidate changed so it would not happen again.

**Q3. Give an example of a time you handled confidential or sensitive information.**
Starter · Also fits: IRC Way Integrity · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* what the information was (in general terms) and why it was sensitive. The specific steps taken to protect it. A moment of pressure to share it, if there was one. No confidential detail in the answer itself.

**Q4. Tell us about a time you raised a concern about safeguarding, fraud or misconduct, or helped someone else to raise one.**
Starter · Also fits: IRC Way Accountability · Segments: All (A: university or volunteering examples work)
*A strong answer contains:* knowing the policy. Using the reporting line instead of investigating alone. Protecting the people involved. Safeguarding checks are now standard in INGOs (research/03 §1 point 6, §6.4), so panels may probe this area.

### Value: Inclusion (3)

**Q5. Tell us about a time you made sure a person or group who was not being heard had a voice in a decision.**
Starter · Also fits: IRC Way Equality, Receptive · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* who was left out and why. What the candidate did to bring them in. How the decision changed as a result.

**Q6. Describe working in a team with very different backgrounds, languages or views. How did you make it work?**
Starter · Also fits: Receptive · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* one concrete tension, not "everyone was different". Specific practices: language choice, meeting format, checking understanding. A shared result.

**Q7. Give an example of changing your work so that it reached people who are usually left out.**
Starter · Also fits: IRC Way Equality, Service · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* the barrier (distance, language, disability, gender, legal status). The change made. Evidence that more people were reached.

### Value: Humility (3)

**Q8. Tell us about a time you received difficult feedback. What did you do with it?**
Starter · Also fits: Receptive · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* the feedback in plain words. The first reaction, told honestly. What the candidate did differently afterwards, and a sign that it worked.

**Q9. Describe a time you were wrong about something important at work. How did you find out, and what changed?**
Starter · Also fits: Receptive · Segments: All (B: a decision as a lead)
*A strong answer contains:* a real misjudgement. How the evidence reached them. Changing course openly. What they check now.

**Q10. Tell us about a time you asked for help when you could have tried to manage alone.**
Starter · Also fits: Receptive, Resourceful · Segments: All (A: strong for new grads)
*A strong answer contains:* why asking was the right call. Who they asked and how. What the help made possible. Credit given to the helper.

### Value: Humanity (3)

**Q11. Tell us about a time you put the needs of the people you serve ahead of what was easiest for your team.**
Starter · Also fits: IRC Way Service · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* a real trade-off. Who gained and who carried the extra work. How the team was brought along. The effect on the people served.

**Q12. Describe a time you had to give bad news to a community, a partner or a beneficiary. How did you do it?**
Starter · Also fits: IRC Way Service · Segments: All (D: project closures after funding cuts)
*A strong answer contains:* preparation, honesty, timing and language. Listening to the reaction. What was offered next, even if small.

**Q13. Give an example of treating someone with dignity in a difficult situation.**
Starter · Also fits: IRC Way Service, Equality · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* the situation from the other person's point of view. Small, specific actions. No hero framing.

### Behaviour: Connect and Collaborate (4)

**Q14. "Give an example of a time when you used your problem solving abilities to resolve an issue between staff members."**
**Published** (research/03 §3.3) · Also fits: Resourceful · Segments: All (B: as a manager)
*A strong answer contains:* the conflict in neutral terms. Listening to both sides. The candidate's own steps, not the manager's. A working solution and how the relationship looked after.

**Q15. Tell us about a time you worked with a partner organisation or another agency to deliver something neither could do alone.**
Starter · Also fits: Deliver Results · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* why the partnership was needed. Roles agreed. One friction point and how it was handled. The joint result.

**Q16. Describe a time you got something done through people who did not report to you.**
Starter · Also fits: Resourceful · Segments: B, C
*A strong answer contains:* influence without authority. What the others needed. How the candidate made it easy to say yes. The result and the timeline.

**Q17. Tell us about a disagreement with a colleague or manager. How was it resolved?**
Starter · Also fits: Receptive · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* a real disagreement on substance, not personality. Respectful, direct conversation. A decision, and acceptance if it went the other way.

### Behaviour: Analyse and Plan (4)

**Q18. "How have you handled situations when you had a number of demands being made for delivery at the same time?"**
**Published** (research/03 §3.3) · Also fits: Resourceful, Deliver Results · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* one specific crunch, not a general habit. How priorities were set and who was told. What was delayed or delegated. Everything delivered, or a clear explanation of what was not.

**Q19. Tell us about a decision you made using data. What data, and what did you decide?**
Starter · Also fits: Deliver Results · Segments: All (C: corporate examples translate well)
*A strong answer contains:* the question the data answered. The source and its limits. The decision. What happened after.

**Q20. Describe a plan you built for a project or activity. How did you set priorities and track progress?**
Starter · Also fits: Deliver Results · Segments: All (A: a thesis or event plan works)
*A strong answer contains:* the goal, the steps and the timeline. The tool used to track. One adjustment made along the way.

**Q21. Tell us about a time a plan went wrong. How did you notice, and what did you do?**
Starter · Also fits: Resilient, Resourceful · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* the early signal. A quick, honest response. The recovery. What the candidate builds into plans now.

### Behaviour: Deliver Results with Positive Impact (4)

**Q22. "Tell us about a challenging assignment when you went above and beyond your manager's expectations."**
**Published** (research/03 §3.3) · Also fits: Deliver Results · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* what "expected" was, so the "beyond" is visible. The extra the candidate chose to do and why. A measurable result. The effect on the people served, not only on the manager.

**Q23. Give an example of delivering on time with fewer resources than you needed.**
Starter · Also fits: Resourceful, Deliver Results · Segments: All (D: strong after funding cuts)
*A strong answer contains:* the gap in money, time or people. The trade-offs made. What was protected. The result.

**Q24. Tell us about a result you are proud of. How did you measure it?**
Starter · Also fits: Deliver Results · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* a result with a number or a clear change. How it was measured. The candidate's share of it, stated honestly.

**Q25. Describe a time you were accountable for a deliverable to a donor or a client. How did you make sure it was good?**
Starter · Also fits: IRC Way Accountability · Segments: B, C, D
*A strong answer contains:* the deliverable and the deadline. Quality checks. A problem found and fixed before delivery. The donor's or client's response.

### Behaviour: Learn and Develop (3)

**Q26. Tell us about a skill you had to learn quickly for a task. How did you learn it?**
Starter · Also fits: Receptive, Resourceful · Segments: All (A, C: strong)
*A strong answer contains:* why it was needed now. The learning method. How fast. The task done with the new skill.

**Q27. Describe how you helped a colleague, volunteer or team member grow.**
Starter · Also fits: Receptive · Segments: B (A: peer or volunteer examples)
*A strong answer contains:* where the person started. What the candidate did: coaching, feedback, opportunities. Where the person got to.

**Q28. What have you learned in the last year that changed how you work?**
Starter · Also fits: Receptive · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* one concrete learning, with an example of the change. If the answer is about AI, it describes responsible use. UNDP's rules are a good reference: AI for proofreading, structuring and practice, never to invent experience (research/03 §3.7).

### Behaviour: Adapt and Innovate (3)

**Q29. Tell us about a time the context changed suddenly (security, funding or policy) and you had to change your approach.**
Starter · Also fits: Resilient, Resourceful · Segments: All (D: strong)
*A strong answer contains:* the change and how fast it came. What was kept and what was dropped. How people were kept informed. The new result.

**Q30. Describe a new idea or way of working you introduced. What happened?**
Starter · Also fits: Resourceful · Segments: All (C: corporate innovation translates well)
*A strong answer contains:* the problem the idea solved. How others were persuaded. A test or pilot. An honest result, including what did not work.

**Q31. Tell us about a time you had to work with much less certainty than you wanted.**
Starter · Also fits: Resilient · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* what was unknown. How the candidate decided anyway. How they kept options open. What they would do the same next time.

### UK FCDO behaviours (2)

The FCDO example post named "Communicating and Influencing" and "Seeing the Big Picture" (research/03 §5.2). Each advert names its own. Use these two only for FCDO-type roles, or as extra practice.

**Q32. Communicating and Influencing: tell us about a time you persuaded someone more senior to change their view.**
Starter · Also fits: Connect and Collaborate · Segments: B, C, D
*A strong answer contains:* the original view and why it mattered. Evidence and framing chosen for that person. Listening to objections. The changed decision. Answered in STAR form (research/03 §5.2).

**Q33. Seeing the Big Picture: give an example of linking your work to your organisation's wider strategy or priorities.**
Starter · Also fits: Analyse and Plan · Segments: B, C
*A strong answer contains:* the wider strategy named in one line. The link the candidate saw that others had not. What they changed because of it. Answered in STAR form.

### Motivation and fit (3)

**Q34. Why do you want this role, with this employer, now?**
Starter · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* three parts. Something specific about the employer's work (from research, not the homepage slogan). The match with the candidate's experience. Why now in their career. Under 2 minutes.

**Q35. Walk us through your career so far.**
Starter · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* the positioning statement from Week 1, told as a short story with a direction. A: from studies to this role. C: the shift story, told as a move towards, not away. D: the end of the last contract in one neutral line, then forward. Under 2 minutes.

**Q36. Where do you see yourself in a few years, and how does this role fit?**
Starter · Segments: All (B: strong)
*A strong answer contains:* a realistic direction linked to this role. Commitment to the role itself. No grade-chasing.

### Field roles: stress and resilience (2)

NRC asks field candidates about stress, resilience and health (research/03 §6.1). Clients do not have to share medical details.

**Q37. How do you look after your own wellbeing in a high-pressure or insecure environment?**
Starter · Also fits: Resilient · Segments: All (field roles)
*A strong answer contains:* specific, real habits. A time they were tested. Knowing their own warning signs and when to ask for support.

**Q38. Tell us about the most stressful period of your work so far. How did you manage it?**
Starter · Also fits: Resilient · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* the pressure, told honestly. What they did, for the work and for themselves. What they learned about their limits.

### Strengths questions, UK style (2)

The official advice is "Do not rehearse your answers" (research/03 §5.2). Do not script these with the client. Ask them cold in mocks.

**Q39. What kind of tasks give you energy?**
Starter · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* a real, specific answer said with genuine energy. A short example. Not what the candidate thinks the panel wants.

**Q40. Tell us about something you do well that others find hard.**
Starter · Segments: All
*A strong answer contains:* a specific strength, a quick example, and how it shows up in the role. Modest but clear.

## 4. How to merge your own lists

Your own interview and test question lists go in `Kristin-question-lists/` (see its README). When you merge:

1. **Keep the numbers Q1-Q40.** Add your questions from Q41 up, so mock feedback that cites a number stays valid.
2. **Tag the source** on every question: **Published** (from an employer's own page, with the link), **Starter** (this file), **Kristin** (your lists) or **Client-reported** (asked in a real interview, shared with consent, names removed).
3. **Put each question in a group.** If none fits, add a group. Technical questions per role family (for example M&E, finance, protection) can get their own groups.
4. **Remove duplicates.** If your version of a question is better, keep yours and note the starter number it replaces.
5. **Write a "strong answer contains" note** for each new question. Keep it to what the answer must show, not a model answer.
6. **Never store test content an employer marked confidential.** For tests, record the format, time and task type only.

You can ask Claude to do the merge: "Merge the lists in `Kristin-question-lists/` into `question-bank.md` following section 4. Show me the duplicates before you remove any."
