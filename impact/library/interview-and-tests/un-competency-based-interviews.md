## 1. How UN hiring works

Almost every employer in the sector, the UN included, uses the same five steps (research/03 §2):

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Screening | Your application is checked against the minimum requirements. Many systems ask pre-screening questions. |
| 2. Assessment | A written test, a pre-recorded video interview, a work sample or an assessment centre. |
| 3. Interview | A panel interview, mostly competency-based, sometimes with technical questions. |
| 4. Checks | References and background checks. Sometimes medical and security clearance. |
| 5. Offer or roster | An offer, or a place on a roster for later posts. |

**Where you apply.**
- **UN Secretariat:** jobs are on careers.un.org and you apply through **Inspira**. Each job opening has a "Competencies" section and an "Assessment" section (research/03 §3.1).
- **UN agencies use their own portals.** UNDP, UNICEF and UN Volunteers each have their own (research/03 §3.1). For UN Volunteers you first build a profile in the UNV Global Talent Pool.
- **Openings close fast.** One P-3 opening checked for the research was open for 14 days (research/03 §3.1). Check the tracker every week.

**The 2026 picture.** The UN's proposed 2026 budget would cut 2,681 posts, an 18.8% reduction, and nearly all of those cuts hit posts already left empty by a hiring freeze (research/03 §3.6). That means fewer openings and more experienced applicants for each one. Strong test and interview performance matters more than before, and so do rosters (research/03 §3.6).

**Rosters.** Qualified candidates who are not selected can be put on a roster and offered later jobs in the same job family and grade without a new competition (research/03 §3.4). A US government guide says a roster place lasts 2 years for men and 3 years for women. That was not checked against the UN's own rules (research/03 §3.4). If you reach an interview and are not selected, ask whether you were rostered.

## 2. Screening questions (Inspira and agency portals)

**What we know.**
- Screening checks your application against the minimum requirements first. Many systems ask pre-screening questions (research/03 §2). UNDP uses them (research/03 §9 module F).
- Each UN Secretariat opening lists its competencies and its assessment method (research/03 §3.1).

**What we do not know.** The exact wording of the questions was not recorded in the research. Kristin collects real examples from clients' applications: [INSPIRA-SCREENING-EXAMPLES].

**How to answer them.**
1. Before you start the application, copy the opening's minimum requirements, the "Competencies" section and the "Assessment" section into one document.
2. For every requirement, find the line in your CV or profile that proves it, in the advert's own words. If there is no line, fix the CV first (Week 2).
3. **Answer yes/no questions truthfully.** A "yes" you cannot prove can surface at the reference and document checks in step 4 (research/03 §2).
4. **Count years of experience exactly.** Make the dates match your CV.
5. **State your language level honestly.** Language is one of the things UN tests can measure (research/03 §3.2).
6. **Free-text questions:** answer with one real example in Situation, Action, Result form (section 4 below). Stay inside the word or character limit.
7. **Save every answer** in a document. Reuse the wording next time so your applications stay consistent.
8. **AI:** fine for proofreading and structure. Not fine for inventing or inflating experience (research/03 §3.7).

## 3. The competency framework

The UN interview tests how you behaved in the past, because past behaviour predicts future behaviour. You match your own examples to the competencies named in the job opening (research/03 §3.3).

**The UN Values and Behaviours Framework** (research/03 §3.3):

| Type | Name | Stories that tend to fit (coaching suggestion, not an official definition) |
|---|---|---|
| Value | Inclusion | A time you made sure someone or some group was heard or included |
| Value | Integrity | A time you did the right thing when it cost you something |
| Value | Humility | A time you were wrong, asked for help or gave credit away |
| Value | Humanity | A time you put the people you serve at the centre of a decision |
| Behaviour | Connect and Collaborate | Working across teams, partners or agencies to get something done |
| Behaviour | Analyse and Plan | Planning under pressure, setting priorities, using data to decide |
| Behaviour | Deliver Results with Positive Impact | Delivering on time with limited means, and showing the result |
| Behaviour | Learn and Develop | Learning a new skill fast, or acting on feedback |
| Behaviour | Adapt and Innovate | Changing approach when the context changed, or trying a new way |

**Three cautions.**
- **Use the list in the job opening.** Some postings may still use the older UN competency list (research/03 §3.3, secondary source). Whatever the opening names is what the panel scores.
- **Agencies add their own guidance.** UNDP published a short CBI guide for applicants in 2025 (research/03 §3.3). Read it before a UNDP interview.
- **Humanitarian and field roles** may also draw on the Core Humanitarian Competency Framework. It exists in English, Arabic, French and other languages (research/03 §6.4).

## 4. How to structure an answer: SAR, STAR or CAR

The UN asks for **Situation, Action, Result** (research/03 §3.3). The UK uses **STAR**: Situation, Task, Action, Result (research/03 §5.2). **CAR** (Context, Action, Result) is the same skeleton as SAR with a different first word. CAR is a common coaching name, not one research/03 found at an employer. Use the name the employer uses. The skeleton does not change.

**Rules for every answer.**
1. **One real example per question** (research/03 §1 point 2). Not "I usually...". One time, one place.
2. **Say "I", not "we".** The panel scores what you did.
3. **Keep the situation short.** Spend most of your time on your actions. A rough split that works in practice: situation 20%, action 60%, result 20% (coaching guideline).
4. **End with a result:** a number, a change, a decision, a reaction. Then one line on what you learned.
5. **Aim for about 2-3 minutes** per answer (coaching guideline). Stop when the result is said.

**Story card (one per story, 10-12 in total in Week 5):**

| Field | Your notes |
|---|---|
| Story title (3-5 words) | |
| Competencies it shows (2-3, from the opening) | |
| Situation / context (2 sentences) | |
| Task (what you had to do) | |
| Actions (3-4 things you did, each starting with "I") | |
| Result (number or change) | |
| What I learned | |
| Questions it answers (from the question bank) | |

## 5. How panels score

**What the research says.**
- The panel matches your examples to the competencies in the job opening (research/03 §3.3).
- Each employer scores against a named framework (research/03 §1 point 4).
- In UN tests, grading is anonymous, except for interviews and presentations (research/03 §3.2).
- UNDP's second phase is mainly a competency-based interview, which may include technical questions and extra tests (research/03 §3.2).

**What the research did not find.** A published rating scale for UN panels. So do not try to guess the scale. Make the panel's job easy instead.

**How to make the competency easy to hear.**
- Repeat a key word from the question in your first sentence.
- Tell one example, not three.
- Make your own actions specific: what you said, wrote, decided, changed.
- Say the result out loud. Panels cannot score a result you did not mention.
- If you blank, ask for a moment, or ask to come back to the question.

**Habits to cut** (from practice, not from a UN source): long background, "we" all the way through, a hypothetical answer ("I would..."), a story with no result, and the same story for every question.

## 6. The written assessment and other tests

**Formats the UN lists** (research/03 §3.2):
- a written test on the **UN Online Examinations and Tests System (UNOETS)**
- an **asynchronous (pre-recorded) video interview**
- **work samples**, for example a portfolio
- an **assessment centre**

These can measure cognitive ability, situational judgement, personality, integrity, subject knowledge, communication or drafting skills, and language (research/03 §3.2).

**Agency examples** (research/03 §3.2):
- **UNDP**, phase 1: a pre-recorded video interview and/or a written test on motivation, technical skills and language. Phase 2: the competency interview. Then a reference check for people who are not already UN staff.
- **UNICEF:** blended, technical and competency interviews, presentations, timed tests and exercises (writing, analysis, strategic thinking), assessment centres (more often for senior roles), desk review, and verbal, numerical and logical tests, including online psychometric tests.

**How to prepare.**
1. **Timed drafting.** Write under a real time limit. Kristin marks your work on five points: logical structure, concision, clarity, fit to audience and purpose, and good use of the material provided. These are the EU essay "anchors", used here as a marking rubric (research/03 §4.1, §9 module B).
2. **Reasoning practice:** verbal, numerical and abstract reasoning, and situational judgement (research/03 §9 module C).
3. **Video practice:** camera at eye level, quiet room, good light, timing each answer (research/03 §9 module D).
4. **AI is banned in any test where it is not explicitly allowed,** and in live interviews (research/03 §3.7).

**Two-week practice plan (Week 5 and after):**

| Day | Task | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Timed drafting task 1, then self-mark against the five points | 1-1.5 h |
| 3 | One reasoning practice set | 30-60 min |
| 5 | Record 3 video answers from the question bank; keep the best take | 45 min |
| 8 | Timed drafting task 2, sent to Kristin for marking | 1-1.5 h |
| 10 | One reasoning practice set | 30-60 min |
| 12 | Timed drafting task 3, focused on the weakest point from task 2 | 1-1.5 h |

## 7. The Young Professionals Programme (for new grads)

- An entrance exam held once a year, open to nationals of the countries taking part that year. The list changes yearly (research/03 §3.5).
- Applications usually open in June, through Inspira (research/03 §3.5).
- To apply you must be 32 or younger in the exam year, hold at least a first university degree in the field, and be fluent in English or French (research/03 §3.5).
- The written exam has a **General Paper** (the same for all exam areas) and a **Specialized Paper** (multiple-choice and open questions on the subject). People who pass take a competency-based video interview (research/03 §3.5).
- Other agencies run their own YPPs. For example, FAO's YPP closes 14 October 2026 (research/03 §3.5, secondary source). Check the official page.
