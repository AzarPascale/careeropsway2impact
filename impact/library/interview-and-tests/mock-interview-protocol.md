## 1. What a mock is, and what you say about it

A mock is a full practice interview under real conditions, followed by specific feedback. It turns the story bank from Week 5 into answers the client can give under pressure.

**Say this once, in the intro call or at the first mock:** you have not sat on hiring panels and have not held an HR role (`context/founder-profile.md`). Your mocks are built on employers' published guidance (research/03 §3.3, §5.2) and on your experience in the sector. Do not present your scoring sheet as the way a real panel scores. No published UN panel rating scale was found in the research.

**What a mock is not:** a prediction. Never tell a client they "would pass" or "would fail". Tell them what was clear, what was missing and what to practise.

## 2. Before the mock

**The client sends, at least 48 hours before (proposal):**
- the job opening (real or target), with its named competencies or behaviours;
- the story bank from Week 5;
- the format they expect, if known: panel, pre-recorded video, language check, presentation.

**You prepare (about 20 minutes):**
1. Pick 6-7 questions from `question-bank.md`, matched to the competencies in the opening. Include at least one the client has not seen.
2. Add one motivation question ("Why this role, why this employer?") and one technical question from the job description.
3. If the role needs a second language, plan a 5-minute segment in that language (research/03 §6.1, §9 module G).
4. Decide your three panel "hats": hiring manager, technical person and HR. Panels in the research often mix these roles (research/03 §5.1, §6.1).
5. Check the recording works on Zoom or Meet.

**Consent.** Ask before you record. The client can say no. Then you take written notes instead.

## 3. The 45-minute mock: run sheet

| Min | Block | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | Brief and warm-up | State the role, the panel and the rules. Start recording (with consent). Ask one warm-up question, not scored: "Tell us about yourself." |
| 5-35 | Interview (30 min) | Ask 6-7 questions. Announce your hat when you switch ("Now as the technical lead..."). Use follow-up probes (below). Run the language segment if planned. End with: "What questions do you have for us?" |
| 35-38 | Step out of role | Stop the "panel" clearly. Ask the client: "What went well? What would you change?" Let them speak first. |
| 38-45 | Feedback | Give 3 things to keep and 3 to change (section 5). Name one drill for each change. |

**Follow-up probes** (practice probes, use one or two per answer):
- "What exactly did you do?"
- "What was the result? How did you know?"
- "What would you do differently next time?"
- "What was your role, compared to the team's?"

**Stay in role during the interview.** Do not coach mid-answer. If the client freezes, wait 10 seconds, then offer: "Take a moment, or we can come back to it." That is what you want them to practise asking a real panel.

### Fitting the mock into a 60-minute session

| Session | How the 60 minutes run |
|---|---|
| **Week 5, Interviews and tests** | The weekly session has only a short mock (min 50-55): two questions on camera, with feedback on voice, pace and length of answers. The client records more answers as homework. Deliverable: written feedback with 3 to keep and 3 to change (`program/6-week-programme.md`, Week 5). For a full mock, use an extra mock (Accompaniment, Plus) or a single-service mock. |
| **Week 5, B "Moving up" swap** | Replace two competency questions with a 10-minute (proposal) strategy or plan presentation, then panel questions on it (research/03 §3.2, §9 module E). |
| **Extra mock (Accompaniment, Plus) or single-service mock** | 0-5 check-in; 5-50 the full 45-minute mock; 50-55 play back the best answer; 55-60 next steps. Written feedback follows. |

### Variants

- **Pre-recorded video interview.** These are common in early rounds (research/03 §3.2, §9 module D). Send 4 questions one at a time. Give the client 30 seconds to read each and a fixed time to answer (match the real invitation if it states one; otherwise 2-3 minutes, coaching default). No retakes unless the real invitation allows them. Review the recordings together.
- **Strengths questions (UK).** Do not let the client script these. The official advice is not to rehearse (research/03 §5.2, §9 module J). Ask them cold and judge only whether the answer sounds real and specific.
- **Field roles.** Add one question on stress, resilience and health. NRC asks field candidates about these (research/03 §6.1). Do not ask about medical details.

## 4. The scoring sheet

This is your coaching scale, not an employer's scale. Use it to find patterns, not to give a grade.

**Score each answer from 1 to 4 on five points.**

| Point | 1: missing | 2: partly | 3: clear | 4: strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Structure** | No clear situation, action or result | One part missing or muddled | Situation, action, result all present | Tight and easy to follow, no wasted words |
| **B. Own actions** | "We" throughout, or a hypothetical ("I would...") | Some "I", but vague actions | Specific actions the client took | Specific, with the reasoning behind each choice |
| **C. Result** | No result | Result implied, not said | Result said out loud | Result with a number or a clear change, plus what was learned |
| **D. Fit** | Answers a different question | Touches the competency | Clearly shows the named competency | Shows the competency and links it to the role |
| **E. Delivery** | Over 4 minutes, or under 1, or hard to follow | Too long or rushed in places | 2-3 minutes, steady pace (coaching guideline) | Calm, clear voice, good eye line to the camera |

| Q | Question (and competency)       | A | B | C | D | E | Note (quote the client's words) |

**How to read it.** Look down the columns. A low column across several answers is the pattern to work on. One weak answer is a story problem, not a skill problem: fix the story in the bank.

## 5. The feedback format

### Live (the last 7-15 minutes)

1. **Client first.** "What went well? What would you change?"
2. **Start with what went well.** Watching yourself is uncomfortable for most people (`program/6-week-programme.md`, Week 5).
3. **3 to keep.** Each one specific, with the client's own words: "When you said 'I rewrote the budget in two days', the result was clear."
4. **3 to change.** Each one specific and doable. Pair each with one drill.
5. **One question to redo now,** if time allows. Let the client hear the difference.

**Feedback rules.**
- Comment on behaviour the client can change: structure, actions, result, length, pace.
- Comment on accent only if it blocks understanding, and then on clarity, not on the accent.
- Never comment on appearance, beyond the setting (light, camera height, background).
- One message per change. Do not stack five points into one.

### Written (send within [MOCK-FEEDBACK-TURNAROUND])

```
Mock interview feedback: [client first name], [date]
Role practised: [role, employer type]

3 things to keep
1. [What you did] - [why it works] - [quote or time in the recording]
2.
3.

3 things to change
1. [What happened] - [what to do instead] - Drill: [specific exercise]
2.
3.

Stories to fix in your story bank: [story titles]
Questions to practise again this week: [question numbers from the question bank]
Next step: [real interview date / next mock / pre-interview call]
```

**Common drills.**

| Change needed | Drill |
|---|---|
| Situation too long | Tell the situation in two sentences, out loud, timed at 20 seconds |
| "We" instead of "I" | Rewrite the actions in the story card, each starting with "I" |
| No result | Add one number or one change to every story card |
| Too long | Record the answer, cut to 2 minutes, record again |
| Freezing | Practise the phrase "May I take a moment?" and the first sentence of each story |
| Language segment weak | Prepare the introduction and 3 stories in that language (`embassy-and-donor-processes.md` §5) |

## 6. The pre-interview call: 20-minute agenda

**What it is:** one short call before each real interview or test. Proposal in `business/offer-and-pricing.md`: up to 20 minutes, before each real interview or test, in every tier; not part of single services. This is the one run sheet for the call; `program/support-and-check-in-rules.md` §4 points here. If you set a shorter length, shorten the rehearsal or weak-point block. Confirm the length: [PRE-INTERVIEW-CALL-LENGTH].

**Booking.** The client books through [PRE-INTERVIEW-BOOKING-LINK] or asks on [MESSAGING-CHANNEL], ideally 1-3 days before the interview (proposal). Ask them to send the invitation and the job opening before the call.

**What the call is not:** a new lesson. Do not rewrite stories or add new material. Confidence comes from what the client already has.

### Interview version

| Min | Block | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Arrival | "How are you arriving? One word." Listen. Name it. |
| 2-5 | Logistics | Date, time zone, platform and link tested, panel names if known, language, format, rules on notes and AI (AI is not allowed in live interviews, research/03 §3.7), documents to have ready. |
| 5-11 | Top 3 | The three competencies most likely to come up, from the opening. The client names the story for each and says its first line and its result. Not the full story. |
| 11-16 | One rehearsal | One full answer to the question they fear most. Give one change only. |
| 16-20 | Close | Their 2-3 questions for the panel. Their first 30 seconds ("tell us about yourself"). Agree they message you after the interview. |

### Test version

| Min | Block | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Arrival | One word. Listen. |
| 2-6 | Rules and tech | Format, time limit, language, platform, whether AI or notes are allowed (if not stated, assume not, research/03 §3.7), file format, power and internet backup. |
| 6-12 | The first five minutes | Read the whole task, underline what is asked and who the reader is, write a plan, then write. Agree a time split (coaching guide: about 10% plan, 75% write, 15% check). |
| 12-16 | One weak point | The one thing from their last practice task to watch for. |
| 16-20 | Close | Sleep, food, a quiet room. Message you after. |

## 7. After a real interview or test

1. **Debrief message from the client, the same day (proposal):** what was asked, how it went, any next step.
2. **Capture the questions.** With the client's consent, remove names and add the questions or the task format to `Kristin-question-lists/`. Do not store test content an employer marked confidential. Note only the format, the time and the task type. This is how you replace the unverified INGO test formats (research/03 §6.5).
3. **Update the tracker** stage: interview, test, offer or closed.
4. **If it is a "no", or silence,** use the client's 24-hour rejection plan (programme Week 6, or built early). Many employers give no feedback (research/03 §5.1, §6.1). If what you hear goes beyond coaching, pause and refer: [THERAPIST-REFERRAL].
5. **If an offer comes,** book the negotiation conversation (Week 6 material) and remind the client not to resign before the formal offer (research/03 §5.1).
