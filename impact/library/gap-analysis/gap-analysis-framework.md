## 1. What the gap analysis is

The gap analysis tells you, and the client, exactly where they stand against the roles they want. It happens after sign-up, in Week 0. It is not one of the 12 sessions.

Every programme tier includes it. It is also sold alone on the à-la-carte menu (section 10). It is never done on the free intro call. The intro call only checks fit.

It ends in three things:
1. A written gap report (template: `program/gap-report-template.md`).
2. The client's segment confirmed (A, B, C or D) and their swapped weeks marked.
3. The client's personal 6-week plan.

Research supports this as a real difference: nobody in the competitor scan sells a structured gap analysis that ends in a named certificate plan (research/02 §9 gap 8).

---

## 2. The three gap types

| Gap type | What it means | Typical fixes | Who fixes it | Where in the programme |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Profile gap** | The client does not have something the target roles ask for: a skill, a certificate, a language level, a degree, years or type of experience, or the right to work. | Certificates, courses, language study, experience (volunteering, internships, UN Volunteers, a board seat, short-term work). | The client, with a plan you suggest. | Plan written in Week 0. Started in Week 1 homework. Checked at the Week 4 mid-point check. |
| **CV gap** | The client has the experience, but the CV and LinkedIn do not show it. | Document work: wording, structure, results, format, variants. | You and the client. | Week 2 (CV and cover letters), Week 3 (LinkedIn). |
| **Positioning gap** | The client aims at the wrong roles, aims too wide, or tells a story that does not fit the roles. | Narrative and targeting: positioning statement, role families, employer list, bridge story. | You and the client. | Week 1. Revisited at the Week 4 mid-point check. |

**The key move:** many things that look like profile gaps are really CV gaps. The client did the work but never wrote it down. Your Week 0 call exists mainly to find these. Always ask "have you ever done anything like this?" before you call something missing.

**A second key move:** if a client is missing two or more minimum requirements in most of their target adverts, that is usually a positioning gap, not a profile gap. Change the target before you send them on a course.

## 4. Before the call: the requirements matrix

This is the core tool. It turns "I don't know why I'm not getting interviews" into a list.

Screening checks applications against the minimum requirements first, often with pre-screening questions (research/03 §2). So start with the minimum requirements.

**How to build it:**
1. Take the 3-5 adverts the client sent. If they sent fewer, add adverts from the tracker sources that match their target (research/03 §10).
2. List every requirement from each advert. Mark each one **Must** (minimum requirement) or **Nice** (desirable, asset).
3. For each requirement, check the CV and LinkedIn and mark one status:

| Status | Meaning | Gap type it points to |
|---|---|---|
| **S** Shown | The CV shows it clearly, ideally in the advert's own words. | None |
| **H?** Maybe hidden | The intake suggests the client has it, but the CV does not show it. | CV gap (confirm on the call) |
| **M?** Maybe missing | Nothing in the CV or intake shows it. | Profile gap (test on the call) |

4. Note anything about the target itself: right to work, nationality rules, age limits, language demands. Embassies do not sponsor work permits, so a client needs the right to work locally (research/03 §5.5). The UN Young Professionals Programme has age, degree, language and nationality rules (research/03 §3.5).
5. Read the CV and LinkedIn side by side. Do they tell the same story? Does the LinkedIn headline match the roles in the adverts?

**Simple sheet layout (Google Sheets):**

| Requirement | Advert(s) | Must / Nice | Status before call | Status after call | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. 3 years in project management | 1, 3, 4 | Must | M? | H (ran a student project budget, 2 years in an internship) | Write into CV, Week 2 |

On the call, every **H?** and **M?** becomes either **S**, **H** (confirmed hidden, a CV gap) or **M** (confirmed missing, a profile gap).

## 5. The Week 0 call (60-90 minutes)

Hold it in the language the client chose. Keep the matrix open.

| Min | Block | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | Arrive | Ask how they are arriving. Say what today is for: "to find out where you stand, not to judge it". Remind them it is confidential. |
| 5-15 | Their story | Ask for a 30-second introduction, as they would give it to a recruiter. Do not help yet. This is your positioning test. Then ask what they want from the 6 weeks. |
| 15-40 | Walk the matrix | Go through every **M?** and **H?**. For each, ask: "Have you ever done anything like this? At work, in studies, volunteering, at home, in a family business?" Ask for one concrete example and a result. Mark the new status. |
| 40-55 | Targets and constraints | Check right to work for each target country, languages (switch language for two minutes if a role needs a second language), hours per week, money and time pressure, notice period, whether the current employer knows. Ask what happened with past applications and any tests. Ask them to share any real test invitations they still have (research/00 §5). |
| 55-65 | How they are doing | Return gently to the intake answers on coping. Ask what helps. Ask what they want from you on hard weeks. See section 9. |
| 65-75 | Close | Say which segment you think fits and why. Name the swapped weeks that come with it. Say what the report will contain and when it arrives. Ask: "Is there anything I did not ask that I should know?" |
| 75-90 | Buffer | Use only if needed. Do not fill it for the sake of it. |

**Questions that find hidden experience:**
- "Walk me through a normal week in that role. What did you actually do?"
- "Who did you report to, and who relied on your work?"
- "Did you ever handle money, a budget, a donor, a partner or a report?"
- "What would your old manager say you were best at?"
- "What did you do that was not in your job description?"

**Questions that test positioning:**
- "If you could only apply to one type of role, which one, and why?"
- "Why this employer and not a similar one?"
- "Read me your LinkedIn headline. Is that who you are now?"

## 6. Diagnosing each gap type

### 6.1 Profile gap

**Signs:**
- A **Must** stays **M** after the call.
- A language level below what the adverts ask for.
- No right to work in the target country for embassy roles (research/03 §5.5).
- A degree field or age that rules out a programme such as the UN YPP (research/03 §3.5).
- A certificate or test the target employers check (for example, EU Delegations verify diplomas and language certificates, research/03 §4.3).

**Rules for a fix plan:**
- Fix **Musts** before **Nices**.
- Only suggest a certificate or course if the target adverts ask for it, or if it clearly closes a Must.
- Prefer free courses first. Free course libraries exist for humanitarian and development staff, such as Kaya and DisasterReady (research/02 §6; their course counts are unverified).
- For sustainability and CSR roles, the GRI Certified Sustainability Professional is one credential that helps (research/03 §8).
- For corporate shifters, close the "mission credibility" gap with volunteering, a board seat or a short certificate (research/01 §8.2).
- For new grads, the research points to AI, data and fundraising skills, fellowships and free courses (research/01 §8.2).
- The fix must fit the client's hours. The weekly homework is already 3-5 hours (`program/6-week-programme.md`). If a fix takes longer than 6 weeks, say so, and adjust the targets for now.
- Your own curated list of courses and certificates goes here: [Kristin-COURSE-LIST].
- If you ever receive a payment or commission for recommending a course or provider, tell the client. The ICF Code requires disclosure of referral payments (research/04 §9).

### 6.2 CV gap

**Signs:**
- A requirement is **H** (the client has it, the CV hides it).
- The CV uses different words from the advert, so it may fail screening on requirements the client actually meets (research/03 §2).
- Duties listed, no results or numbers.
- Wrong format for the target: US embassies want every requirement covered and personal details such as religion or marital status left off (research/03 §5.1); FCDO wants anonymised CVs (research/03 §5.2); EU Delegations ask for a Europass CV (research/03 §4.3).
- CV and LinkedIn say different things.
- For shifters: corporate words that sector readers will not recognise.

**Fix:** the CV and cover letters in Week 2, then the LinkedIn makeover in Week 3. How much you co-write depends on the tier (`business/offer-and-pricing.md` §1).

### 6.3 Positioning gap

**Signs:**
- The 30-second introduction is vague, long, or apologetic.
- More than 3 role families, or employer types that need very different documents, with no priority.
- Most adverts have two or more **Musts** marked **M**: the client is aiming too high or in the wrong place for now.
- Aiming only at the roles hit hardest by the cuts, such as project lead and M&E roles, with no bridge story (research/01 §8.1 point 4).
- Ignoring where money is still moving: fundraising and partnerships, development banks, Gulf donors, the EU, foundations, reconstruction (research/01 §5, §7, §8.1).
- Refusing short-term, consulting or roster work when the client's situation needs it (research/01 §8.1 point 6).
- Targeting embassy roles without the right to work there (research/03 §5.5).

**Fix:** the Week 1 main block (segment version), then a check at the Week 4 mid-point check.

## 7. Scoring grid

Score each gap type from 0 to 3. Use the general anchors, then the segment grid for what to look for. Do not add the three scores into one number. The pattern matters more than the total.

### 7.1 General anchors

| Score | Profile gap | CV gap | Positioning gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** None | Meets every Must in most target adverts. | Every Must the client meets is shown, in the right format. | Clear two-sentence statement. 2-3 role families that fit the profile. CV and LinkedIn agree. |
| **1** Small | Missing one or two Nices, or one Must that a short course or certificate can close inside 6 weeks. | Some Nices hidden. Wording does not match adverts. Few results. | Statement exists but is fuzzy, or CV and LinkedIn disagree. |
| **2** Medium | One Must missing that takes longer than 6 weeks to close, or several Nices missing. | One or more Musts hidden. Wrong format for the main target employer. | Too wide (more than 3 role families), or aimed at hard-hit roles without a bridge story, or a right-to-work problem for some targets. |
| **3** Large | Two or more Musts missing in most adverts. Check positioning first: this is often a targeting problem. | Most strengths hidden. The CV would likely fail screening on requirements the client meets. | Targets do not fit the profile, or the client cannot yet say what they want. |

### 7.2 Segment grids

**A "New grads" (breaking in; lead segment)**

| Gap | What to look for | What a 3 looks like | First fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profile | Degree field, language levels, internships, volunteering, AI and data skills (research/01 §8.2). YPP eligibility: age, first degree in the field, English or French, nationality list (research/03 §3.5). | Targets need years of experience the client does not have. | Change targets first, then a short free course or a volunteering or internship route. |
| CV | Education and projects buried. Volunteering left off. CV too long. No UNV-ready version if they target UN Volunteers (research/03 §3.1). | Strong studies and projects, none of it visible. | Week 2 swap: CV with little paid experience. |
| Positioning | Aiming at junior international UN posts, which were cut harder in several agencies, instead of national roles, internships, short-term contracts, Gulf and local organisations and UNV (research/01 §8.2). | Only international UN posts on the target list. | Week 1 swap: the breaking-in map. |

**B "Moving up" (insiders wanting a bigger role)**

| Gap | What to look for | What a 3 looks like | First fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profile | Experience the next grade asks for: team, budget, partnerships, fundraising or strategy (research/01 §8.2). | No budget, team or partnership responsibility at all, and the target grade asks for it. | Find a stretch task in the current job or a lateral move. Certificates rarely close this alone. |
| CV | Duties instead of results. No revenue, partnership, cost or change-leadership results (research/01 §8.2). | The CV reads like the current grade, not the next one. | Week 2 core, with results rewritten for the next grade. |
| Positioning | Aiming only at senior posts, which are fewer and more contested (research/01 §8.2). No next-grade story. Not considering banks, foundations or Gulf donors (research/01 §8.2). | Only one employer type, and it is shrinking. | Week 1 swap: the next-grade story. |

**C "Shifters" (corporate to impact)**

| Gap | What to look for | What a 3 looks like | First fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profile | The "mission credibility" gap: no volunteering, board seat or sector certificate (research/01 §8.2). For sustainability roles, credentials such as GRI CSP (research/03 §8). | No link to the sector at all, and targets ask for sector experience. | Volunteering, a board seat or a short certificate (research/01 §8.2). |
| CV | Corporate words. Results that matter to sales, not to donors and programmes. No ATS-friendly version for CSR roles, which follow normal corporate hiring (research/03 §8, secondary sources). | A sector reader cannot see why this person fits. | Week 2 swap: the translation CV. |
| Positioning | Treating the corporate background as a weakness. Commercial, finance and partnership skills are in demand (research/01 §8.2). Targets not yet narrowed to CSR, impact investing, foundations, banks or fundraising (research/01 §8.2). | Applying to programme roles that need field experience. | Week 1 swap: the translation. |

**D "Repositioning" (laid off in the funding cuts)**

| Gap | What to look for | What a 3 looks like | First fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profile | Usually small. Check if the old field was among the hardest hit (project leads, M&E, health, humanitarian, governance) and if a bridge skill is needed (research/01 §8.1 point 4, §8.2). | The only skills are in a field with few openings, and no bridge exists yet. | A bridge skill or certificate that fits a field that is holding. |
| CV | Old titles and donor jargon. Results not in transferable language. The end of the contract not explained (`program/6-week-programme.md`, Week 2 D). No proof of efficiency (research/01 §8.1 point 3). | A strong record that only reads well to the old employer. | Week 2 swap: the reframe CV. |
| Positioning | Aiming only at the old role type. Refusing consulting or short-term work as "failure" (research/01 §8.2). Grief for the mission showing in the story (research/01 §4). | Every target is a copy of the lost job. | Week 1 swap: take stock before you reposition. |

## 8. How the result selects the swapped weeks

### Step 1. Confirm the segment

The segment sets the swapped weeks. Use the client's main situation today, not their hopes.

| If the client... | Segment |
|---|---|
| Has little or no paid experience in the sector and is breaking in | **A New grads** |
| Is in the sector now and wants a bigger role | **B Moving up** |
| Comes from a corporate or private-sector career and wants an impact role | **C Shifters** |
| Lost a sector job in the funding cuts and needs to reposition | **D Repositioning** |

**When two fit:** choose by the bigger gap.
- Laid off after only a short time in the sector: if the positioning gap is about breaking in, use A. If it is about loss and translating past work, use D.
- Corporate person who was also laid off: use C if the target is new to them, D if they are returning to a field they worked in.
- In the sector and recently laid off, aiming higher than before: use D. Moving up comes after landing.

### Step 2. Mark the swapped weeks

These are fixed by segment (`program/6-week-programme.md`, one-page schedule):

| Segment | Swapped weeks |
|---|---|
| A New grads | 1, 2, 4, 9, 11 |
| B Moving up | 1, 6, 10, 11 |
| C Shifters | 1, 2, 6, 9 |
| D Repositioning | 1, 2, 4, 7, 11 |

### Step 3. Borrow one block if the client is mixed (proposal)

Sometimes one week of another segment fits better. You may swap in **one** block from another segment for the same week number. The week keeps its slot, topic and type of deliverable, so this follows the programme's own swap rule.

Examples:
- A new grad who is not eligible for the UN YPP and targets CSR roles can take the **C** Week 5 block (the CSR and ESG case) instead of the **A** block (YPP exams).
- A shifter who lost their corporate job and is struggling can take the **D** blocks in Weeks 1 and 6 (loss and identity).
- A new grad with no contacts in the sector at all can take the **C** Week 6 block (entering a sector where you know no one).

Write any borrowed block in the score record and in the report. Do not borrow more than one. If you want to borrow more, the segment is probably wrong.

### Step 4. Let the scores set the emphasis

The scores do not change the order of weeks. They change where you spend time and homework.

| Result | What changes |
|---|---|
| Profile gap 2-3 | The course and certificate plan goes in the report. The client starts it in Week 1 homework. Check progress at the Week 4 mid-point check. |
| Profile gap 3 with positioning gap 2-3 | Change the targets in Week 1 before any course. A course does not fix a wrong target. |
| CV gap 2-3 | Weeks 2-3 are the priority. Use the tier's co-writing time here first. Ask for an application review before every early application. |
| CV gap 0-1 | Move faster through Weeks 2-3 and give more time to cover letters and criteria answers. |
| Positioning gap 2-3 | Use the full Week 1 main block. Hold the CV variants until the target list is agreed. Revisit at the Week 4 mid-point check. |
| All three 0-1 | The client mainly needs rhythm, practice and support. Weeks 4 and 5 carry the most value. |
| A real interview or test already booked | Do not reorder the core. Use a pre-interview call (up to 20 minutes, `business/offer-and-pricing.md` §1) and pull in what is needed from Week 5 (`program/6-week-programme.md`). |

## 9. Emotional state: read it, do not score it

The intake is often the first time the client says out loud how the search feels (`program/6-week-programme.md`, Week 0). Read that part of the intake first, before the CV.

- Do not score it. Do not use clinical screening tools. You are a coach, not a therapist.
- Use what you read to set the tone of the call and to choose the order of topics.
- People who lost sector jobs often report grief for the mission, not only for the job, and long searches (research/01 §4, §8.1 point 9). Give this room on the call, especially for segment D.
- **If anything points to depression, severe anxiety or crisis**, stop the gap analysis. Offer the referral to a therapist you trust ([THERAPIST-REFERRAL]) and the pause clause: the client can pause once, for up to 4 weeks, and the end date moves (`business/offer-and-pricing.md` §5). Write the referral in your client notes. Restart Week 0 when the client is ready.

## 11. Quality check before you send the report

- Every gap names the advert requirement it comes from.
- Every profile-gap fix names a provider, a cost band and a time estimate, or says "to confirm".
- No promise of a job, an interview or a salary (research/04 §9).
- Nothing in the plan asks the client to claim experience they do not have.
- The segment, the swapped weeks and any borrowed block match the score record.
- The report is in the client's chosen language.
