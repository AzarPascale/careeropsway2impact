## 1. Who this is for

Clients aiming at EU Delegations, embassies and bilateral donors. The research found no coach or firm targeting embassy or donor-agency roles, and demand for this niche is unmeasured (research/02 §9 gap 7). It is also the realistic EU route for Lebanese nationals, who cannot sit most EU civil-service exams (research/03 §1 point 5).

| Door | What the application needs | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **EU Delegation, Local Agent** (example: Project Officer, EU Delegation to Lebanon, posted 2024) | A cover letter, a **Europass-format CV** and a declaration on honour, sent by email. Minimum in that example: a BA and 3 years in cooperation projects. Selection criteria are listed separately and **earn points**. Shortlisting, then a written test and interviews. Medical and background checks, and **verification of diplomas and language certificates**. | research/03 §4.3 |
| **EU Delegation, Contract Agent** | A pass in the EU contract-staff (CAST) tests in the right function group. CAST asks for C1 in one EU language and B2 in another. | research/03 §4.2 |
| **US embassies** | Application through the ERA system. The CV or DS-174 form must cover **every** requirement, or it is not considered. Leave race, religion, marital status and similar details off. 3 personal and 3 professional references. | research/03 §5.1 |
| **UK (FCDO, British Embassy Beirut)** | An application form with behaviour questions answered with STAR, scored on the Success Profiles. An **anonymised CV**: no name, address, email, age, date of birth, gender or nationality. Applications only through the official tal.net site. | research/03 §5.2 |
| **Australian embassy** | A CV, two referees, and a 500-700 word statement answering the selection criteria with examples. **This example ad is from 2017.** Check the current format. | research/03 §5.3 |
| **GIZ** | A CV and cover letter at first. Apply in the language of the advert. Country offices may use their own form. | research/03 §7 |
| **Other embassies** (France, Germany, the Netherlands, Canada, Gulf states) | **Not researched.** Read the advert line by line and follow it. | research/03 §5.5, Limitations |

Two rules for every embassy door (research/03 §5.5):
- The client must **already have the right to work locally**. Embassies do not sponsor permits. Check this in the intro call or Week 1 before the client targets one.
- **Language tests and security vetting are normal.**

## 2. Structure

### 2a. Europass (EU Delegations)

**(General practice, not in the research: check on the Europass site.)** A Europass CV is made in the EU's free online Europass editor. The editor sets the layout. The client controls the content and the order of optional sections. Typical sections:

1. Personal information
2. A short profile ("About me" or similar)
3. Work experience (reverse date order)
4. Education and training
5. Language skills, self-rated on the CEFR scale (A1 to C2) for listening, reading, speaking and writing
6. Digital skills
7. Other sections the client adds (for example projects, publications, volunteering)

The EU itself uses CEFR levels in hiring: CAST asks for C1 and B2 (research/03 §4.2). So rate honestly on that scale. The Delegation verifies language certificates (research/03 §4.3).

The Europass CV never travels alone. In the Lebanon example it goes with a **cover letter** and a **declaration on honour** (research/03 §4.3). The cover letter answers each selection criterion. See `impact/library/cover-letter/framework.md`.

### 2b. Criteria-led CV (US embassies, Australian and other embassies, GIZ)

A normal CV in the NGO layout (`ngo-two-page.md`), with one change: **the order and the wording follow the requirement list in the advert.** Every requirement must be visibly met, because a US embassy does not consider a CV that misses one (research/03 §5.1).

### 2c. Anonymised CV (FCDO)

The same content as the criteria-led CV, with every identifying detail removed (research/03 §5.2). On FCDO forms the behaviour questions carry a lot of weight. Spend more time on those answers than on the CV. They use STAR (research/03 §5.2), the same stories as the Week 5 story bank (`program/6-week-programme.md`).

## 4. Must-have sections

| Section | What it must show | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Every selection criterion or requirement | Visible on the page, in the advert's wording | EU criteria earn points (research/03 §4.3). A US embassy CV that misses a requirement is not considered (research/03 §5.1) |
| Years of experience in the named field | Exact months, and the total years in the field the advert names (for example "cooperation projects") | The EU Lebanon example set a minimum of 3 years (research/03 §4.3) |
| Contract and financial management, drafting | Results and scale, with examples the written test can build on | Both were selection criteria in the EU Lebanon example. A written test follows (research/03 §4.3) |
| Knowledge of the donor in the country | One line or one bullet, for example work with EU-funded projects, if true | "Knowledge of the EU in Lebanon" was a criterion (research/03 §4.3) |
| Languages | Honest level, with certificate name and year | Certificates are verified (research/03 §4.3). Language tests are normal (research/03 §5.1, §5.5). The EU Lebanon example asked for English and Arabic (research/03 §4.3) |
| Education | Degree, field, institution, year, exactly as on the diploma | Diplomas are verified (research/03 §4.3) |
| References | US: 3 personal and 3 professional. Australia example: 2 referees | research/03 §5.1, §5.3 |

## 5. Language and wording

- **One criterion at a time.** Use the criterion's own words. If the criterion says "negotiation and advocacy", the CV says "negotiation and advocacy", not "stakeholder engagement" (research/03 §4.3).
- **Donor words.** Cooperation, partners, contract and financial management, reporting, results. Show where the client worked with or on funds from this donor, if true.
- **Write in the language of the advert** (GIZ asks for this, research/03 §7). A CV in a second language counts as one CV variant (`business/offer-and-pricing.md` §1).
- **Personal details:** follow the employer. US embassies: no race, religion or marital status (research/03 §5.1). FCDO: nothing that identifies the person (research/03 §5.2). Europass: personal fields beyond name and contact are optional **(general practice, check on the Europass site)**. Default to leaving them out.
- **Drafting quality matters twice.** The CV is the first sample of the client's writing, and a written test follows (research/03 §4.3). Short sentences, no errors.

## 6. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Sending a normal CV when the advert asks for Europass | Rebuild it in the Europass editor from the donor variant |
| Answering most criteria, not all | Make a checklist from the advert. Tick each criterion against a line on the CV and a paragraph in the letter |
| Leaving the name or the photo on an FCDO CV | Remove every detail on the FCDO list (research/03 §5.2) |
| Forgetting the declaration on honour or another required document | List every document the advert asks for before writing anything (research/03 §4.3) |
| Rating languages too high | Rate on the CEFR scale honestly. Certificates are checked (research/03 §4.3) |
| Applying to an embassy without the right to work locally | Check first. Embassies do not sponsor permits (research/03 §5.5) |
| Applying outside the official channel | FCDO only accepts tal.net applications (research/03 §5.2). Use the channel the advert names |
| Waiting for feedback | US embassies give none after an unsuccessful interview (research/03 §5.1). The EU Delegation does not discuss the procedure (research/03 §4.3). Keep applying |
| Resigning on a tentative offer | US embassies: do not resign until the formal offer arrives (research/03 §5.1) |

## 7. Annotated skeleton

### 7a. Europass content plan (paste into the Europass editor section by section)

```
PERSONAL INFORMATION
{Full name} · {City, Country} · {email} · {phone}
>> Optional fields (photo, date of birth, nationality): leave out unless the
>> advert asks. Check what the editor offers on the day.

ABOUT ME (3-4 lines)
{Role family} with {n} years in {cooperation / programme / grant} work in {country}.
{Criterion 1 in the advert's words}, shown by {one result}.
{Criterion 2 in the advert's words}, shown by {one result}.

WORK EXPERIENCE (reverse date order)
{Job title} · {Employer} · {City, Country} · {Month Year} to {Month Year}
- {Contract or financial management result, with scale}
- {Drafting result: reports, proposals, notes, with audience and frequency}
- {Negotiation, advocacy or partner result}
>> Map each bullet to a numbered criterion in your own checklist.
>> 3-5 bullets for relevant roles, 1 line for others.

{Career transition entry, if any}
>> One neutral line. See cv-variant-strategy.md §4 D.

EDUCATION AND TRAINING
{Degree}, {Field} · {University}, {Country} · {Year}
>> Exactly as on the diploma. Diplomas are verified (research/03 §4.3).
{Course or certificate} · {Provider} · {Year}

LANGUAGE SKILLS (CEFR, self-assessed)
Mother tongue: {language}
{Language}: listening {level} · reading {level} · speaking {level} · writing {level}
            {certificate and year, if any}
>> Honest levels. Certificates are checked.

DIGITAL SKILLS
{Tools the client really uses, including AI and data tools}

OTHER (optional)
{Knowledge of the donor in the country, if not shown above}
```

## 8. Before you use this file for a new embassy or donor

The research covered only a few employers, and some examples are old (research/03 Limitations). For any other embassy or donor:
1. Open the live advert. List every document it asks for.
2. Copy the selection criteria into a checklist.
3. Note the channel, the language and the deadline.
4. Ask the client to send you any test invitation they receive, with their consent, so your prep bank grows (research/00 §5).
