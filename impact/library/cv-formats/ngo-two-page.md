# NGO format: the two-page CV

**For:** you, Kristin. "You" is Kristin. "The client" is the person you coach. You can share section 7 (the skeleton) with a client.
**Status:** draft, 4 October 2026.
**Sources:** research/03 §2 and §6 for INGO hiring. The two-page length is a **house rule (proposal)**, not a research finding. Change it once your own samples are in `Kristin-samples/`.

---

## 1. Who this is for

International and local NGOs: INGO country offices, local NGOs, and the NGO roles listed on Daleel Madani and ReliefWeb (research/03 §10). Also the default layout when a UN agency portal or a foundation asks for an uploaded CV and gives no format.

What the reader does with it:
- **NRC checks languages first, then experience,** at screening (research/03 §6.1).
- Interviews cover technical and behavioural competencies (research/03 §6.1). The IRC looks for its Success Model: Receptive, Resourceful, Resilient, Deliver Results (research/03 §6.2). The sector also shares the Core Humanitarian Competency Framework (CHCF), which exists in English, Arabic, French and other languages (research/03 §6.4).
- Reference, background and safeguarding checks follow. More than 380 organisations use the Misconduct Disclosure Scheme (research/03 §1 point 6, §6.4).

---

## 2. Structure

1. Header (name and contact details)
2. Profile (2-3 lines)
3. **Languages** (near the top, because screening checks them first, research/03 §6.1)
4. Key skills or technical areas (one short line or a short list)
5. Work experience (reverse date order)
6. Education
7. Training and certificates
8. References: "Available on request", with referees ready

---

## 3. Length

- **Two pages, maximum** (house rule, proposal).
- New grads (segment A): one page until they have more than one year of relevant work (proposal, see `cv-variant-strategy.md` §4 A).
- Recent and relevant roles get 3-5 bullets. Older or unrelated roles get one line.

---

## 4. Must-have sections

| Section | What it must show | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Languages | Each language with an honest level | Checked first at NRC screening (research/03 §6.1). Language tests often happen inside the interview (research/03 §6.1) |
| Work experience | Technical area, partners, scale, results, donor reporting | Screening checks experience against minimum requirements (research/03 §2) |
| Results | Numbers that show efficiency and donor value | Organisations now work "with more complexity and fewer resources" and want proof of efficiency and donor value (research/01 §8.1 points 1 and 3) |
| Training | Sector courses and certificates, including the certificate plan from the gap analysis | Closes profile gaps (`program/6-week-programme.md` Week 0) |
| References | Referees lined up | Checks are standard, and MDS needs the candidate's consent (research/03 §6.4) |

---

## 5. Language and wording

- **Use the advert's words** for each requirement. Screening is a match against the advert (research/03 §2).
- **Programme language:** partners, communities, households, donors, reporting, technical area. Spell out acronyms once, unless the advert uses them.
- **Write in the language of the advert** (GIZ asks for this, research/03 §7; same rule in `program/6-week-programme.md`). A CV in a second language counts as one CV variant (`business/offer-and-pricing.md` §1).
- **Show regional strength.** Arabic, regional networks and knowledge of Gulf donors and reconstruction actors are a strength for MENA candidates (research/01 §8.1 point 8).

---

## 6. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Languages buried at the bottom | Move them under the profile |
| Duties copied from the job description of the old role | Results with numbers |
| Three or more pages | Cut older and unrelated roles to one line |
| A field the advert names is missing (for example M&E, safeguarding, a sector) | Add it if true, in the advert's words. If not true, it is a profile gap: note it for the certificate plan |
| Personal details the employer does not need (photo, marital status, religion) | Leave them off unless the advert asks. US embassies explicitly ask candidates to leave such details off (research/03 §5.1); it is a safe default |
| Referees not warned | Ask referees before the first application goes out |
| One CV for every NGO | Keep a technical-area variant per role family from the master CV |
| Too many acronyms and too much jargon | Spell out once. Readers include HR staff, not only specialists (NRC interview panels include HR, research/03 §6.1) |

## 7. Annotated skeleton

Curly braces `{ }` are fields to fill. Lines starting with `>>` are notes. Delete them before sending.

```
{FULL NAME}
{City, Country} · {phone} · {email} · {LinkedIn URL}
>> No photo, date of birth or marital status unless the advert asks.

PROFILE
{Role family} with {n} years in {technical area} across {countries or region},
working with {partners / donors / communities}. {One standout result}.
>> 2-3 lines. Mirror the job title in the advert.

LANGUAGES
{Arabic}: {native / fluent / professional} · {English}: {level} · {French}: {level}
>> Near the top. NRC screens languages first (research/03 §6.1).

KEY SKILLS
{Skill from advert} · {Skill from advert} · {Tool} · {Tool}
>> 4-8 items. Only skills the client can show in an interview.

WORK EXPERIENCE
{Job title} · {Organisation} · {Location} · {Month Year} to {Month Year}
- {Managed/Led/Coordinated} {what} {with partners} for {scale}, {result}.
- {Reported/Wrote} {what} to {donor}, {frequency}, {result if any}.
- {Efficiency result: time, cost or reach}.
>> 3-5 bullets for relevant roles, 1 line for others.

{Career transition entry, if any}
>> One neutral line. See cv-variant-strategy.md §4 D.

EDUCATION
{Degree}, {Field} · {University} · {Year}
>> New grads: put education above work experience.

TRAINING AND CERTIFICATES
{Course} · {Provider} · {Year} {or "in progress, due {Month Year}"}

REFERENCES
Available on request.
>> Referees confirmed before the first application (research/03 §6.4).
```
