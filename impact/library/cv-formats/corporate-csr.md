# Corporate CSR format: the ATS-friendly CV

**For:** you, Kristin. "You" is Kristin. "The client" is the person you coach. You can share section 7 (the skeleton) with a client.
**Status:** draft, 4 October 2026.
**Sources:** research/03 §8 and §10, research/01 §8.2. **The evidence on CSR hiring is thin.** CSR hiring has no shared standard, and most CSR claims in research/03 rest on secondary sources (research/03 §8, Limitations). CSR hiring in MENA was not measured (research/00 open question 5). Anything not in the research is marked **(general practice, not in the research)**.

## 1. Who this is for

Roles in CSR, ESG, sustainability, corporate foundations and impact teams inside companies.

| Client | Why they use this format |
|---|---|
| **C Shifters** (main users) | Their corporate background is an asset now. Commercial, finance and partnership skills are in demand (research/01 §8.2). The Week 2 swap for C asks for an ATS-friendly variant for CSR roles (`program/6-week-programme.md` Week 2). |
| **D Repositioning** | CSR is one possible second direction. Use one of the client's tier variants for a second direction such as CSR or consulting (`business/offer-and-pricing.md` §1). |
| **B Moving up** | Only for lateral moves toward banks or foundations (research/01 §8.2) that hire like companies. |

**Where these jobs appear:** Bayt and LinkedIn are more likely than humanitarian boards for corporate CSR roles in MENA **(unverified, research/03 §8)**. Climatebase lists climate and sustainability roles (research/03 §10).

**What the reader does with the CV** (research/03 §8, secondary sources):
- Normal corporate hiring: an applicant tracking system (ATS), a recruiter screen, a hiring-manager interview and a panel.
- Sustainability and ESG roles often add a **case study or presentation**, for example an emissions scope 1/2/3 problem, a materiality assessment or a CSR strategy pitch. The Week 5 swap for C prepares for this.

## 2. Structure

1. Header (name, city, contact, LinkedIn)
2. Headline: the target job title, in the advert's words
3. Profile (3 lines)
4. Core skills (keywords from the advert)
5. Work experience (reverse date order)
6. Certifications (for example the GRI Certified Sustainability Professional, research/03 §8)
7. Education
8. Languages
9. Volunteering, board seats and community work (this is the client's "mission credibility", research/01 §8.2; put it above education if it is strong)

**Making it readable by an ATS (general practice, not in the research):**
- One column. No tables, text boxes, icons or charts. Photos: none by default, except Gulf and Middle East corporate and CSR roles, where a professional headshot is permitted and often expected (place it in the body at the top of page 1, not in a header or text box).
- Standard headings: "Work experience", "Education", "Skills".
- Contact details in the body, not in the page header or footer.
- Each acronym written in full once, with the short form in brackets, for example "Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG)".
- The file type the advert asks for. If it says nothing, a Word file is the safer choice for an ATS.
- The same date format everywhere.

---

## 3. Length

- **Two pages, maximum** (house rule, proposal).
- **One page** for new grads until they have more than one year of relevant work (proposal, same as `cv-variant-strategy.md` §4 A).
- Recent, relevant roles: 3-5 bullets. Older or unrelated roles: one line.

---

## 4. Must-have sections

| Section | What it must show | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Headline and core skills | The advert's own job title and key terms | An ATS and a recruiter screen come first (research/03 §8, secondary sources). Matching terms is general practice |
| Results | Business-style results: cost, time, reach, revenue, partnerships, risk | Commercial and partnership skills are in demand (research/01 §8.2) |
| Sustainability or ESG knowledge | Only what is true: reporting, materiality, emissions, community programmes | These topics appear in CSR case studies (research/03 §8, secondary sources) |
| Certifications | Name, provider, year, or "in progress" | A certificate such as the GRI CSP helps positioning and closes a profile gap (research/03 §8) |
| Mission credibility | Volunteering, a board seat, a short certificate | Closes the "mission credibility" gap for shifters (research/01 §8.2) |
| Languages | Honest levels | You deliver documents in English, French and Arabic (`context/founder-profile.md`). List every working language the client can use at work |

## 5. Language and wording

**For shifters (C):** keep the corporate words. This is the one variant where they belong. The translation table in `cv-variant-strategy.md` §4 C is for the NGO, UN and donor variants, not this one.

**For sector people moving into CSR (B, D):** translate the other way. Check each swap against the real advert and use its words where they differ.

| Sector wording | Corporate CSR wording |
|---|---|
| Beneficiaries | People reached, communities |
| Donor reporting | Stakeholder reporting, impact reporting |
| Logframe, results framework | KPI framework, impact measurement framework |
| Monitoring and evaluation (M&E) | Impact measurement, performance tracking |
| Implementing partners | Delivery partners |
| Grant management | Budget and contract management |
| Proposal writing | Business cases, funding bids |
| Resource mobilisation | Partnerships and funding |
| Capacity building | Training, enablement |
| Field office, duty station | Location, site |

**Show numbers as business readers expect.** A drop from 10 days to 6 days can also be written as 40% faster. Both are the same fact (see the worked example in `cv-variant-strategy.md` §3). Never round a number up.

**Language of the CV:** follow the advert. GIZ asks candidates to apply in the language of the advert (research/03 §7), and the same rule is used across this programme (`program/6-week-programme.md`, "Languages").

## 6. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| A designed, two-column CV with icons | Use the one-column layout above (general practice) |
| Sector jargon an HR recruiter skips (logframe, "IP" for implementing partner, OCHA cluster) | Use the table in section 5. Spell out acronyms once |
| Corporate duties without impact | Add what changed for people or the planet, if true, next to the business result |
| Claiming ESG expertise from one course | Name the course and what the client did with it. Case studies test real knowledge (research/03 §8, secondary sources) |
| Hiding the corporate past (shifters) | Lead with it. It is the asset (research/01 §8.2) |
| Volunteering under "Interests" at the bottom | Move it up and give it full bullets (research/01 §8.2) |
| Applying only on humanitarian boards | Add Bayt and LinkedIn, and Climatebase for climate roles (research/03 §8, §10) |

## 7. Annotated skeleton

Curly braces `{ }` are fields to fill. Lines starting with `>>` are notes. Delete them before sending.

```
{FULL NAME}
{City, Country} · {phone} · {email} · {LinkedIn URL}
>> In the body of the page, not in the header.

{TARGET JOB TITLE FROM THE ADVERT}

PROFILE
{n} years in {function: finance / partnerships / operations / programmes} at
{type of organisation}, now focused on {CSR / ESG / sustainability area}.
{One measurable result}. {One line of mission credibility, if strong}.

CORE SKILLS
{Keyword from advert} · {Keyword from advert} · {Keyword} · {Tool} · {Tool}
>> 6-10 items. Each one must appear again in a bullet below.

WORK EXPERIENCE
{Job title} · {Company or organisation} · {City, Country} · {Month Year} to {Month Year}
- {Action verb} {what} {for or with whom} {scale}, {business result}.
- {Result in cost, time, reach or partnerships, with a number}.
- {Impact or sustainability result, if true}.
>> 3-5 bullets for relevant roles, 1 line for others.

{Career transition entry, if any}
>> One neutral line. See cv-variant-strategy.md §4 D.

CERTIFICATIONS
{Certificate} · {Provider} · {Year} {or "in progress, exam {Month Year}"}

VOLUNTEERING AND BOARD ROLES
{Role} · {Organisation} · {Month Year} to {Month Year}
- {What the client did and what changed}
>> Shifters: move this above education if it is strong.

EDUCATION
{Degree}, {Field} · {University} · {Year}

LANGUAGES
{Language}: {level} · {Language}: {level}
```
