## The fictional advert (short version)

**Programme Officer, Development Cooperation**, [EMBASSY-D], Beirut. Local hire. Ref. [JOB-REF-D].

[EMBASSY-D] is a fictional embassy that funds development projects in Lebanon through NGO partners. The advert asks for a CV and a cover letter that answers each selection criterion. Criteria are scored.

Minimum requirements: a bachelor's degree and at least 5 years of experience in development projects. The right to work in Lebanon.

Selection criteria:
1. Project cycle management, from design to final report.
2. Monitoring of implementing partners, including field visits and checks on results.
3. Grant and financial management.
4. Strong drafting in English.
5. Fluent English and Arabic. French is an asset.
6. Knowledge of the development context in Lebanon.

> [EMBASSY-D] funds local livelihoods and water projects through Lebanese partners, and asks each partner to report against a short set of shared results. I have spent six years producing exactly that kind of reporting for donors. I would now like to do the work from the donor side, where one programme officer can strengthen many partners at once.
>
> From 2019 to 2025 I worked at [INGO-D], first as M&E Officer and then as Project Manager on a livelihoods programme. My contract ended in [CONTRACT-END-MONTH] 2025, when the programme's US funding was stopped. Since then I have completed two short evaluation consultancies for local NGOs. The Programme Officer role uses everything I know about implementation, seen from the other side of the grant.
>
> I answer each selection criterion below.
>
> **1. Project cycle management.** I managed a three-year livelihoods project of USD 2.4 million, from the inception workshop to the final report. When funding stopped, I led an orderly close-out in six weeks, and the donor accepted all final reports.
>
> **2. Monitoring of implementing partners.** I supervised four local partner organisations. I ran quarterly field visits and spot checks, and introduced a one-page results checklist. Partner reports that needed major corrections fell from about half to one in five within a year.
>
> **3. Grant and financial management.** I managed four sub-grants with a combined value of USD 900,000. I reviewed partner budgets and monthly financial reports with our finance team, and closed all four sub-grants with no audit findings.
>
> **4. Drafting in English.** I wrote quarterly and annual donor reports and two successful concept notes. In my recent consultancies I wrote both evaluation reports in English, with summaries in Arabic.
>
> **5. Languages.** Arabic is my mother tongue. I work fully in English. My French is at a working level for reading and meetings.
>
> **6. Knowledge of the Lebanese context.** I have worked with municipalities and local NGOs in the Bekaa and the North. I followed how the funding cuts and the 2026 war changed what partners can deliver, and I adjusted our targets with the donor twice.
>

## Why this works

> **Each criterion answered, in order, with a number.** Donors and embassies score criteria one by one. EU Delegation criteria earn points (research/03 §4.3), and US embassies say an application that does not cover every requirement is not considered (research/03 §5.1). The numbered headings make scoring easy.
>
> **The gap in one neutral line.** The end of the contract is stated once, as a fact, with the reason. No apology, no blame. Then the letter moves on to what the candidate did next (programme Week 2, D swap).
>
> **A bridge story, not a lost title.** Project and M&E roles were among the hardest hit, and people from them need a bridge story (research/01 §8.1 point 4). Here the bridge is: "I produced what donors need; now I want to work on the donor side." That leads with donor value (research/01 §8.1 point 1).
>
> **Efficiency and short-term work count.** The six-week close-out shows doing more with less (research/01 §8.1 point 3). The two consultancies show the candidate kept working. Consulting is a bridge, not a failure (research/01 §8.2).
>
> **Right to work stated.** Embassies do not sponsor work permits, so this belongs in the last paragraph (research/03 §5.5).

## Make it yours

- Replace every fact with a true fact from your master CV (Week 2). Use your own words for the end of your last contract. Keep it to one line.
- If the advert sets a word limit or asks for a separate statement against the criteria, follow it. Keep paragraphs 1, 2 and the ask as a short covering note (framework §3).
- Embassy jobs come with language tests and security vetting (research/03 §5.5). Have referees and documents ready before you apply.
- Western embassies are also shrinking (research/01 §5), and how many local roles they open in Beirut is not known (research/00, open question 3). Apply to them, but do not apply only to them.
- If writing about the cuts still feels heavy, say so in your session. Week 1 and Week 6 make room for this.
