### What his "ideal CV" looks like (from the sample CV PDFs he attaches)
**Section order:**
1. **Header:** full name, a professional title line matched to the target role, city/country, phone, email, LinkedIn URL. Nationality is included on the UN CV.
2. **Professional Summary:** 4 to 5 sentences. Years of experience and sector, plus years inside the UN system. Current grade and scope (for example "division of 220 staff", "portfolio of 28 projects worth USD 85M"). 2 to 3 headline achievements with numbers, key skills, highest degree, languages with UN proficiency levels.
3. **Core Competencies:** a keyword block. In the newest sample it is split into **Technical** (keywords lifted from the vacancy) and **Leadership** (phrased in the agency's own competency-framework language, for example "Builds and maintains partnerships; Drives to achieve results for impact; Manages ambiguity and complexity").
4. **Professional Experience:** reverse chronological. Each role shows `Title (Grade) - Organisation, City, Country` and `Month Year - Month Year`. Current role gets 6 to 8 bullets, older roles 3 to 5, the oldest 3.
5. **Education:** degree, institution, year.
6. **Certifications:** with year (PMP, CIPS, SHRM, UN-specific certificates).
7. **Languages:** with level (Native / Fluent / Working knowledge; UN Level III/II).
8. **Technical Skills:** ERP, CRM, Power BI, Excel and so on.
9. "References available upon request" (in one sample).

Length is 2 to 3 pages for P4/P5. For D-level and above he says to condense the history: about 4 roles, not 20 bullets, focused on scale.

### His explicit ATS / CV rules (paraphrased checklist from the "How to make sure your CV is ATS-optimised" page and his posts)
- Most large employers, including UN agencies, screen with an ATS (he names Taleo, Inspira, Workday) before a human reads the CV.
- Mirror the vacancy's keywords, but only where they are true for you. Use the exact phrases (for example "IPSAS", "SAP", "voluntary contributions", "implementing partner"). Name the specific system, not just "an ERP".
- Use a single-column layout. No tables, text boxes, columns, icons, photos or graphics.
- Use standard headings: Professional Summary, Core Competencies, Professional Experience, Education, Languages.
- Use a standard font (Calibri or Arial, 10 to 11 pt). Submit Word or a text-based PDF, never a scan.
- Give title, employer, location and month/year dates for every role.
- Spell out acronyms once, then abbreviate, for example "Country Office (CO)".
- Prove results with numbers: budgets, %, team sizes, number of countries.
- Keep key details out of headers and footers.
- Make the minimum requirements obvious: education level, years of experience, languages. Put certifications and years of experience up front when they are mandatory (for example ACCA/CPA for finance).
- Make the online portal profile match the CV, because UN portals often screen the profile itself.
- Plain-text test: paste the CV into a .txt file. If it reads in a logical order, an ATS can likely parse it.
- Use a competency-based CV: structure achievements around the UN core and managerial competencies in the vacancy, not plain duties. Prove each competency with a measurable result. Map your experience to the tasks in the job description.
- Align LinkedIn (headline, About, experience, skills) with the CV and the target role.
- For a board or senior-governance application, use an Executive Bio instead of a CV: one page, third person, no bullets. Open with your current title, scope and impact. Give your career arc in 2 to 3 sentences, add 2 to 3 hard numbers, and close with credentials, board roles and languages.
- WHO Representative roster CVs should lead with country-level health diplomacy (Ministries of Health/Finance), UN Country Team and Resident Coordinator collaboration, resource mobilisation and budget stewardship, emergency response leadership, multi-country experience and people leadership.
- Executive-level CVs (USG/ED) should emphasise scope over tasks: budget, headcount and reach, resource mobilisation and funding diversification, humanitarian diplomacy, change leadership and governance/ethics.

### Interview rules (from his 9-page STAR guide and other posts)
- **UN interview format:** competency-based panels of 3 or more (hiring manager, subject expert, sometimes another unit). The same questions go to every candidate, each tied to a competency and scored against a rating guide. The usual sequence is screening → written test/assessment → interview → reference checks.
- **What gets assessed:** UN values (integrity, professionalism, respect for diversity), core competencies (communication, teamwork, planning & organising, accountability, creativity, client orientation, continuous learning, technological awareness), and managerial competencies at senior levels (leadership, vision, empowering others, building trust, managing performance, judgement & decision-making).
- **At P-4/P-5,** panels look for strategic impact, leadership of teams and partnerships, judgement under uncertainty and results at scale.
- **STAR weighting:** Situation 2 to 3 sentences; Task is your own responsibility; Action is about 60% of the answer and uses "I" not "we"; Result has numbers plus what you learned or changed.
- Build a story bank of 8 to 10 examples from the last 5 to 7 years, matched to the competencies in the vacancy. Keep answers to 2 to 3 minutes and practise aloud with a timer. Include a challenge or lesson, since honest reflection signals maturity.
- **Traps to avoid:** vague answers, hypothetical "I would..." answers, blaming others, results with no evidence. Never reuse a story that didn't happen to you.
- **Technical assessments are scenario-based** (for example "a vacancy unfilled for 4 months", "a donor project behind schedule", "audit finds unsupported expenditure"). They test approach, not definitions. Read the ToR, study the mandate and current programmes, use real examples and offer practical solutions.
- **Reference checks:** brief your referees on the role and remind them of shared achievements. Choose people who directly know your work, and never assume a reference will be positive.
- **Feedback example:** a finalist failed the P5 Judgement competency by not addressing donor and stakeholder communication in a disruption scenario. Also, a mentoring answer described delegation instead of capacity-building.
- **Salary question:** he has a guide on answering pay-expectation questions, benchmarked to the ICSC scale (P-1 to D-2).

### Takeaways
**(a) Rules for the AI job-search system**
- Default CV template = the Orestes section order above. Add a "Leadership competencies" sub-block written in the target organisation's own competency-framework wording (UN Values & Behaviours, UNICEF, WHO, NGO frameworks).
- Add an "ATS lint" step: single column, standard headings, no tables or graphics, dates as Month Year, acronyms expanded once, nothing in header/footer, a plain-text test, and keywords mirrored only when true.
- Add a "minimum requirements visible" check: degree level, years of experience, languages and mandatory certifications all appear in the summary.
- Bullet generator: each bullet = action verb + scope (USD, people, countries) + result (from → to, or %).
- Grade/level calibration: map the client to UN grades (P1-D2, NO-A-D, G-levels, NPSA, UNV, consultant) and the equivalent INGO band. Flag over- or under-targeting. Contract-type awareness: FT, TA, IC/consultant, roster.
- Executive Bio generator for board, roster and senior roles (the 5-step recipe above).
- Interview module: story bank of 8 to 10 STAR stories tagged by competency, roughly a 60% Action ratio, "I" language, numbers in the Result. Add a P4+ check that each story covers stakeholder/donor communication and capacity-building. Also include a technical-assessment scenario drill and a reference-briefing checklist.
- Job scoring: add a "portal profile matches CV" reminder for Inspira/Workday/Taleo, and record the closing date and job number for each role.

**(b) Ideas for the Way2Impact LinkedIn page**
- **"Sample CV for a live vacancy" series:** pick one real role a week (NGO, UN, foundation, CSR or social enterprise), build a fictional-candidate CV PDF, and add a final page with ATS tips. Use a different sector each week to reach all 4 segments.
- **"Role of the Week" breakdown:** what the role screens for, the competencies to prove, one CV tip and one LinkedIn tip.
- **High-value PDF guides,** his top performers: salary and pay-scale explainers (UN/INGO/foundation pay), an interview question bank with model answers, a grade-level guide.
- An insider-voice opinion post now and then (for example how recruiters should give feedback).

### What to avoid or improve
- Don't republish identical posts 5 to 9 times; engagement collapses on the copies. Post once and repurpose later in a new format.
- Leave comments on. He turns them off, which kills reach and conversation.
- Use a branded design. His PDFs are plain Word-style documents. Way2Impact can use the same content value with brand-consistent covers.
- His audience is UN-heavy and East Africa-focused, and his off-topic political posts dilute the brand. Way2Impact should stay focused and cover NGOs, CSR and social enterprises, not only the UN.
- Use a booking link or lead magnet instead of a Gmail address as the CTA.

## 2. How To Find A Job (company page)

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/company/how-to-find-a-job/ (posts: /posts/)
- **Who they are:** a "Business Content" page with 2 to 10 employees and about 239K followers. Tagline: "Your Guide to Landing the Right Job, Faster." Generic job-search content (resumes, interviews, salary negotiation, remote jobs, AI tools), mostly US/tech-oriented.
- **Posting rhythm:** very high, about 2 to 3 posts a day (86 posts in about 5 to 6 weeks).

### Core model: curation ("Brilliant Share")
Nearly every post repackages another creator's post:
1. The page copies the creator's 1 to 2 line hook as its opener.
2. "Brilliant Share By: [Creator Name]" (tagged)
3. "Original Post Below 👇 👇 👇"
4. Full original text
5. A fixed footer: "------ / DM us to be featured on our next post / [tagline] / Follow How To Find A Job for more content like this."

The image or carousel is the creator's own visual. So the design is inconsistent from post to post: one is a green spreadsheet-style table of startup job boards, the next a colourful infographic ("Your Resume Is Not a Job History"), the next a blurry screenshot of an email template. There is no brand system, which explains the coach's sense that the design "could be nicer". Brand identity comes only from the text footer and the logo avatar.

**Growth mechanism:** tagging the creator gives them a reason to engage and reshare. The "DM us to be featured" line turns creators into a supply chain, and 2 to 3 posts a day keeps the page in feeds.

### Formats (of 86)
About 65 image posts (mostly single images), about 12 short videos, and about 8 PDF carousels. There are almost no pure-text posts.

### Takeaways
**(a) Rules for the AI job-search system** (generic, adapt to the sector)
- Interview prep should cover the classic questions they keep recycling: tell me about yourself (a 30-second structure), why are you leaving, why should we hire you, salary on the first call, questions for the end of the interview. Avoid "it depends", "what's the culture like?" and vague answers.
- Job-search strategy: target rather than volume, apply early after a posting goes live, contact real hiring people, send letters of interest to organisations with no current opening, know your financial runway and salary floor.
- Resume: check "every line passes 3 questions" (does it show you're qualified, show scope or impact, could someone verify it); use a "PCAR" bullet (Problem, Constraint, Action, Result); a resume self-check score.

**(b) Ideas for the Way2Impact LinkedIn page**
- **"Impact Voices" curation series:** feature social-impact recruiters, hiring managers and coaches (with permission and a tag), always inside a Way2Impact branded template. This gives you their reach without their messy design.
- **Weekly empathy post** for each segment. Examples: a "behind every application" style post about people laid off by funding cuts; career shifters from corporate; new graduates competing for unpaid internships.
- **Hiring-philosophy debates** suited to the sector: "values alignment vs technical skills", "is a master's degree really required?", "national vs international staff". These drive comments.
- **"Hidden job boards for impact careers" lists** (ReliefWeb, Devex, Idealist, Impactpool, UNjobs, Bond, Work for Good, ESG and CSR boards, B Corp job boards). These are highly saveable.
- **Common interview questions, sector edition:** "Why do you want to work for an NGO after corporate?", "How do you handle donor reporting pressure?", "Tell me about working with communities".
- **Footer CTA idea:** a fixed footer line inviting impact professionals to share their story or get featured, which builds both community and a content pipeline.

### What to avoid or improve
- Don't let other people's visuals define the brand. Use one consistent template: brand colours, cover style and logo placement.
- Their content is US/tech-centric with salary figures in dollars. Way2Impact must localise: global South roles, UN grades, NGO pay realities.
- At 2 to 3 posts a day with no original insight, there's no authority. Way2Impact should mix curation with original expertise (sample CVs, sector data).
- Some posts slide into off-topic founder stories or app promotion, which gets low engagement.

## 3. Raise for Good (company page)

### Weekly Social Impact Jobs Roundup: exact anatomy
1. **Opening line (rotates):**
   - "✨ Check out our latest list of #socialimpactjob openings!" (the default)
   - "✨ Social Impact Jobs Roundup" as a title line

4. **Roles grouped by career level** in capitals: **SENIOR/EXECUTIVE**, **MID-CAREER**, **EARLY CAREER**.
5. **Fields per job** (one line each):
   `Job Title` + `Organisation` + `(Work mode - City, State/Country)` + `| $salary range` (or hourly rate, or EUR) + `[Hiring manager or recruiter name, tagged]` when known.
   For example: Director-level title / foundation name / (Hybrid - New York, NY) / $180,000-$200,000 / [tagged hiring contact].
   Work mode is always shown (Remote / Hybrid / On Site). The salary range is shown whenever it's public.
6. **Number of roles in the post:** about 5 to 20, usually 8 to 13. The full list on their site has 50+ roles. The image says "Featuring 50+ roles across 3 career levels".
7. **Hashtags:** always #SocialImpactJobs #Philanthropy #Hiring.
8. **Visual:** in the earlier months, a branded card. Lavender background, big black stacked "RAISE FOR GOOD" logotype, deep-indigo rounded panel, small yellow "SOCIAL IMPACT" kicker, white bold "JOBS ROUNDUP", "WEEK OF [MM.DD.YY]", a hand-drawn arrow and the italic line "Featuring 50+ roles across 3 career levels". Later roundups are text-only. One was a short video version and got their best roundup engagement.

Each line shows funder + programme + (amount range), then "Deadline: [date / Rolling]" + link. It uses 10 hashtags and has a branded image card.

### CV and job-search rules she teaches (paraphrased)
- **Remove from senior CVs:** objective statements ("seeking a challenging position..."); routine duty lists (attended meetings, prepared reports); detail on jobs from 15 to 20 years ago; achievements without numbers; empty adjectives (dynamic, visionary, results-oriented); every workshop attended; operational and technical tasks that make you look junior; repeated achievements across employers; personal data (marital status, religion, children, ID numbers); a profile that describes your current job rather than the next level.
- **Weak verbs to limit:** coordinated, assisted, supported, participated. Prefer led, influenced, transformed, delivered.
- **A Director CV must show 7 things:** strategic leadership, financial leadership (budgets managed or mobilised), people leadership (staff and teams), organisational impact, stakeholder influence (boards, governments, donors), regional or multi-country scope, and decision-making authority.
- **10 achievement categories for a Director CV:** organisational growth, budget management, team leadership, strategic planning, revenue and resource mobilisation, organisational transformation, stakeholder engagement, regional or multi-country leadership, risk/governance/compliance, and measurable programme results.
- **The summary must state** what you lead, at what scale and what value you bring. It is not "hardworking, results-oriented...".
- **Show career progression;** older roles must earn their space.
- **Target list method:** 6 categories (UN and multilaterals, INGOs, development banks, foundations, consulting firms, regional institutions) × 6 organisations = 30. Study priorities, presence, programmes and partnerships. Identify the target department and its leaders, monitor careers pages and alerts, and keep a spreadsheet. Then tier the 30 by priority and find 3 key people in each organisation.
- **Recruiter outreach:** state the exact target role, your value in numbers (budget, team, geography), your sector expertise and your geographic preferences, and write an intentional introduction rather than "let me know if anything comes up".
- **In the funding crisis:** don't apply to everything you are minimally qualified for. Target organisations that are still investing. Widen to consultancies, interim and advisory roles. Build LinkedIn visibility before vacancies appear. Use Nov-Dec for repositioning.

### Takeaways
**(a) Rules for the AI job-search system**
- Add a "level-up audit" for clients moving up: flag weak verbs, duty-only bullets, missing scale numbers, an objective statement, old-role bloat, training lists and personal data. Score the CV against the 7 Director signals and 10 achievement categories.
- Add a target-organisation planner: a 6 × 6 matrix of organisation types adapted to Way2Impact's sectors (UN, INGO, local/national NGO, donor/foundation, CSR/ESG, social enterprise), priority tiers and 3 contacts each.
- Add a recruiter and hiring-manager outreach template generator (role, value, sector, geography).
- Repositioning playbook for people hit by funding cuts: consultancies, interim, advisory, roster and pipeline roles, plus targeting organisations with growing funding.

**(b) Ideas for the Way2Impact LinkedIn page**
- Use her best formats: "If I were [segment] targeting [role] in [year], here's what I'd do" and numbered "N things your CV must show" lists. Rotate them across the 4 segments: graduates ("If I were a new graduate targeting my first NGO role..."), moving up, corporate switchers ("If I were moving from corporate to CSR or an NGO..."), and funding-cut repositioning.
- **"Target list" series** across a week: build your 30 organisations, tier them, find your 3 people, track them.
- **Weak vs strong line rewrites** for each segment.
- A sector-moment hook (the funding crisis, year-end planning) as a seasonal anchor.
- Personal proof posts (the coach's own track record), sparingly.

### What to avoid or improve
- Avoid ALL-CAPS hard-sell posts. They consistently get 1 to 6 reactions.
- Don't post 5 to 7 times a day. Fewer, stronger posts (for example 3 to 5 a week on the company page) avoid cannibalising reach.
- Add visuals. Her posts are almost all plain text. Way2Impact can turn her strongest list formats into branded carousels.
- She is narrowly focused on executives. Way2Impact serves 4 segments, so use segment-specific hooks and avoid one-size messaging.
- Use a proper booking or lead page, not a Gmail address with a subject line.
