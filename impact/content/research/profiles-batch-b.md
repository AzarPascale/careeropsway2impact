## 1. Climate and Remote Opportunities (company page)

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/company/climate-and-remote-opportunities/ (posts: /posts/)
- **Who:** A "Staffing and Recruiting" page based in Harare with about 18.5K followers and 2 to 10 employees. Its tagline promises verified climate and remote jobs worldwide. It appears linked to a sister page, "Remote Jobs Opportunities" (RJO, remotejobsopportunities.com), which runs a longer, more structured job format (see note at the end of this section).
- **Scope in practice:** Broader than climate. Only about half the posts are climate or sustainability jobs. The rest are NGO jobs (human rights, health, gender), UN internships (UNDP, UNHCR), fellowships, scholarships (Mastercard Foundation, Cambridge, CMU Africa), grants for NGOs and youth groups, and some generic remote gigs. The audience leans Africa and Global South, early to mid career.

### Posting rhythm
- About **7 to 9 posts a day**, every day (48 posts in 6 days).
- Each post covers **one opportunity**. There are no roundups. The page reposts the same vacancy a few days later (for example, the Global Methane Hub role appeared on day 1 and again on day 4).

### Format: one post per opportunity, always the same template
1. **Headline line:** `[Org] is Hiring: [Role] | Fully Remote` or `[Org] Is Offering a Paid [X] Internship!` or `[Program] 2027 - Up to $X!`. The money or remote status goes in the headline and it ends with an exclamation mark.
2. **One-sentence context:** what the org does or what the role covers.
3. **"Learn more and apply:" + short link** (lnkd.in), placed **high up**, before the bullets.
4. **4 to 6 emoji-labelled fact bullets**, with a consistent emoji for each field:
   - 💻 / 📍 / 🏡 Work arrangement or location (Fully remote, Home-based, Remote - MENA, Hybrid)
   - 💰 Salary, stipend or award (exact ranges when public, "Not disclosed" when not)
   - 📋 / 💼 Contract type and length (Full-time, 12-month, part-time 20 hrs/week, independent contractor)
   - 🎯 / 🎓 Experience required (for example "5+ years in grant management")
   - ✈️ Travel percentage
   - 📅 / ⏳ Deadline, sometimes with a time zone ("30 Nov 2026, 9:00 CET"), or "Rolling" or "Not specified"
   - 🚀 Start date
   - 🗣️ Languages
   - 🔹 / ✅ Generic benefit bullets (certificate, mentorship, 20 days' leave)
5. **Multi-vacancy variant:** `[Org] is Hiring - 4 Remote Opportunities!` followed by a list of role titles (📌 or 🔹) and one link to the careers page.
6. **3 to 5 hashtags:** #RemoteJobs #ClimateJobs #NGOJobs plus role and region tags (#AfricaJobs, #MENAJobs).

### Visuals
- Each post has one square or portrait image. Two types:
  - A **branded "WE ARE HIRING" card**: the org's logo at the top, a big bold headline (for example "Paid Climate Internship"), a row of 3 icons with short labels (Remote/Office-based, Monthly stipend, 6 months), a coloured deadline bar and an "Apply Now" button graphic. The design is simple and template-made, readable on a phone, and repeats the caption facts.
  - A **screenshot of the org's own careers page** (for example the IISD "Current Openings" list).
- No carousels, videos or documents.

### Engagement signals (low overall for an 18K page)
Most posts get 1 to 15 reactions. The top posts in the sample:
- Remote research internship (SDG policy institute, certificate plus recommendation letters): ~43 reactions, 5 comments
- UNDP remote internship with a $500 stipend: ~27
- GIS instructor role, fully remote, "no teaching experience needed": ~25 / 3
- Climate Collective comms associate role: ~24 / 3
- Low-paid remote VA/AI ops roles: ~24
- Village Enterprise Chief of Staff (remote, flexible/global): ~18
- Preferred by Nature sustainability specialist: ~15

**Pattern:** posts that are low-barrier, remote, paid and open to beginners (internships, "no experience needed") get the most reactions. Senior roles and fellowships get very little.

### Takeaways
**(a) Rules for the AI job-search system**
- Extract a fixed field set from every vacancy: org, role, work arrangement (remote, home-based, hybrid, on-site plus region restriction), salary or stipend (or "not disclosed"), contract type and length, experience years, languages, travel %, deadline with time zone, start date, eligibility (nationality, region, age band such as 18-35, "African youth-led").
- Flag "remote" claims that carry geographic limits (for example "Remote - MENA" or "United States (Remote)"). Global clients often miss this.
- Treat "rolling / until filled" as urgent: apply early.
- Watch for employer notes that **AI-generated CVs or cover letters are not accepted** (CIVICUS put this in its posting). The system must flag such vacancies and switch to "coach, don't ghost-write" mode.
- Separate out non-job opportunities (fellowships, scholarships, grants, accelerators) so they don't distort job scoring.
- Filter out low-quality listings: very low pay (for example $250-450/month), "closes in 48 hours" pressure, vague firms. A curated system should score these down.

**(b) Ideas for the Way2Impact LinkedIn page**
- Copy the **scannable emoji fact block**, with the same emoji for the same field every time. It is fast to read and easy to template.
- Put the link high and repeat the key facts (remote / pay / deadline) in the image.
- Use the **"[Org] is hiring - N opportunities"** cluster post when one organisation has several openings. It is cheap content and gets saved.
- Make **one** branded visual template (logo slot, role, 3 icon facts, deadline bar). Way2Impact can do this better: use its brand colours instead of a generic blue and green, and leave out the stock "WE ARE HIRING" look.
- Opportunity types that perform best here (paid internships, remote entry roles) match Way2Impact's **new graduates** segment.

### What to avoid / improve
- Volume over curation: 8 posts a day, reposted duplicates and off-topic gigs weaken trust in the "verified" promise. Way2Impact should post fewer, vetted jobs.
- No point of view. The page never says who a role suits or how to stand out, so there is no coaching value and no reason to follow a person or brand. **This is the gap Way2Impact can fill.**
- Captions often get cut off early. Some salary data is wrong (one listing gave "USD 57-78 yearly"). Check figures before posting.
- Weekly roundups are missing entirely. A Way2Impact weekly roundup would be a different format from this page.

**Sector-specific note:** fields that matter in impact jobs and not in corporate ones: stipend versus salary, consultancy versus staff, "renewable subject to funding", home-based UN contracts, eligibility by nationality or region, and grants or fellowships alongside jobs.

**Sister page (RJO) format, for reference:** "📢 [Org] Seeks [Role] (Remote) - Apply Now", then an Org line with a one-line description, then Position / Type / Experience / Salary / Posted date, then Responsibilities (bullets), then Requirements (bullets), then the link and about 10 hashtags. It is a fuller "mini job description" format and fits a single featured job better than a feed.

## 2. Nelly Kerubo (person)

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/in/nelly-kerubo-28661515a/ (activity: /recent-activity/all/)
- **Who:** Nairobi-based "International Development Career Strategist", focused on executive positioning for NGO, INGO and UN professionals and on leadership CVs. Her business is **Elite Resume and Career Solutions** (#EliteResume). Her audience is mid to senior development professionals, many of them in East Africa, with a global reach.

### Posting rhythm
- About **2 to 3 posts a day**, nearly every day (about 65 posts in about 4 weeks). Almost everything she posts is original. She rarely reposts.

### Recurring themes
- Nearly every post is a **"vacancy analysis"**: she takes a live job (UN P-2 to P-5 and D-level, INGO country director, foundation programme officer, Red Cross and Red Crescent, MSF, IRC, DRC, Gates, Ford, GCF, UNOPS, UN-Habitat and others) and explains **what the role is really about** and **what the CV must prove**.
- Main message: **read the mandate behind the title**. A senior title doesn't make you a fit, and the CV must show what you influenced, strengthened and changed, not only what you managed.
- Function-specific CV lessons for finance, grants, supply chain, MEAL, HR, comms, policy and advocacy, country leadership, research and executive assistant roles.
- An occasional explainer, for example "How to transition into international development" in 6 steps. These are aimed at career shifters.
- A few corporate vacancy analyses (Maersk, Ericsson, steel manufacturing) use the same lens.

### Post structure (very consistent, 2,000-3,200 characters)
1. **Hook**, which is one of these:
   - A contrarian reframe: "A [Role] is not simply [obvious duty]. It is [bigger mandate]." (her most common opener, used in dozens of posts)
   - A question: "What does it take to lead [X] in one of the world's most complex environments?"
   - An all-caps banner: "UN WOMEN IS RECRUITING: DEPUTY DIRECTOR OF ADVOCACY - P5, NEW YORK" or "VACANCY ANALYSIS: …" or "NEW OPPORTUNITY | …"
   - An audience call-out: "MEAL professionals: [Org] is recruiting in Syria."
2. Who is recruiting, the location and the grade.
3. **"Understanding the mandate"**: what the org does and why the role exists.
4. **Key responsibilities**: 4 to 7 bullets, paraphrased from the job description.
5. **"Who should consider applying"**: the essential requirements.
6. **"What makes this role demanding"** or **"A career-positioning lesson"**: where she adds her own value. For example, a strong specialist is not automatically a strong candidate.
7. **"The CV must make X things visible"**: a numbered list (scale of portfolio, outcomes, partnerships, complexity, influence beyond the org).
8. Closing line, often a short slogan about making impact measurable or showing value rather than duties.
9. Soft CTA: "At Elite Resume and Career Solutions, we help…", then the job link, then 6 to 8 hashtags (#CareerStrategy #InternationalDevelopment #UNJobs #HumanitarianCareers).

### Visuals
- Weak. Most posts have **no designed visual**, only the automatic link-preview card from the job site or LinkedIn's "View job" card. Formatting is plain text with section labels. Some posts are mostly in all caps.

### Engagement signals
Results are very uneven: many posts get 1 to 10 reactions, and some take off. Top posts:
- Ford Foundation Program Director, Gender Equality: ~197 reactions, 4 comments, 69 reposts
- East Africa Programme Officer, migration and human rights advocacy org: ~166 / 11 / 39
- IRC Senior Supply Chain Officer: ~112 / 13 / 18
- Gates Foundation AI Fellows, Africa: ~103 / 3 / 32
- Equality Fund Coordinator, Global Programs: ~99 / 3 / 16
- Sightsavers Country Director Kenya: ~92 / 6 / 13. CBM Country Director: ~91 / 4 / 15
- German Red Cross Head of Office: ~80. IRC Policy role: ~74. Head of Programme Afghanistan: ~66. UN P-2 Programme Management Officer: ~62. Plan WASH PM: ~59. PxD Country Director Kenya: ~57
- Low: senior UN D-level and very niche roles (1 to 5 reactions)

**Pattern:** well-known employers (Ford, Gates, IRC, Sightsavers) plus roles that many people could plausibly target get reposted heavily, because people repost jobs to their networks. Repost counts are high relative to comments. The posts get shared because they are useful.

### Takeaways
**(a) Rules for the AI job-search system** (her advice, written as rules)
1. **Read the mandate, not the title.** Before scoring fit, summarise what the organisation needs this role to achieve: strategic, operational or technical; specialist or generalist.
2. **Check essential requirements first.** Hard eligibility comes before anything else: degree route versus years (for example UN: 7 years with a Master's, 9 with a Bachelor's), specialist certifications, languages, nationality or "national position". A well-written CV cannot replace a missing essential qualification, so the system should say "not eligible" honestly.
3. **Match the level.** At senior levels, score evidence of influence, strategy, funding scale and institutional change, not years or titles. Flag mismatches where a senior generalist applies to a narrow technical post.
4. **Rewrite responsibilities as evidence.** Turn duty lines ("provided administrative support", "responsible for procurement", "managed social media") into scale + complexity + decisions + result.
5. **Use function-specific CV checklists** (from her posts):
   - Finance and grants: size of funds, controls strengthened, risks addressed, reliability of donor reporting, grant governance (more than administration).
   - Supply chain: scale of procurement, complexity of decisions, controls applied, operational or financial results.
   - MEAL: data quality improved, accountability strengthened, gaps identified, decisions influenced (more than reports produced).
   - Comms: scale of audiences, institutional outcomes, visual or strategic contribution.
   - Country Director: institutional leadership (strategy, people, governance, resources, performance), not project delivery.
   - Foundation Program Officer: change made possible, not grant administration.
   - Policy and advocacy: how evidence moved decision-makers.
   - Early career: show problems solved and initiatives led, including in internships and university projects. Qualifications alone are not enough.
6. **Targeted over volume.** Her rule: 10 well-targeted applications beat 50 generic ones. The system should push clients to apply to fewer, better-fitting roles.
7. **Career-shifter translation** (her 6-step model): find where your function meets the sector's needs, learn the sector's vocabulary, target bridge roles that value your existing expertise, reposition the CV in sector language, build credibility (read org strategies, attend events, take short courses), then apply with a targeted strategy.
8. **AI-use restrictions:** some employers (for example a refugee-led research hub) restrict generative AI in application answers. The system must detect this in the job description and change how it helps.
9. **Pre-empt the panel.** Use the job description to list the questions the interview panel will ask, and make sure the CV answers them first. This doubles as interview preparation.

**(b) Ideas for the Way2Impact LinkedIn page**
- **"Job of the week, decoded"**: one notable vacancy, a 3-line mandate summary, who it suits, then "your CV must show these 3 things". This is Nelly's format made shorter and better designed.
- Turn her repeated contrarian line ("X is not just… It is…") into a **carousel or single graphic hook**.
- Make **function-specific CV checklists** (finance, MEAL, comms, programme) into saveable carousels.
- Run a **"read beyond the title"** series showing two similar titles with different mandates.
- Write a career-shifter explainer such as "Your corporate experience counts more than you think" for the **corporate shifter** segment.
- Pick jobs with wide appeal and recognisable employers, since those get reposted.

### What to avoid / improve
- Posts are too long and repetitive: 3,000 characters and the same skeleton 2 to 3 times a day. Way2Impact should cut to about 1,200 characters and put the details in a carousel.
- No visuals. Way2Impact can add branded carousels or document posts.
- The constant CTA ("At Elite Resume…") reads like an ad. Keep CTAs light and varied.
- The all-caps hooks look shouty.
- Her content is heavily senior and East Africa focused. Way2Impact covers 4 segments worldwide, so rotate levels (graduate, mid-level, shifter, post-cut repositioning).
- The sector expertise is excellent: the grading system (P/D, LICA, IPSA, ICS), Red Cross Movement structure, localisation, donor compliance. Keep this depth.

## 3. Jesús Baena (person)

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/in/jbaenanet/ (activity: /recent-activity/all/)
- **Who:** Spain-based "Humanitarian AI & Data Consultant" with about 15 years in humanitarian field operations and coordination, much of it in Ukraine. He recently opened his practice, **baena.ai**, offering practical AI and data tools for NGOs, the UN and donors. He has about 2.2K followers. He builds and publishes a **live ReliefWeb humanitarian jobs-market dashboard** and a free humanitarian RAG assistant (chat.baena.ai).
- **Note:** his AI content is about **humanitarian operations** (data protection, RAG assistants, dashboards), **not AI for individual job seekers**. His job-market content is **labour-market data**, not application tips. Both are still very useful to the coach.

### Posting rhythm
- Low: about **2 to 4 original posts a month** lately, plus reposts. The gaps between posts are irregular. Recent reposts are mostly of **Chahrazed Anane** (ex-ICRC career coach for aid workers), who builds content on his data. They are a data-plus-coach collaboration pair.

### Recurring themes
1. **The humanitarian job market contracting** (ReliefWeb tracker, about 47K postings since July 2024, each read and classified by an LLM):
   - Monthly postings peaked around 3,300 (Oct 2024) and fell to about 1,400 (late 2026), roughly 57% below the peak.
   - Organisations posting each month fell from about 510 to about 290.
   - US donors were named in 18% of job descriptions in H2 2024 and about 5% by early 2026.
   - Consultancies held steady at about 1 in 5 postings while staff jobs shrank. He calls this a shift to a "gig-based" humanitarian workforce.
   - Location split: about 39-42% in capital-city country offices, 25-29% in field offices, about 25% at HQ or regional level. The field share rose after the funding freeze, meaning cuts hit higher levels first.
   - 84% of field-office jobs are tied to grants, against 49% at HQ.
   - Hiring geography shifted: Syria is now the busiest country, Venezuela more than doubled, US-based postings halved.
   - Jan-Sep 2026 postings were about 21% below 2025 (from Chahrazed's post using his data).
2. **Funding analysis** (country-based pooled funds, Ukraine Humanitarian Fund): bigger, multi-sector partners capture most funding, and localisation is shaping who gets funded.
3. **Responsible AI for NGOs:** Copilot and Gemini surface files that staff technically have permission to open, so run a permissions audit first. Protection and case data should not sit in SharePoint or OneDrive. Data residency is not data sovereignty (the US CLOUD Act). Redact PII before text reaches an external model. Low-resource languages (Hausa, Bangla, Swahili) break the standard redaction tools.
4. **Building in public:** RAG assistant upgrades, multilingual support, a sitrep chat for Venezuela and Colombia, an Ukraine legal assistant, settlement-level maps. Every post includes an honest "this is a demo, verify before acting" caveat.

### Formats and hooks
- **Data-insight posts:** a one-line stat hook (for example "for every field job advertised, one and a half are in a capital"), then 3 to 5 emoji-led stat bullets (📉 🏢 💵 🌍 📝), then an honest limitation ("ReliefWeb isn't the whole market"), then a link to the dashboard, then a closing question for the audience ("What would you want tracked next?" or "Field colleagues: how many posts in your last team were advertised internationally?").
- **Build logs:** a bold Unicode headline, what changed, what they learned (often a counter-intuitive test result), the caveat, then the link.
- **Contrarian ops posts:** "Copilot just made your data protection problem searchable."
- He uses bold Unicode text heavily and a few hashtags.
- Visuals: dashboard screenshots, charts and occasional link cards.

### Engagement signals
- His own top posts: the ReliefWeb "47,000 postings" update (~64 reactions, 11 comments), the UHF partner analysis chart (~57), the "not correcting, contracting" post (~53 / 8), the Ukrainian-language local-NGO offer (~41 / 5), the gig-workforce shift (~40), the new chat interface (~32). Most other posts get under 20.
- Job-market data gets the most engagement and is the topic most reshared by career coaches.

### Takeaways
**(a) Rules for the AI job-search system**
- **Use LLM-based job-description classification, as in his tracker.** For each vacancy, extract: duty station type (HQ / regional / capital country office / field base / home-based), **whether the post is tied to a grant or project** (a proxy for job security), contract type (staff / consultancy / temporary), donor named in the job description, experience level, country. These make good **scoring fields**.
- **Scoring heuristics drawn from his data:**
  - Grant-tied and field posts are less stable. Show a "contract-security" note instead of a penalty.
  - Consultancies make up about 20% of the market and are holding steady. For clients after funding cuts, include consultancies in searches by default.
  - Capital and HQ roles are more competitive and were cut first. Field roles are recovering slightly.
  - Mention of US donors in a job description is a risk signal for funding stability.
  - Where hiring is growing (Syria, Venezuela, pooled-fund countries) and where it is shrinking (US-based roles) should inform target lists.
- **Search strategy rule:** don't rely on one source (ReliefWeb is only part of the market). Combine organisations' own career portals, LinkedIn and sector boards.
- **Market-reality guidance** (from Chahrazed's posts using his data): the market is not frozen, it is more selective. Waiting for "after summer" or "when funding returns" is a losing strategy. Review target-organisation lists against who is hiring now.
- **Data protection rules for the system (important):**
  - Never send client PII (name, phone, ID, references' contact details) to an external model unredacted. Use placeholders and restore the values locally.
  - Ground outputs in cited sources (the vacancy text, the client's own CV). Show where each claim came from.
  - Add a "reading aid, verify before acting" caveat to any market data or AI summaries.
  - Keep client documents in storage the coach controls.
- **Tools he names:** RAG over curated public guidance (Sphere, IASC, ECHO rules, ALNAP), the ACAPS and FEWS NET APIs, Gemini with a self-hosted Qdrant vector DB, n8n for workflows, Microsoft Presidio for PII redaction (weak for some languages). Useful if the coach wants a sector knowledge base behind the system, for example UN competency frameworks, grade guides and donor glossaries.

**(b) Ideas for the Way2Impact LinkedIn page**
- A **monthly "Impact job market pulse"** post: 3 to 5 stats (cite Jesús's public dashboard with credit and tag him), each with "what it means for your search". This works especially well for the **repositioning after funding cuts** segment.
- Partnership potential: he openly shares data with coaches (the Chahrazed model). A data-plus-coach collaboration or co-post could work.
- End posts with a question to the audience. It is his best comment driver.
- Include an honest limitation line ("this dataset misses locally recruited roles"). It builds credibility.
- A "how I use AI safely in your job search" post (anonymising CVs, never pasting references' details) shows the coach's AI integration responsibly.

### What to avoid / improve
- Too technical for job seekers (MRR scores, vector DBs). Translate it into career implications.
- Bold Unicode text hurts accessibility and screen readers. Don't use it.
- Irregular cadence and some multilingual posts that confuse the feed.
- **Sector specificity:** localisation, pooled funds, the US funding freeze, consultancies replacing staff roles, and capital versus field postings are all specific to humanitarian work and directly relevant to Way2Impact's "funding cuts" segment.

## 4. Ma'in Bataineh (person)

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/in/ma-in-bataineh-a7a81b151/ (activity: /recent-activity/all/)
- **Who:** Amman-based Regional HR Manager (talent acquisition, onboarding, emergency response) with about 15.8K followers. He runs or promotes **zaytoonz.com**, a jobs channel with WhatsApp and social media. His job roundups focus on **Jordan and the MENA region** (NGOs, INGOs, UN, embassies, local foundations).

### Posting rhythm
- About **4 to 6 roundup posts a week** (about 24 roundups in about 5 weeks), each with **12 to 15 vacancies**. He also reposts recruiters' "We're hiring" posts (HR roles in Jordan) and occasionally makes an image-only post.

### Format: plain-text multi-job roundup
- **No hook, no intro and no headline.** The post opens straight into the list.
- Each job is a 4-line block, separated by a dashed rule:
  ```
  *Role Title*
  Entity: [Org acronym]
  Location: [Country / Home-based / Multiple]
  [short link]
  -----------
  ```
- Roles are often **grouped by employer** (several SCI, IRC, MSF or SOS roles in a row). Some roundups are **themed**: a UN-only roundup (OCHA, UNEP, UNRWA, OHCHR), a UNICEF-only roundup (mostly Palestine/Gaza), and a home-based UN consultancies roundup.
- Arabic-titled roles are mixed in for local positions.
- **Fixed sign-off:** "Follow us on WhatsApp & Social Media: zaytoonz.com/". Every post funnels to his own channel.
- No hashtags. The automatic link-preview card shows the first job's site.
- **Fields per job:** only title, entity and location. No salary, deadline, level, contract type or remote detail.

### Engagement signals
- Roundups are **very low**: 0 to 3 reactions each. Followers probably read them without reacting, or use the WhatsApp channel instead.
- One image-only post (content didn't load) got ~112 reactions and 5 comments, far more than the text roundups.
- Recruiters' hiring posts he reposts get 10 to 45 reactions.

### Takeaways
**(a) Rules for the AI job-search system**
- His roundups are a **source list** for Jordan and MENA. The system's job feed could watch the same employers: IRC, SCI, MSF, NRC, DRC, GIZ, Expertise France, JRF, KHCF, SOS, IMC, ACF, REACH, UNRWA, UNICEF, WFP, ILO, UNDP, embassies.
- Normalise acronyms (SCI = Save the Children International, EF = Expertise France, JRF = Jordan River Foundation, KHCF/KHCC = King Hussein Cancer Foundation/Center, WHH = Welthungerhilfe, HI = Humanity & Inclusion, WCA = War Child Alliance) so clients know who the employer is.
- Detect "national position" or local-only roles and Arabic-language postings. These are relevant for local clients and ineligible for international ones.
- Treat EOIs, RFPs and calls for consultants as a separate category: consultancy and tender opportunities, which suit the post-funding-cut segment.

**(b) Ideas for the Way2Impact LinkedIn page**
- The **4-line job block** is a good base for a **weekly roundup**. Keep it scannable and **add 2 or 3 fields**: level or grade, remote yes or no, and deadline. Also add **one line on who the role suits**, linked to Way2Impact's 4 segments.
- **Group by theme** (for example "This week: 10 remote roles", "UN home-based consultancies", "Entry-level / internships", "Roles for corporate shifters: finance, HR, comms") instead of by employer. This positions Way2Impact as a curator with expertise.
- Put the roundup in a **document or carousel post** (1 slide = 3 to 5 jobs) and keep the caption short with a hook. Use the comments for links if needed.
- Use a fixed sign-off with a CTA, as he does, but point it at the Way2Impact newsletter or job alert instead of WhatsApp.

### What to avoid / improve
- A wall of text with no hook gets almost no engagement. Way2Impact needs a headline ("12 impact jobs closing before 30 Nov") and a short intro.
- No deadlines or seniority makes the list hard to act on.
- Too local for Way2Impact's global audience. Use a global spread or regional sections.
- An image post getting far more engagement than the text lists confirms that **visual formats beat plain lists**.

## Cross-profile synthesis for Way2Impact

| Need | Best model | Way2Impact upgrade |
|---|---|---|
| Single job spotlight | Climate & Remote (emoji fact block plus branded card) | Add a "who this suits / what to show" line drawn from Nelly |
| Weekly roundup | Ma'in (4-line blocks) | Themed, with deadline, level and remote fields, as a carousel or document post |
| Expert job decoding | Nelly (mandate → requirements → CV must show) | Cut to about 1,200 characters plus a carousel. Rotate segments. Branded visuals |
| Market context | Jesús (ReliefWeb data) | Monthly "job market pulse" with career implications. Credit the source |

**Rules for the AI job-search system, combined (top 10):**
1. Extract the standard fields: org, role, grade or level, duty station type, remote with geographic limits, contract (staff / consultancy / temporary / national), length, salary or stipend, experience, languages, deadline with time zone, eligibility.
2. Check eligibility first: essential degree/years routes, certifications, languages, nationality or national position. Be honest when a client is "not eligible".
3. Summarise the mandate before scoring. Look past the title.
4. Score fit on evidence at the right level (influence and scale for senior roles, problems solved for juniors).
5. Add a security note for grant-tied, project-linked, or "renewable subject to funding" posts.
6. Include consultancies and EOIs by default for clients repositioning after funding cuts.
7. Rewrite duties as evidence using function-specific checklists (finance, grants, MEAL, supply chain, comms, HR, country leadership, policy).
8. Detect employer AI restrictions ("AI-generated CVs not accepted") and switch to coaching mode.
9. Prefer fewer, well-targeted applications (about 10 targeted beat 50 generic).
10. Protect privacy: redact client PII before external model calls, cite sources, and label market data as indicative.
